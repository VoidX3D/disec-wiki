# Reports & Analysis

Key reports, yearbooks and think-tank analyses relevant to LAWS and military AI.

## ICRC Position on Autonomous Weapons (2021) (2021)

**Organization:** ICRC  
**Category:** report

States must retain meaningful human control. Autonomous weapons raise three concerns: unpredictable behaviour, accountability challenges, ethical issues.

**Key findings:**

- Meaningful human control is essential
- Three-part test: predictability, accountability, human judgment
- Calls for international rules

[Official source](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/icrc-position-autonomous-weapon-systems)

## UN Secretary-General's Report on LAWS (A/78/273) (2023)

**Organization:** UN  
**Category:** report

Comprehensive overview of LAWS developments. Notes acceleration of AI in military applications. Calls for urgent multilateral action.

**Key findings:**

- Technology outpacing regulation
- Risk of AI arms race
- Need for binding international instrument

[Official source](https://documents-dds-ny.un.org/)

## UNODA: Autonomous Weapons Systems (2024) (2024)

**Organization:** UNODA  
**Category:** report

Overview of LAWS definitions, GGE process, state positions, current status of negotiations. Key reference for delegates.

**Key findings:**

- No universal definition of LAWS exists
- GGE has produced 11 guiding principles
- States divided on prohibition vs regulation

[Official source](https://www.un.org/disarmament/autonomous-weapons/)

## SIPRI Yearbook 2025: Armaments and Disarmament (2025)

**Organization:** SIPRI  
**Category:** report

Annual assessment of armaments, disarmament, and AI in military applications. Global military expenditure data.

**Key findings:**

- Global military expenditure exceeded $2.4 trillion in 2024
- AI military spending growing 15-20% annually
- 15+ countries developing autonomous weapons

[Official source](https://www.sipri.org/yearbook)

## UNIDIR: The Weaponization of AI (2024) (2024)

**Organization:** UNIDIR  
**Category:** report

Technical and policy analysis of AI integration into weapons systems. Examines strategic stability implications.

**Key findings:**

- AI fundamentally changes speed of warfare
- Verification challenges for AI arms control
- Confidence-building measures essential

[Official source](https://unidir.org/publication/)

## Human Rights Watch: Killer Robots Report (2024) (2024)

**Organization:** HRW  
**Category:** report

Civil society perspective on risks of fully autonomous weapons. Advocates preemptive ban on LAWS.

**Key findings:**

- Fully autonomous weapons should be banned
- Meaningful human control non-negotiable
- Accountability gap is unresolvable without prohibition

[Official source](https://www.hrw.org/topic/arms/autonomous-weapons)

## GGE on LAWS: Report of the 2024 Session (2024)

**Organization:** CCW  
**Category:** report

Official report of the Group of Governmental Experts. Includes 11 guiding principles. States still divided on binding instrument.

**Key findings:**

- 11 guiding principles adopted
- No consensus on legally binding instrument
- Need for further work on definitions

[Official source](https://www.un.org/disarmament/ccw/)

## Campaign to Stop Killer Robots: Annual Report (2024)

**Organization:** NGO  
**Category:** report

Coalition of 200+ NGOs advocating for preemptive ban on fully autonomous weapons. Growing international support.

**Key findings:**

- 166 countries support some form of LAWS regulation
- 70+ countries call for preemptive ban
- Civil society momentum growing

[Official source](https://www.stopkillerrobots.org/)

## IISS Military Balance 2025 (2025)

**Organization:** IISS  
**Category:** report

Comprehensive assessment of global military capabilities. Tracks AI and autonomous weapons development worldwide.

**Key findings:**

- 15+ countries with operational autonomous systems
- US, China, Russia lead in military AI investment
- AI drone capabilities spreading to middle powers

[Official source](https://www.iiss.org/military-balance)

## RAND Corporation: AI and National Security (2024) (2024)

**Organization:** RAND  
**Category:** report

Analysis of AI implications for national security. Examines competitive dynamics, governance gaps, and policy options.

**Key findings:**

- AI creates asymmetric advantages for technologically advanced states
- Governance gaps creating security risks
- International cooperation essential

[Official source](https://www.rand.org/)

## Carnegie Endowment: AI and International Order (2024)

**Organization:** Carnegie  
**Category:** report

Examines how AI is reshaping international order. Competition, governance, and the future of multilateralism.

**Key findings:**

- AI accelerating great power competition
- Existing institutions insufficient
- New frameworks needed

[Official source](https://carnegieendowment.org/)

## Amnesty International: Autonomous Weapons Systems (2024)

**Organization:** Amnesty  
**Category:** report

Human rights perspective on LAWS. Emphasizes impact on civilian populations and human dignity.

**Key findings:**

- LAWS threaten human dignity
- Civilian protection requires human control
- States must regulate AI in weapons

[Official source](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/research/)

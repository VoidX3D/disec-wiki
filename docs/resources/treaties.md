# Treaties, Frameworks & Policies

The legal architecture that frames LAWS discussions, from binding treaties to soft-law frameworks.

## International Treaties

### Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) (1980)

Primary legal framework for LAWS discussions. Protocol V addresses explosive remnants of war. GGE on LAWS established under CCW in 2016. 126 states parties.

**Key provisions:**

- Regulates or prohibits weapons causing unnecessary suffering
- Framework for autonomous weapons negotiations since 2013
- GGE produced 11 guiding principles on LAWS

[Official source](https://www.un.org/disarmament/ccw/)

### Geneva Conventions (1949) & Additional Protocols (1949)

Core international humanitarian law instruments. Common Article 3 applies to non-international armed conflicts. Additional Protocol I (1977) codifies distinction, proportionality, precaution.

**Key provisions:**

- Common Article 3: minimum protections in all conflicts
- Protocol I: rules on means and methods of warfare
- Article 48: obligation to distinguish civilians from combatants

[Official source](https://www.icrc.org/en/law-and-policy/geneva-conventions)

### Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT) (1968)

Precedent for arms control frameworks. Iran is a signatory. Useful analogy for AI arms race arguments — non-proliferation, disarmament, peaceful use.

**Key provisions:**

- Article VI: obligation to pursue nuclear disarmament
- Article IV: right to peaceful nuclear technology
- 191 states parties — most widely adhered arms control treaty

[Official source](https://www.un.org/disarmament/wmd/nuclear/npt/)

### Arms Trade Treaty (ATT) (2013)

Regulates international transfer of conventional arms. Relevant to LAWS proliferation and export controls. Risk assessment required before arms transfers.

**Key provisions:**

- Article 6: prohibitions on transfers if used for genocide or crimes against humanity
- Article 7: risk assessment of potential misuse
- Covers weapons including autonomous systems

[Official source](https://thearmstradetreaty.org/)

### Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC) (1993)

Model for comprehensive weapons prohibition with verification regime. Prohibits development, production, stockpiling of chemical weapons. Iran is a signatory.

**Key provisions:**

- Complete prohibition of chemical weapons
- Verification through OPCW inspections
- 193 states parties

[Official source](https://www.opcw.org/)

### Biological Weapons Convention (BWC) (1972)

Model for preventing hostile use of dual-use technology. Lessons for AI governance — dual-use concerns, verification challenges.

**Key provisions:**

- Prohibition on development and production of biological weapons
- No formal verification mechanism
- 187 states parties

[Official source](https://www.un.org/disarmament/bwc/)

### Ottawa Treaty (Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention) (1997)

Model for comprehensive prohibition of specific weapons category. Ban on anti-personnel mines. 164 states parties. Shows feasibility of humanitarian disarmament.

**Key provisions:**

- Complete prohibition of anti-personnel mines
- Obligation to clear mined areas
- Victim assistance provisions

[Official source](https://www.apminebanconvention.org/)

### Convention on Cluster Munitions (CCM) (2008)

Prohibits cluster munitions due to indiscriminate effects and civilian harm. Model for preemptive regulation of weapons with humanitarian concerns.

**Key provisions:**

- Prohibition on use, production, transfer of cluster munitions
- Obligation to clear and destroy stockpiles
- Victim assistance obligations

[Official source](https://www.clusterconvention.org/)

## Ethics & AI Frameworks

### UNESCO Recommendation on Ethics of AI (2021)

First global AI ethics framework: proportionality, human safety, privacy, effectiveness, transparency, responsibility, accountability, sustainability, human oversight.

**Key provisions:**

- Proportionality: AI should not go beyond what is needed
- Human safety: AI should not harm humans
- Human oversight: humans should be able to intervene

[Official source](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000381137)

### OECD AI Principles (2019)

First intergovernmental AI standards. Inclusive growth, human-centred values, transparency, robustness, security, accountability.

**Key provisions:**

- AI should benefit people and the planet
- AI systems should be transparent and explainable
- AI should be robust, secure and safe

[Official source](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles)

### NATO Artificial Intelligence Strategy (2021)

Allied approach to responsible military AI. Principles: lawfulness, responsibility, explainability, reliability, governability, bias mitigation.

**Key provisions:**

- AI must be used in accordance with international law
- Allied forces must maintain human responsibility
- AI must be explainable and reliable

[Official source](https://www.nato.int/docu/ai-strategy/)

## National & UN Policy

### US DoD Directive 3000.09 on Autonomy in Weapons (2012)

US policy on autonomous and semi-autonomous weapon systems. Requires human judgment in use of force, rigorous testing, legal review.

**Key provisions:**

- Human judgment required in use of force decisions
- Rigorous testing and evaluation required
- Legal review of all autonomous systems

[Official source](https://www.esd.whs.mil/)

### UN Secretary-General's Agenda for Disarmament (2018)

"Securing Our Common Future" — calls for new legal measures on LAWS and emerging technologies. Urges moratorium on fully autonomous weapons.

**Key provisions:**

- Call for new legal instrument on LAWS
- Urges moratorium on fully autonomous weapons
- Emphasizes humanitarian consequences

[Official source](https://www.un.org/disarmament/sg-agenda/)

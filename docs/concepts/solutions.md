# Solutions & Frameworks for LAWS Governance

The complete menu of policy options on the table — from preemptive ban to
non-binding declaration. Each option: proponents, opponents, viability,
Iran alignment, source.

## Option 1 — Preemptive ban on human-out-of-the-loop weapons

**What:** Prohibit any weapon system that selects and engages targets without
human intervention per engagement.

**Proponents**
- Austria (advocate since 2019)
- Brazil
- Holy See
- New Zealand
- Chile, Costa Rica
- 30+ countries with civil-society backing

**Opponents**
- United States, Russia, Israel, India
- Countries developing autonomous capability

**Iran alignment:** Iran **does not** support a preemptive ban — argues it
would freeze existing technological asymmetries.

**Mechanism:** A new protocol to the CCW, or a stand-alone treaty under
UNGA process.

**Source:** [stopkillerrobots.org](https://www.stopkillerrobots.org/)

## Option 2 — Non-binding political declaration

**What:** A UNGA resolution or ministerial declaration affirming principles
without legally binding obligations.

**Proponents**
- France
- Germany
- Netherlands
- UK
- US (preferred outcome)

**Mechanism:** UNGA First Committee resolution.

**Iran criticism:** "Rule by law" — binds weaker states, exempts
technologically dominant ones. Selective application erodes credibility.

## Option 3 — Binding treaty (CCW protocol or new instrument)

**What:** A new legally binding instrument on LAWS, negotiated within or
outside the CCW.

**Proponents**
- Many NAM, G77, EU civil society
- ICRC formally recommends new rules

**Mechanism options**
- **CCW Additional Protocol VI** — but CCW consensus rule allows any P5 to veto.
- **New stand-alone treaty** — UNGA process; opens signature without CCW consensus.
- **Hybrid** — soft-law track in CCW, hard-law track elsewhere.

**Verification challenges**
- Dual-use AI is hard to define.
- Software is intangible and exportable.
- Testing autonomous systems is opaque.
- No precedent for AI verification regimes.

**Iran alignment:** Supports binding instrument but insists on inclusive
negotiation, balance with defensive needs, and access to autonomous
defensive technologies.

## Option 4 — Two-tier regulation (Iran's preferred framework)

**What:** Distinguish between (a) regulation of critical functions for
existing and emerging LAWS, and (b) categorical prohibitions only for the
most clearly IHL-violating systems.

**Proponents**
- Iran
- India (similar posture)
- Several NAM states

**Components**
- **Tier 1 (regulated):** All autonomous critical functions must meet
  meaning human control, IHL compliance, traceability, accountability.
- **Tier 2 (prohibited):** Systems that cannot comply with distinction,
  proportionality, or precaution — and that target civilians.

**Iran's argument** (CCW GGE 2023): regulation is achievable; blanket
prohibition is impossible to verify and freezes asymmetries.

## Option 5 — Voluntary "code of conduct"

**What:** Non-binding best-practice document agreed among like-minded states.

**Examples**:
- US DoD Directive 3000.09 (2012, updated 2023) — internal US policy.
- UK MoD doctrine on autonomous weapons.
- NATO AI Strategy (2021).
- Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI (REAIM 2023, US-led) — 44+ states.

**Iran criticism:** Voluntary frameworks are **rule by law** — no
enforcement, no verification, no reciprocity.

**Source:** [state.gov/political-declaration-responsible-military-use-ai](https://www.state.gov/bureaus-offices/under-secretary-for-arms-control-and-international-security-affairs/political-declaration-on-the-responsible-military-use-of-artificial-intelligence/)

## Option 6 — Confidence-building measures (CBMs)

**What:** Practical transparency steps that build trust and lay groundwork
for future binding rules.

**Examples**
- State declarations on autonomous weapons programmes
- Exchange of doctrine
- Joint exercises and inspections
- Voluntary reporting to UNODA

**Iran alignment:** Receptive as long as CBMs are reciprocal and
non-discriminatory.

## Option 7 — Hybrid track (incremental)

**What:** CCW consensus on GGE work-plan that incrementally builds binding
norms.

**Mechanism**
- Annual GGE meetings.
- Working groups on definitions, accountability, verification.
- Possible eventual protocol via consensus (P5 risk).

**Critique:** Slow, consensus-bound, susceptible to dilution by veto-prone
states.

## Iran's specific negotiating position

From CCW GGE submissions:

| Position | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Two-tier regulation | Balance between humanitarian concerns and defensive autonomy |
| Meaningful human control | Operational concept, not a per-engagement requirement |
| No preemptive blanket ban | Would legitimise existing asymmetries |
| Multilateral negotiation | Inclusivity, not P5 exclusivity |
| Article 51 self-defence | Defensive autonomous systems are legitimate |
| No restrictions on dual-use | Technology is dual-use by nature |
| Verification must be solved first | Before binding instrument |
| Common but differentiated responsibilities | Developed states bear greater obligation |

## Cross-cutting issues

### Accountability

Who is responsible when an autonomous weapon causes unlawful harm?

| Option | Proponent |
|---|---|
| Commander / operator | Traditional IHL |
| Programmer / manufacturer | ICRC |
| State (strict liability) | Humanitarian NGOs |
| Multi-level (joint) | Most realistic |

### Verification

- **Software verification** — high uncertainty.
- **Hardware verification** — export controls (Wassenaar, MTCR).
- **Testing** — agreed test protocols?
- **Declarations** — state reporting of programmes.

### Definitions

| Term | Contested? |
|---|---|
| "Autonomous" | Yes — varies across NATO, ICRC, GGE |
| "Critical functions" | Less loaded term, used by GGE |
| "Meaningful human control" | Operational, contested |
| "LAWS" | Loaded; some prefer "AWS" (autonomous weapon systems) |

## Possible resolution text (illustrative)

For a DISEC draft resolution:

> *"The General Assembly, recalling the principles of sovereign equality,
> non-intervention and peaceful coexistence, and reaffirming the applicability
> of international humanitarian law to all weapons systems including those
> with autonomous critical functions, calls upon all Member States to:
>
> 1. Affirm the applicability of international humanitarian law to weapons
>    systems with autonomous critical functions;
> 2. Commit to meaningful human control over the selection and engagement
>    of targets;
> 3. Establish a working group under UNGA auspices to consider a legally
>    binding instrument on weapons systems that cannot comply with the
>    principles of distinction, proportionality and precaution;
> 4. Encourage voluntary confidence-building measures, including
>    transparency on autonomous weapons programmes;
> 5. Recognise the right of states to develop and acquire autonomous
>    defensive technologies consistent with Article 51 of the Charter."*

## Iran-aligned draft elements

| Provision | Rationale |
|---|---|
| Reference Panchsheel & Bandung Principles | Anchors NAM position |
| Reference Article 51 UN Charter | Preserves defensive autonomy |
| Reference Common but differentiated responsibilities | CBDR principle |
| Binding instrument on prohibition only | Two-tier approach |
| Operational "meaningful human control" | Avoids per-engagement mandate |
| Verification mechanism | Prevents asymmetric compliance |
| Inclusive negotiation | No P5 exclusivity |
| Technology transfer provisions | Allows defensive autonomy transfer |

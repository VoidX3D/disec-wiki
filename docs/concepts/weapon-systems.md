# Weapon Systems — Classification

Every weapon system relevant to LAWS & Military AI, categorised by domain,
autonomy level, function and effect. Sourced from SIPRI, IISS, UNODA, ICRC and
official national inventories.

## Classification by autonomy level

This is the central LAWS taxonomy. The ICRC / US DoD / UK MoD definitions
converge on five levels:

| Level | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Human-operated** | All targeting decisions by human operator | RPG, ATGW crew-served |
| **Human-assisted** | AI aids targeting, but human engages | F-35 sensor fusion, AI-assisted target recognition |
| **Human-supervised** (human-on-the-loop) | System can engage autonomously but human can override | Phalanx CIWS, Iron Dome, C-RAM |
| **Human-delegated** (human-out-of-the-loop) | Once activated, no per-engagement human control | **Shahed-136, Harpy, loitering munitions**, some autonomous naval mines |
| **Fully autonomous** | System selects, prioritises, and engages without human input | Experimental; the contested frontier |

### LAWS-specific category (GGE on LAWS)

The CCW GGE on LAWS distinguishes:
- **Critical functions** — selecting and engaging targets.
- **Weapons with autonomous critical functions** — a more cautious definition,
  avoiding the loaded term "lethal autonomous weapons".

## LAWS economic argument

A central Iran-aligned argument: **cheap autonomous systems can defeat expensive defensive systems**.

| System | Unit cost | Counter-system cost | Cost ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shahed-136 | ~$20,000 | Stinger missile: ~$38,000; Sidewinder: ~$400,000 | ~1:2 – 1:20 |
| Switchblade 300 | ~$6,000 | Counter-UAS effectors | ~1:5 – 1:20 |
| Switchblade 600 | ~$55,000 | Air-defence missile | ~1:10 |
| Cruise missile (Tomahawk) | ~$1.5 M | SM-2 missile: ~$2 M | ~1:1.3 |

This cost-imposition model is the **economic argument** Iran and others use
to support autonomous defensive systems — and the humanitarian argument
NGOs use to call for their prohibition.

## Conventional arms categories (UNROCA)

The UN Register of Conventional Arms tracks transfers of seven categories:

1. Battle tanks
2. Armoured combat vehicles
3. Large-calibre artillery systems
4. Combat aircraft
5. Attack helicopters
6. Warships
7. Missiles and missile launchers (MLLS)

Plus small arms and light weapons (SALW) under the PoA.

**Official source:** [un.org/disarmament/un-register](https://www.un.org/disarmament/un-register/)

## Counter-LAWS systems

Counter-LAWS systems are themselves a regulatory challenge — what counts as
a defensive autonomous system?

| System | Type | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Iron Dome | C-RAM | Defensive, high-autonomy |
| Phalanx CIWS | Naval C-RAM | Defensive, full autonomy |
| Patriot PAC-3 | Air defence | Semi-autonomous |
| S-400 / S-300 | Air defence | Semi-autonomous |
| Trophy APS | Tank active protection | Semi-autonomous |
| DroneDefender | RF disruption | Counter-drone |
| Microwave counter-UAS (THOR) | Directed energy | Counter-swarm |

**Iran argument:** Defensive autonomous systems are legitimate (Article 51);
offensive autonomous systems should be regulated. NGOs argue the distinction
collapses in practice.

               Chair’s summary – First 2025 session of the GGE on LAWS

               Submitted by the Chairperson

                  The Chairperson submits this summary, under his sole authority, with the intention of capturing his
          understanding of the work carried out during the first 2025 session of the Group and to maintain an
          informal record of proceedings to help inform the work of the Group in 2025 and ahead.

       I. Background and procedural aspects
               1.     The first 2025 session of the Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on emerging
               technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS) (hereinafter “the
               Group”) was opened by the Chairperson, H.E. Mr. Robert in den Bosch of the Kingdom of
               the Netherlands, on Monday 3 March 2025. It was then closed on Friday 7 March 2025.
               2.      The Group’s mandate was decided by the 2023 Meeting of the High Contracting
               Parties to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW). The Meeting decided
               as follows: “That the work of the open-ended Group of Governmental Experts related to
               emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapon systems established by
               Decision 1 of the Fifth Review Conference as contained in document CCW/CONF.V/10,
               adhering to the agreed recommendations contained in document CCW/CONF.V/2, is to
               continue, to strengthen the Convention. In the context of the objectives and purpose of the
               Convention, the Group is to further consider and formulate, by consensus, a set of elements
               of an instrument, without prejudging its nature, and other possible measures to address
               emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapon systems, taking into account
               the example of existing Protocols within the Convention, proposals presented by High
               Contracting Parties and other options related to the normative and operational framework on
               emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapon systems, building upon the

               the GGE, with the aim to develop an instrument, without prejudging its nature, and other
               possible measures. She stated that only a shared sense of collaboration would allow States to
               develop a framework that will balance their security needs with the necessary legal,
               humanitarian and ethical imperatives and considerations of our shared humanity
               7.     The Chairperson welcomed Trinidad and Tobago as a High Contracting Party to the

               8.      The session gathered 84 High Contracting Parties, 1 Signatory State, 11 Observer
               States, 1 organization with observer status in the United Nations, 1 United Nations entity, the
               International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), 6 academic organizations and 19 non-
               governmental organizations. Participants held substantive, technical and detailed discussions,
               the summary of which is contained in section II below.

       II. Overview of discussions
               9.     As proposed by the Chairperson, the Group agreed to refrain from holding a general
               exchange of views. Rather, the Group engaged directly in substantive discussions. The Group
               had before it the rolling text (version as circulated on 8 November 2024, with numbers for
               the elements as included in the version circulated on 26 November 2024), building on the
               discussions held during the second 2024 session of the Group in August 2024. The rolling
               text is structured along 5 boxes addressing various elements, including a working
               characterization of LAWS, the application of IHL, prohibitions and regulations and
               accountability and responsibility.

     1.   A lethal autonomous weapon system can be characterized as an
          integrated combination of one or more weapons and technological
          components that enable the system to identify and/or select, and engage
          a target, without intervention by a human user in the execution of these
          tasks.
     2.   The above description is without prejudice to any future understanding
          and the potential modification of this characterization, as well as the
          possible exclusion of certain types of systems.

    10.    The Chair stated that the working characterization remained one of the more firmly
    debated part of the rolling text and identified lethality, the cumulative nature (or not) of
    autonomous tasks, human intervention, and integration of components as key issues to be
    further discussed.
    11.    Delegations welcomed the first box of the rolling text as a meaningful step toward
    advancing the Group's mandate, considering it a solid foundation for further discussions and
    a contribution to bridging remaining divergencies between delegations. Many participants
    emphasized the urgency of swift action, given the rapid pace of technological advancements.
    To ensure the text remains relevant in the face of future technological progress, several
    delegations advocated for maintaining a technology-agnostic approach. In addition, many
    delegations argued for a broad scope of the working characterization.
    12.    Similar to the 2024 sessions of the Group,3 divergent opinions were expressed on the
    need, or not, to continue including the term “lethal” when referring to autonomous weapons
    systems. On the one hand, many delegations requested its deletion on the basis that “lethality”
    was an effect of a weapon and not a characterization of a weapon system, the fact that the
    term does not have a clear basis in IHL, the CCW and IHL also covers injuries and destruction
    caused to civilian object. Some of these delegations also argued that the use of “lethal” would
    narrow down the scope of the characterization too much. On the other hand, some delegates
    argued that the term “lethal” should be retained, given that it is the terminology used in the
    mandate of the Group. According to some of these delegations, leaving the notion of “lethal”
    out would broaden up the scope of the characterization too much. Various delegations
    suggested to integrate a definition of “lethality” into the text, which would be used in the
    context of the CCW and work of the Group.
    13.    The Chairperson proposed a definition of “lethality”, based on a proposal put forward
    by several delegations, which would be included in a new paragraph in the rolling text.
    Several delegations welcomed this attempt at defining the term and declared they could
    support it. However, others did not support the definition, as it linked “lethality” to the
    weapon itself, instead of linking it to the capacity or capability of the weapon. Many
    delegations also welcomed suggested text placing the definition within the scope of the
    application of the CCW.
    14.     Delegations had two rounds of detailed exchanges on the key tasks of LAWS (“to
    identify and/or select, and engage”). Some delegations were in favour of a cumulative
    approach, retaining only the word “and” between the functions (as per the language in para
    19 (a) of the 2019 GGE report), whilst others deemed it essential to keep “and/or” in order to
    have a broad scope and avoid potential loopholes. This divide reflects two different visions
    when it comes to the elements of an instrument that the Group is working on: there are the
    delegations who think we need a broad characterization to ensure that all LAWS will be
    covered under the working characterization, whilst other delegations prefer a cumulative
    approach in order to avoid the risk of prohibiting/regulating weapons systems that are already
    used in accordance with IHL, and which are, in their view, not LAWS per se. Discussions
    also centred around the exact interpretation of the words “identify” and “select”.

      B.       Box II

                In Box II, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 8 November 2024)
                proposed the following elements:
                1.   The right of the parties to an armed conflict to choose methods and
                     means of warfare is not unlimited.
                2.   IHL applies to armed conflict, whether international or non-
                     international, and governs the use of all weapons, means and methods
                     of warfare, those of the past, those of the present and those of the future,
                     and is, consequently, applicable independent of the military technology
                     used.
                3.   IHL, therefore, continues to apply fully to the development and use of
                     LAWS.
                4.   In cases involving LAWS not covered by the CCW and its annexed
                     Protocols or by other international agreements, the civilian population
                     and the combatants at all times remain under the protection and
                     authority of the principles of international law derived from established
                     custom, from the principles of humanity and from the dictates of public
                     conscience.
                5.   Context-appropriate human control and judgement with regard to the
                     use and effects of LAWS is essential in order to ensure its employment is
                     in compliance with international law, and in particular IHL, including
                     the principles and requirements of distinction, proportionality and
                     precautions in attack.

               16.     Most of this section already enjoyed broad support during previous sessions of the
               Group. Overall, most delegations welcomed the fact that the Chairperson used language from
               previous reports adopted by the Group, of the Guiding Principles – notably Guiding Principle
               (a)4 - and of the preambular part of the CCW, notably the first three paragraphs.
               17.     Many delegations welcomed the incorporation of the Martens Clause provision, as it
               had been requested during the previous formal session of the Group.5 However, some
               delegations underlined that the current formulation could mean that some LAWS would fall
               outside of the scope of the elements discussed. In addition, some delegations recalled that the
               Martens Clause is already part of existing IHL and therefore proposed to delete it from the
               rolling text.
               18.    Delegations had an in-depth exchange of views on the notion of “context-appropriate
               human control and judgement” based on the text in section 5 of box II and on a set of
               questions put forward by the Chair. Some delegations reiterated their preference for using
               “meaningful” as a qualifier of “human control”, Most delegations reaffirmed the need for
               human control as a precondition for compliance with IHL and recognized the need to find a
               compromise solution on how to qualify human control. Other delegations repeated their
               concern that human control might imply direct control, which is not always required under
               IHL. Most delegations stated that the level of human control is depended on the specific

C.   Box III

      In Box III, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 8 November 2024)
      proposed the following elements:
         1.   It is prohibited in all circumstances to use LAWS which are incapable
              of being employed in compliance with IHL, including the principles
              and requirements of distinction, proportionality and precautions in
              attack.
         2.   It is prohibited in all circumstances to employ LAWS which are of a
              nature to cause superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering, or which
              are inherently indiscriminate.
         3.   It is prohibited in all circumstances to make the civilian population
              as such, individual civilians or civilian objects the object of attack by
              LAWS.
         4.   It is prohibited in all circumstances to deploy or use LAWS if their
              effects in attack cannot be anticipated and controlled, as required by
              IHL in the circumstances of their use.
         5.   It is prohibited in all circumstances to employ LAWS that operate
              without context- appropriate human control and judgement.
         6.   For this purpose, parties to a conflict need to:
              A. Ensure that LAWS and their effects are adequately predictable,
                 reliable, traceable and explainable to be used in compliance with
                 IHL.
              B. Ensure that LAWS are operated with context-appropriate human
                 control and judgment. This includes ensuring moral and legal
                 consideration is given by a human, in particular, during
                 identification and/or engagement stages.
              C. Limit the types of targets, duration, geographical scope, and
                 scale of the operation of LAWS, including through:
                       i.   Ensuring that LAWS can be deactivated by a human user
                             after activation.
                      ii.   Ensuring that LAWS incorporate self-destruct, self-
                             deactivation or self-neutralization mechanisms.
                     iii.   Limiting the number of engagements that LAWS can
                             undertake.
                     iv.    Avoiding concentrations of civilians or civilian objects.
                      v.    Limiting the types of targets, for instance by considering
                             to restrict the use of LAWS against objects that are
                             military objectives by nature.

               7.   Ensure that LAWS’ mission parameters cannot be modified by the
                    system without context- appropriate human control and judgement.

           21.     Delegations continued expressing their support for an approach containing both
           prohibitions and regulations (the so called “two tier approach”) as a potential way forward
           and underlined that elements contained in box III could be core components of a possible
           future instrument.
           22.     Delegations engaged in detailed discussions on this box, including on key elements
           of context-appropriate human control and judgment. Regarding the paragraphs framing the
           “prohibitions” part, delegations were in general positive about the language used, given that
           it is extracted from existing IHL, and from previously agreed language of the Group.
           However, some delegations stated that even though using language from existing law was
           necessary to ensure continuity, the Group should further build on them, including through
           inclusion of elements specifically relevant to LAWS such as predictability, reliability,
           traceability and explainability. The introduction of these terms in the context of IHL led to
           further discussion on their precise meaning and whether they can be directly based on current
           IHL.
           23.    A number of delegations indicated that some of the regulations put forward were too
           prescriptive and questioned their relevance, stating that they did not consist of primary IHL
           obligations. However, other delegations highlighted that it was essential to have such limits
           in place. It was also proposed to restructure the elements listed in paragraphs 6 and 7 to better
           highlight the connection with existing IHL obligations. Others argued that it might be useful
           to move certain elements from box III to box IV.
           24.    In a second round of interventions, following textual suggestions by the Chair,
           delegations shared views on the possibility of including in the rolling text the prohibition of
           developing LAWS whose effects cannot be anticipated and controlled. Many delegations
           opposed this, stating that it could prevent the development of technology as a whole, and that
           it would complicate the production of such weapon systems at a too early stage.

      D.   Box IV

            In Box IV, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 8 November 2024)
            proposed the following elements:
            Prior to potential employment and as applicable throughout the entire life
            cycle of LAWS, States should:
               1.   Determine, in accordance with their obligations under international
                    law, in the study, development, acquisition, or adoption of a new
                    weapon, means or method of warfare, whether its employment would,
                    in some or all circumstances, be prohibited by international law. In
                    this context, the voluntary exchange of relevant best practices
                    between States is encouraged, bearing in mind national security
                    considerations or commercial restrictions on proprietary
                    information.
               2.   Ensure that LAWS are operated with context-appropriate human
                    control and judgment across the entire life cycle of the weapon
                    systems.
               3.   Ensure that capabilities and limitations of LAWS, in expected
                    circumstances of use, and its anticipated effects in different scenarios
                    are properly understood.
               4.   Ensure that weapon systems under development or modification that
                    significantly changes the use of existing weapons systems, are
                    reviewed to ensure compliance with IHL.

              5.   Conduct rigorous testing and evaluation in realistic simulated
                   operational environments to enable a human user to have a reliable
                   expectation of how the weapon system will perform in the anticipated
                   circumstances of its use.
              6.   Conduct periodic assessments to detect and correct, as much as
                   possible, harmful bias in data sets and implement measures to
                   mitigate automation bias.
              7.   Implement measures to address possible harmful bias in artificial
                   intelligence capabilities relied upon by LAWS.
              8.   States should ensure appropriate guidance, training and instructions
                   for human users of LAWS.

         25.     Many delegations welcomed the language in this box on the basis that it emphasized
         the importance of conducting legal reviews to ensure compliance with international law, and
         in particular with IHL.
         26.    A few delegations recalled Guiding Principle (j),6 and stated that it should be indicated
         in the rolling text that these measures should not hamper scientific and technological
         advancement.
         27.    Regarding the notion of “context-appropriate human control and judgement”,
         delegations reiterated their diverse positions shared during the exchanges on previous boxes.
         28.    In the previous sessions of the Group, there had been a call for stronger language on
         the need to prevent biases – in particular gender and racial ones – or to reduce their
         occurrence. Regarding this specific point, even though most delegations welcomed the
         inclusion of language on this risk, many recommended to use “unintended” instead of
         “harmful” in order to qualify it, as “harmful” was seen as a subjective term. One delegation
         also suggested to have a clearer distinction between algorithmic biases and unintended biases.
         Other proposed to merge the paragraphs on data and AI bias and create a separate paragraph
         on automation bias.
         29.    Several delegations requested the inclusion of an additional paragraph to address the
         need to prevent the proliferation and diversion of LAWS by non-States actors.

E.       Box V

           In Box V, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 8 November 2024) proposed
           the following elements:
              1.   States must ensure that humans at all times remain responsible and
                   accountable in accordance with applicable international law for
                   decisions with regard to LAWS.
              2.   States must ensure that human responsibility for decisions on the use
                   of LAWS is retained since accountability cannot be transferred to
                   machines. This should be considered across the entire life cycle of the
                   weapon system.
              3.   States must ensure accountability across the entire life cycle of LAWS
                   in accordance with applicable international and national law,
                   including through the operation of such systems within a traceable
                   and responsible chain of human command and control.

               4.   States must ensure individual responsibility for the employment of

               5.   States must ensure effective internal oversight, investigative and

           30.     Overall, delegations welcomed this box and there were relatively few substantive
           comments to the proposed text in in this box, as much of its content reflects previously agreed
           language. Nonetheless, technical amendments were suggested by numerous delegations, such
           as to change “must” into “should” throughout the box.
           31.    Some delegations noted that a specific reference to the notion of international
           responsibility of a State was absent from the text of box V, and that it would be necessary to
           include a paragraph on that.
           32.    Several delegations objected to the inclusion of the word “traceable” and explained
           that this was impractical from a technical point of view. Furthermore, some of them
           underlined that there is no consensus on what this term means, and that the Group should use
           notions which have already been discussed. Those delegations therefore requested to have
           only a reference to a “responsible chain of human command and control”.
           33.    Several delegations expressed their opposition to the inclusion of an obligation for
           High Contracting Parties to take specific legislative, penal or administrative measures
           regarding the restriction and/or prohibition of the use of LAWS. They suggested to delete
           such references on the basis that these were national prerogatives, which fell outside the
           mandate of the Group.

    III. Way forward
           34.    The Group succeeded in concluding a complete reading of the rolling text, and of
           conducting preliminary yet in-depth discussions on all major issues. The Group also held a
           second round of interventions, following textual suggestions by the Chair regarding boxes 1
           to 3. This first 2025 session was generally considered as productive, substantive and
           constructive, contributing to the delivery by the mandate of the Group.
           35.     At the conclusion of the session, the Chairperson informed delegations that he would
           share his summary of the discussions of the first session, to be followed by an updated version
           of the rolling text, based on the discussions during this session.
           36.    The Chair also announced that he would organize intersessional informal
           consultations before the second 2025 session of the Group in September, to facilitate moving
           towards a common understanding on some key notions, mindful of delegations’ capacity and
           the constraints of the disarmament calendar.
           37.     The Chairperson urged delegations to remain actively engaged and invited them to
           seek interaction with the Friends and the Chair’s Team during the intersessional period. The
           Chair concluded by reminding delegations that the Group has only three remaining sessions
           to finalize its mandate before the deadline of the Seventh CCW Review Conference, in 2026.

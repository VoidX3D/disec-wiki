From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

(Redirected from [Military artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Military%5Fartificial%5Fintelligence&redirect=no%20%22Military%20artificial%20intelligence%22))

[Artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial%5Fintelligence "Artificial intelligence") (AI) has many [applications in warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI%5Fwarfare "AI warfare"), including in [communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military%5Fcommunications "Military communications"), [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military%5Fintelligence "Military intelligence"), and [munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munitions "Munitions") control. AI is not limited to new weapons such as [drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned%5Fvehicles "Unmanned vehicles") or [strategic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategy "Strategy") means such as [cyberwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwar "Cyberwar"), as it can affect all forms of military planning.[\[1\]](#cite%5Fnote-Allen%5FWest%5F2020-1)

## Uses

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Military%5Fapplications%5Fof%5Fartificial%5Fintelligence&action=edit&section=1%20%22Edit%20section:%20Uses%22)\]

AI can enhance [command and control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command%5Fand%5Fcontrol "Command and control"), [communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military%5Fcommunications "Military communications"), sensors, integration and interoperability.[\[2\]](#cite%5Fnote-Slyusar-2019-2) AI technologies enable coordination of sensors and effectors, threat detection and identification, marking of enemy positions, [target acquisition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target%5Facquisition "Target acquisition"), coordination and deconfliction of distributed [Joint Fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward%5Fobservers%5Fin%5Fthe%5FU.S.%5Fmilitary "Forward observers in the U.S. military") between networked combat vehicles, both human operated and [autonomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicular%5Fautomation "Vehicular automation").[\[2\]](#cite%5Fnote-Slyusar-2019-2)

AI has been used in military operations in [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq "Iraq"), [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria "Syria"), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine"), [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran "Iran") and [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel "Israel").[\[3\]](#cite%5Fnote-CRS-2019-3)[\[4\]](#cite%5Fnote-Lavender%5F972-4)[\[5\]](#cite%5Fnote-Ukraine%5Fkamikaze%5Fdrones-5)[\[6\]](#cite%5Fnote-6) An AI-powered Automatic Target Classifying System, which employs sensors and algorithms to automatically identify and classify targets on radar, was patented by the Indian Army in 2025\. It swiftly and precisely compares real-time data—like pictures or radar signals—to a database of stored data. It can be used for disposable purposes, such as guiding missiles.[\[7\]](#cite%5Fnote-7)[\[8\]](#cite%5Fnote-8)

### Autonomous armament

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Military%5Fapplications%5Fof%5Fartificial%5Fintelligence&action=edit&section=2%20%22Edit%20section:%20Autonomous%20armament%22)\]

See also: [Lethal autonomous weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lethal%5Fautonomous%5Fweapon "Lethal autonomous weapon") and [Unmanned combat aerial vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned%5Fcombat%5Faerial%5Fvehicle "Unmanned combat aerial vehicle")

Military drones capable of autonomous action are in wide use.[\[9\]](#cite%5Fnote-9)\[_[where?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Naming%5Fconventions%5F%28geographic%5Fnames%29 "Wikipedia:Naming conventions (geographic names)")_\]

### Command and control

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Military%5Fapplications%5Fof%5Fartificial%5Fintelligence&action=edit&section=3%20%22Edit%20section:%20Command%20and%20control%22)\]

In 2024 a Chinese laboratory at the Joint Operations College of the National Defense University in [Shijiazhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shijiazhuang "Shijiazhuang") has created an AI military commander, for use in large-scale [war simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military%5Fsimulation "Military simulation") in the role of the [commander-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief "Commander-in-chief").[\[10\]](#cite%5Fnote-10)

In 2024, the Ukrainian Army developed autonomous [Kamikaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze "Kamikaze") drones in order to make Russian interference during flight ineffective.[\[5\]](#cite%5Fnote-Ukraine%5Fkamikaze%5Fdrones-5)

In November 2024, [Joe Biden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe%5FBiden "Joe Biden") and [Xi Jinping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi%5FJinping "Xi Jinping") affirmed the need to maintain human control over the use of [nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear%5Fweapons "Nuclear weapons") as opposed to artificial intelligence.[\[11\]](#cite%5Fnote-11)[\[12\]](#cite%5Fnote-12)

During the [2025 India–Pakistan conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%5FIndia–Pakistan%5Fconflict "2025 India–Pakistan conflict"), the Indian military’s AI-enabled Meteorological Reporting System ([Project Anumaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery%5FCombat%5FCommand%5Fand%5FControl%5FSystem#Upgrades "Artillery Combat Command and Control System")) was fed [IMD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%5FMeteorological%5FDepartment "India Meteorological Department") data to assist the [Artillery Combat Command and Control System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery%5FCombat%5FCommand%5Fand%5FControl%5FSystem "Artillery Combat Command and Control System") with planning and accurate targeting with extended range artillery shells. Up to 200 kilometres (120 miles) inside Pakistan's borders, the system can predict exact wind speed and other meteorological conditions 48-72 hours ahead of time.[\[13\]](#cite%5Fnote-13)[\[14\]](#cite%5Fnote-14)[\[15\]](#cite%5Fnote-15) The AI also aided in long-range missile trajectory calculations.[\[16\]](#cite%5Fnote-16)

### Military intelligence

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Military%5Fapplications%5Fof%5Fartificial%5Fintelligence&action=edit&section=4%20%22Edit%20section:%20Military%20intelligence%22)\]

In 2023, the [United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United%5FStates%5FDepartment%5Fof%5FDefense "United States Department of Defense") tested generative AI based on large language models to digitize and integrate data across the military.[\[17\]](#cite%5Fnote-17)

[Israel used two AI systems in the Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-assisted%5Ftargeting%5Fin%5Fthe%5FGaza%5FStrip "AI-assisted targeting in the Gaza Strip") to generate targets to strike: Habsora (translated: "[the gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The%5Fgospel "The gospel")") was used to compile a list of buildings to target, while "Lavender" produced a list of people. "Lavender" produced a list of 37,000 people to target.[\[4\]](#cite%5Fnote-Lavender%5F972-4)[\[18\]](#cite%5Fnote-Davies-2023-18) The list of buildings to target included Gazan private homes of people that were suspected of affiliation to [Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas "Hamas") operatives. The combination of AI targeting technology with policy shift away from avoiding civilian targets resulted in unprecedented numbers of civilian deaths. [IDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%5FDefense%5FForces "Israel Defense Forces") officials say the program addresses the previous issue of the air force running out of targets. Using Habsora, officials say that suspected and junior Hamas members' homes significantly expand the "AI target bank." An internal source describes the process as a "mass assassination factory".[\[19\]](#cite%5Fnote-19)[\[18\]](#cite%5Fnote-Davies-2023-18) By 2026, Israel expanded the use of AI in the military beyond Gaza, developing an army-wide "Operational Data and AI Factory," which was used for drone strikes, targeting and defensive measures as part of the [war with Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026%5FIran%5Fwar "2026 Iran war") as well as in [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbollah–Israel%5Fconflict%5F%282023–present%29 "Hezbollah–Israel conflict (2023–present)")[\[20\]](#cite%5Fnote-20). Some reports stated that AI-assisted systems were used to assist in identifying and tracking senior Iranian officials during Israeli military operations in the 2026 Iran conflict.[\[21\]](#cite%5Fnote-21)[\[22\]](#cite%5Fnote-22)[\[23\]](#cite%5Fnote-23)

Researchers and human rights organizations described Israeli AI-assisted targeting systems as relying on large-scale analysis of surveillance footage, drones, mobile devices, and communications metadata to identify potential targets. They expressed concern that such systems may occasionally misidentify individuals or contribute to targeting errors when relying heavily on automated data analysis.[\[24\]](#cite%5Fnote-24)[\[25\]](#cite%5Fnote-25)[\[26\]](#cite%5Fnote-26)[\[27\]](#cite%5Fnote-27)

In 2024, the U.S. military trained artificial intelligence to identify [airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airstrike "Airstrike") targets during its operations in Iraq and Syria.[\[28\]](#cite%5Fnote-28)

In [2025 India–Pakistan conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%5FIndia–Pakistan%5Fconflict "2025 India–Pakistan conflict"), the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian%5FArmy "Indian Army") employed AI to create a shared operational picture, analyze intelligence, evaluate threats, and create predictive models for long-range attacks. Twenty-three applications were developed for different purposes that handled inputs and data for real-time multi-sensor and multi-source data fusion. Applications include the Electronic Intelligence Collation and Analysis System, which has been integrated with [Project Sanjay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery%5FCombat%5FCommand%5Fand%5FControl%5FSystem#Upgrades "Artillery Combat Command and Control System") to provide a common operational picture for improved coordination, [situational awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situation%5Fawareness "Situation awareness"), and decision superiority, and the Trinetra system, which has been used to identify and prioritize critical threats in order to achieve strategic dominance. AI-enabled weather forecast, allowed for accurate planning and targeting of artillery units and long-range vectors. The battlefield AI model was fed 26 years of data that had recorded and archived the [Pakistan Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan%5FArmed%5FForces "Pakistan Armed Forces")' frequency signature and radio emission. This specified which military unit in Pakistan was responsible for each piece of equipment and where it had previously been used. Integrating feeds from sensors, drones, radars, and satellites allowed for the collection of real-time data. All the data, including information on adversary positions, resources, and logistics, were combined and presented to the military commanders for appropriate action.[\[29\]](#cite%5Fnote-29)[\[30\]](#cite%5Fnote-30)

In June 2026, court filings revealed that a government version of [Grok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok%5F%28chatbot%29 "Grok (chatbot)") was used within the Pentagon's Maven Smart System during military operations against Iran. According to Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Officer Cameron Stanley, the system supported the deployment of more than 2,000 munitions against 2,000 distinct targets within a 96-hour period. The disclosure was first reported by [The Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The%5FInformation%5F%28website%29 "The Information (website)").[\[31\]](#cite%5Fnote-31)[\[32\]](#cite%5Fnote-32)[\[33\]](#cite%5Fnote-33) Separate reporting in 2026 also indicated that [Anthropic’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic "Anthropic") [Claude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude%5F%28language%5Fmodel%29 "Claude (language model)") was used in U.S. military operations in Iran, particularly in intelligence analysis and in assisting with target identification and prioritization.[\[34\]](#cite%5Fnote-34)[\[35\]](#cite%5Fnote-35)[\[36\]](#cite%5Fnote-36)

## Global trends

\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Military%5Fapplications%5Fof%5Fartificial%5Fintelligence&action=edit&section=5%20%22Edit%20section:%20Global%20trends%22)\]

Various countries are researching and deploying AI military applications,[\[3\]](#cite%5Fnote-CRS-2019-3) in what has been termed the "[artificial intelligence arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial%5Fintelligence%5Farms%5Frace "Artificial intelligence arms race")". Ongoing research is focused on [intelligence collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military%5Fintelligence "Military intelligence") and [analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence%5Fanalysis "Intelligence analysis"), [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military%5Flogistics "Military logistics"), [cyber operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare "Cyberwarfare"), information operations, and semiautonomous and [autonomous vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicular%5Fautomation "Vehicular automation").[\[3\]](#cite%5Fnote-CRS-2019-3)

Worldwide annual military spending on robotics rose from US$5.1 billion in 2010 to US$7.5 billion in 2015.[\[37\]](#cite%5Fnote-37)[\[38\]](#cite%5Fnote-38)\[_[needs update](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual%5Fof%5FStyle/Dates%5Fand%5Fnumbers#Chronological%5Fitems "Wikipedia:Manual of Style/Dates and numbers")_\]

In November 2023, US Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala%5FHarris "Kamala Harris") disclosed a declaration signed by 31 nations to set guardrails for the military use of AI. The commitments include using legal reviews to ensure the compliance of military AI with international laws, and being cautious and transparent in the development of this technology.[\[39\]](#cite%5Fnote-39)

Many AI researchers try to avoid military applications, with guardrails to prevent military applications integrated into most mainstream large language models.[\[2\]](#cite%5Fnote-Slyusar-2019-2)

Some analysts have noted that some military applications of artificial intelligence could pose challenges to and may eventually degrade the [Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese%5FCommunist%5FParty "Chinese Communist Party")'s [absolute control of the military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The%5FParty%5Fcommands%5Fthe%5Fgun "The Party commands the gun").[\[40\]](#cite%5Fnote-40)

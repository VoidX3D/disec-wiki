                Submitted by the Chairperson*

           The Chairperson submits this summary, under his sole authority, with the intention of capturing his understanding of
   the work carried out during the second 2025 session of the Group of Governmental Experts on Lethal Autonomous Weapons
   Systems and to maintain an informal record of proceedings to help inform the future work of the Group.

        I. Background and procedural aspects
                1.     The second 2025 session of the Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on emerging
                technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS) (hereinafter “the
                Group”) was opened by the Chairperson, H.E. Mr. Robert in den Bosch of the Kingdom of
                the Netherlands, on Monday 1 September 2025. It was closed on Friday 5 September 2025.
                2.      The Group’s mandate was decided by the 2023 Meeting of the High Contracting
                Parties to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW). The Meeting decided
                as follows: “That the work of the open-ended Group of Governmental Experts related to
                emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapon systems established by
                Decision 1 of the Fifth Review Conference as contained in document CCW/CONF.V/10,
                adhering to the agreed recommendations contained in document CCW/CONF.V/2, is to
                continue, to strengthen the Convention. In the context of the objectives and purpose of the
                Convention, the Group is to further consider and formulate, by consensus, a set of elements
                of an instrument, without prejudging its nature, and other possible measures to address
                emerging technologies in the area of lethal autonomous weapon systems, taking into account
                the example of existing Protocols within the Convention, proposals presented by High
                Contracting Parties and other options related to the normative and operational framework on

         II. Overview of discussions
                   8.     As proposed by the Chairperson, the Group agreed to refrain from holding a general
                   exchange of views. Rather, the Group engaged directly in substantive discussions. The Group
                   had before it the rolling text (version as circulated on 12 May 2025),2 building on the
                   discussions held during the first 2025 session of the Group. The rolling text is structured
                   along 5 boxes addressing various elements.

          A.       Box I

                   9.    In Box I, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 12 May 2025 as corrected on 20
                   August 2025) proposed the following elements:
    1.   Within the scope of the application of the CCW, a lethal autonomous weapon system can be characterized as an
         integrated combination of one or more weapons and technological components, that can select and engage a
         target, without intervention by a human user in the execution of these tasks.

    2.   For the purposes of these elements of an instrument, lethal refers to the capacity to cause death to a person. The
         fact that a LAWS can be used in circumstances that do not result in death, such as to damage or destroy objects
         or to cause injury, does not exclude it from this characterization.

    3.   The above description is without prejudice to any future understanding and the potential modification of this
         characterization, as well as the possible exclusion of certain types of systems.

                   10.     The Chairperson presented the most significant changes introduced in Box I of the
                   rolling text since the last session. In particular, the Chairperson highlighted the decision to

    formulation “identify and/or select, and engage”. This choice was made with the
    understanding that identification is typically a precondition for the function of selection. The
    Chairperson also drew attention to the new language in paragraph 2 of Box I, which aims to

    instrument. The new language was an attempt to reflect a middle ground between more
    narrow and broader definitions, which were put forward during the first 2025 session of the
    Group.
    11.     On the functions of LAWS, many delegations supported the revised formulations,
    designating “select and engage” as the critical functions. These delegations argued that the
    “identification” phase is inherently included within the “selection” process, and that the
    current wording avoids an overly restrictive approach, while minimizing potential loopholes.
    Conversely, a number of States maintained that “identify” is not necessarily encompassed by
    “select”, emphasizing that identification and selection represent two distinct phases in the
    targeting process. They expressed concern that the current wording could potentially blur the
    distinction between LAWS, as addressed in the rolling text, and other weapon systems not
    being considered by the Group.
    12.    As during previous sessions of the Group,3 delegations engaged in extensive
    exchanges on the notion of “lethality”, and its role in the rolling text. There was general
    support for paragraph 2 of Box I, which proposed a definition of “lethality”, applicable solely
    to these elements. Delegations in favour of this approach viewed it as a constructive effort to
    bridge positions between those wanting to retain “lethal” as a characteristic of LAWS, and
    those calling for its deletion. Nonetheless, several delegations reiterated their respective
    positions on whether the term should be included in the elements.
    13.    After the first reading of the rolling text was concluded, the Chairperson put forward
    several new text suggestions for Box I.
    14.     One proposal reintroduced the function of “identify” before “select and engage” to
    characterize LAWS, accompanied by a paragraph clarifying the meaning of identification.
    This was intended to address different concerns raised by delegations with regard to the scope
    of the characterization, either being too narrow or too wide.
    15.     In paragraph 1, a proposal to replace the phrase “integrated combination” with
    “functionally integrated combination” received broad support. Delegations welcomed this
    revision as it clarified that the characterization does not require all components to be
    physically co-located, while still ensuring that technological elements are meaningfully
    linked to the weapon system. Also in paragraph 1, there was general agreement to refer to a
    human “operator” rather than a human “user”, with delegations noting that “operator” better
    reflects the role of individuals with direct control over the system and aligns more closely
    with military and legal terminology. In this regard, several delegations underlined the
    importance of retaining the word “human” before “operator”.
    16.     On lethality, the Chairperson suggested to amend the first sentence and retain the
    second as a standalone paragraph with minor adjustments. Given that the Group’s work
    concerns lethal autonomous weapon systems, it was considered unnecessary to define
    lethality in terms of causing death, as this causality is broadly understood. The Chairperson
    explained that this suggestion ensures that other relevant effects, such as injury and damage
    to objects, remain captured in the characterization.

          B.       Box II

                   18.   In Box II, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 12 May 2025 as corrected on 20
                   August 2025) proposed the following elements:
    1.   The right of the parties to an armed conflict to choose methods or means of warfare is not unlimited.

    2.   IHL applies to armed conflict, whether international or non-international, and governs the use of all weapons,
         means and methods of warfare, those of the past, those of the present and those of the future, and is,
         consequently, applicable regardless of the technology used.

    3.   IHL applies fully to the development and use of LAWS.

    4.   In cases not covered by the CCW and its annexed Protocols or by other international agreements, the civilian
         population and the combatants at all times remain under the protection and authority of the principles of
         international law derived from established custom, from the principles of humanity and from the dictates of
         public conscience.

    5.   Context-appropriate human judgement and control is needed to ensure the use and effects of LAWS are in
         compliance with international law, in particular IHL, including the principles and requirements of distinction,
         proportionality and precautions in attack.

                   19.     During previous sessions of the Group, most of Box II already enjoyed broad support,
                   as it draws on language from past reports adopted by the Group, the Guiding Principles, and
                   the preamble of the CCW, particularly the first three paragraphs. Some changes were made
                   following requests put forward by delegations during the first 2025 session, including a
                   revision of paragraph 4 to make it less specific to LAWS. The Chair noted that paragraph 5
                   retained the notion of “context-appropriate human judgement and control”, as it represents
                   an effort to bridge divergences with regard to the level of control over LAWS necessary to
                   be able to use them in accordance with international law, in particular IHL.
                   20.    Delegations broadly welcomed paragraphs 1 to 3 and reiterated the importance of
                   referencing existing IHL and previously agreed language. A number of delegations also
                   supported the revised paragraph 4, noting its alignment with the Martens Clause, and the
                   CCW preamble.
                   21.     Most exchanges focused on paragraph 5, particularly on the concept of “context-
                   appropriate human judgement and control”. Many delegations expressed support for the
                   term while others voiced reservations, noting that combining “judgment” and “control” could
                   lead to divergent interpretations and conflate the two notions. Others indicated their support
                   for “context-appropriate human judgement and control”, provided that its meaning is clearly
                   defined in the text. Several delegations welcomed a newly submitted working paper by a
                   group of States, which offered concrete elements to develop a shared understanding of the
                   notion.4

      C.     Box III

             22.   In Box III, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 12 May 2025 as corrected on 20
             August 2025) proposed the following elements:
1.   It is prohibited in all circumstances to use LAWS which are of a nature to cause superfluous injury or
     unnecessary suffering, which are inherently indiscriminate, or which are otherwise incapable of being used in
     compliance with IHL.

2.   LAWS may only be used in accordance with IHL, including the principles and requirements of distinction,
     proportionality and precautions in attack.

3.   It is prohibited in all circumstances to make the civilian population as such, individual civilians or civilian
     objects the object of an attack by LAWS.

4.   It is prohibited to use LAWS if their effects in attack cannot be anticipated and controlled, as required by IHL
     in the circumstances of their use.

5.   The prohibitions set forth in the preceding paragraphs must be taken into account in the design, development,
     and use of LAWS.

6.   In order to uphold compliance with IHL, States should ensure context-appropriate human judgement and
     control in the use of LAWS, through, inter alia, the following measures:

        (a) Ensure the effects of LAWS are adequately predictable, reliable, traceable and explainable to those
            responsible for their use.

        (b) Ensure that LAWS are operated under a responsible chain of command and control. This includes
            ensuring assessment of legal obligations and ethical considerations by a human, in particular, with
            regard to the effects of the selection and engagement functions.

        (c) Ensure that LAWS’ mission parameters with regard to the types of targets, duration, geographical
            scope, and scale of the operation cannot be modified by the system without context-appropriate human
            judgement and control.

        (d) Control, limit or otherwise restrict the types of targets, duration, geographical scope, and scale of the
            operation of LAWS, including through, as appropriate

                  (i) Ensuring that LAWS can be deactivated in a timely manner and/or incorporate self-destruct,
                       self-deactivation or self-neutralization mechanisms;

                  (ii) Limiting the number of engagements that LAWS can undertake autonomously;

                  (iii) Limiting the operation of LAWS to a defined perimeter in particular to reduce the likelihood
                        of harm to civilians or damage to civilian objects;

                  (iv) Restrict the use of LAWS to objects that are military objectives by nature;

                  (v) Limiting real-time machine learning with regard to target selection and engagement functions.

             23.    The Chairperson introduced Box III by noting that paragraphs 1 to 5 reaffirm a number
             of principles agreed within existing IHL, while not reinterpreting existing obligations. Then
             the Chairperson indicated that paragraph 6 and its subparagraphs aim to clarify the link
             between IHL and the specific measures designed to ensure compliance with IHL. Paragraph
             6 also serves as a way to elaborate the degree of human judgement and control needed to
             enable compliance with IHL.
             24.    Many delegations reiterated support for the so-called “two-tier approach”, containing
             both prohibitions and regulations, and underlined that elements in Box III could serve as core
             components of a possible future instrument.
             25.     Delegations broadly expressed support for paragraphs 1 to 4, recognizing that they
             reflect existing obligations under IHL and the Guiding Principles adopted by the Group in
             2019. Several delegations proposed textual refinements to enhance clarity and consistency,
             including merging paragraphs, replacing “controlled” with “limited” in paragraph 4, and

                 26.     However, paragraph 3 prompted diverging views. While many delegations supported
                 its intent to prohibit attacks by LAWS against civilians and civilian objects, concerns were
                 raised regarding the phrase “in all circumstances.” Some delegations were concerned that
                 this would also include civilians directly participating in hostilities. Consequently, several
                 delegations called for alignment with Article 51 of Additional Protocol I to the Geneva
                 Conventions.
                 27.     Several delegations had diverging views on 6A, in particular on whether to use the
                 concepts “predictable, reliable, traceable and explainable”. Some delegations argued that
                 these terms are all non-legal terms and that they should be deleted, while others stated they
                 would like to see them retained. Again, others saw merit in retaining a version of
                 “predictable” and “reliable”, possibly through an alternative wording, and omitting
                 “traceable” and “explainable” as there is no common understanding on what these mean.
                 28.    Divergent views were also expressed regarding paragraph 6B, particularly concerning
                 the reference to “ethical considerations” as factors to be assessed by a human. Many
                 delegations emphasized that, unlike legal obligations, ethical considerations do not have a
                 legal basis and therefore should not be included or not be presented on an equal footing.
                 However, other delegations took the floor to support the inclusion of ethical considerations,
                 arguing that LAWS raise new ethical questions that warrant reflection in the rolling text.
                 29.     Delegations engaged in detailed exchanges on paragraph 6D and its subparagraphs,
                 which propose potential limitations and restrictions for the use of LAWS. Several delegations
                 argued that subparagraph 6D(ii), concerning the limitation on the number of autonomous
                 engagements, is incompatible with existing IHL. However, several other delegations
                 supported its retention, viewing it as an important safeguard to reduce risks. Regarding
                 paragraph 6D(iv), numerous delegations expressed concern that the phrase “by nature”
                 introduces a narrower definition of military objectives than found in other IHL instruments
                 and considered it overly restrictive. Regarding 6D(v), which invites limitation on real-time
                 machine learning in target selection and engagement functions, several delegations called for
                 its deletion, while others advocated for measures to mitigate associated risks.

          D.     Box IV

                 30.   In Box IV, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 12 May 2025 as corrected on 20
                 August 2025) proposed the following elements:
    Prior to potential deployment and as applicable throughout the entire life cycle of LAWS, States should:

    1.   Conduct legal reviews to determine, in accordance with their obligations under international law, in the study,
         development, acquisition, or adoption of LAWS, whether its deployment would, in some or all circumstances,
         be prohibited by international law. In this context, the voluntary exchange of relevant practices between States
         is encouraged, bearing in mind national security considerations or commercial restrictions on proprietary
         information.

    2.   Ensure there is context-appropriate human judgement and control across the LAWS lifecycle.

    3.   Ensure that the capabilities and limitations of LAWS, in its expected use, and its anticipated effects in different
         circumstances, are understood.

    4.   Ensure that any further development or modification to existing LAWS that have an impact on their target
         selection and engagement functions or on its anticipated effects, are reviewed to ensure compliance with IHL.

    5.   Conduct rigorous testing and evaluation, including realistic simulations of its use in operational environments,
         to enable a human user to have a reliable expectation of how LAWS will perform in the anticipated
         circumstances of its use.
    6.   Implement measures to detect, correct or mitigate, as much as possible, unintended bias in artificial intelligence
         capabilities, including bias in data sets and AI models.

    7.   Implement measures to detect, correct or mitigate, as much as possible, automation bias.

8.    States should ensure appropriate guidance, training and instructions for those in the responsible chain of
     command and human users of LAWS, in order to provide them with a proper understanding of the capabilities

              31.     The Chairperson explained that Box IV had been revised to clarify the obligation of
              States to conduct legal reviews in accordance with applicable international law, as reflected
              in paragraph 1. In response to concerns raised during the March session regarding bias, the
              Chairperson introduced a distinction between unintended bias in AI capabilities (paragraph
              6) and automation bias affecting human operators (paragraph 7). Paragraph 8 was updated to
              emphasize the importance of guidance, training, and instructions for those in the responsible
              chain of command and control, including direct users of LAWS. A new paragraph (paragraph
              9) was added to address the broader life cycle of LAWS, reaffirming that systems which
              cannot be used in compliance with IHL should not be manufactured, acquired, stockpiled, or
              transferred.
              32.    Many delegations welcomed the language in Box IV, noting its emphasis on the need
              for States to conduct legal reviews to ensure that the development and use of LAWS is in
              compliance with international law, and in particular with IHL.
              33.     Nearly all delegations expressed support for the paragraphs referring to the obligation
              of conducting legal reviews, in particular paragraph 1. However, paragraph 4 prompted
              further discussions, as it highlights the need for additional legal reviews when LAWS are
              updated and modified. Some delegations suggested qualifying the term “impact” with
              “significant” to avoid requiring a legal review for every minor change. One delegation,
              subsequently supported by others, proposed introducing “periodic reviews” to enable regular
              assessments of the weapon systems.
              34.    Regarding the notion of “context-appropriate human judgement and control”
              referenced in paragraph 2, several delegations reiterated their diverse positions expressed
              during the exchanges on previous Boxes or expressed doubts whether this notion should also
              be repeated in this Box.
              35.    In previous sessions of the Group, several delegations called for stronger language on
              the need to prevent and mitigate biases – particularly gender and racial biases. This time,
              there was widespread support for the way biases were addressed in the rolling text. Most
              delegations welcomed the change from “harmful” to “unintended” biases, as reflected in the
              current version, considering it a more neutral and technically appropriate term. However,
              some delegations argued that a certain degree of bias is inevitable since it is an intrinsic
              feature required for making the necessary distinction in target identification.
              36.   States welcomed the inclusion in paragraph 8 of the rolling text of the requirement to
              develop adequate guidance, trainings and instructions for those who will operate LAWS.
              37.    Some delegations welcomed the inclusion of paragraph 9, recognizing its effort to
              address the full lifecycle of LAWS. In this context, a few delegations recalled Guiding
              Principle (j),5 and emphasized the importance of indicating in the rolling text that the
              measures outlined do not hamper scientific and technological advancement.
              38.    As in the previous session of the Group, several delegations requested the inclusion
              of an additional paragraph to address the need to prevent the proliferation and diversion of
              LAWS by non-States actors.6 In addition, one delegation, supported by others, proposed the
              inclusion of a new paragraph to reflect security concerns which could affect the functioning
              of LAWS, such as jamming and spoofing.

          E.          Box V

                      39.   In Box V, the Chairperson’s rolling text (version 12 May 2025 as amended on 20
                      August 2025) proposed the following elements:
    1.   IHL imposes obligations on States, parties to armed conflict and individuals, not on machines.

    2.   States, parties to armed conflict, and individuals must at all times remain responsible and accountable in
         accordance with applicable international law for decisions with regard to LAWS since responsibility and
         accountability cannot be transferred to machines. This should be considered across the entire life cycle of the
         weapon system.

    3.   States must ensure individual accountability for the means or methods of warfare involving the use of LAWS in
         accordance with their obligations under IHL.

    4.   States must ensure accountability across the entire life cycle of LAWS to ensure compliance with applicable
         international law, including through the operation of such systems within a responsible chain of human
         command and control.

    5.   To promote human responsibility and accountability in the use of LAWS and to mitigate the risk of unintended
         engagements, States should provide:

            (a)       Readily understandable human-machine interfaces and controls;

            (b) Guidance (e.g., policies, doctrine, and procedures), consistent with applicable IHL, for personnel
                regarding the proper use of LAWS;

            (c) Training of personnel to understand such guidance and the capabilities and limitations of the LAWS’
                autonomous functions in the anticipated circumstances of its use; and

            (d) Appropriate rules of engagement or other directives or orders circumscribing the use of the weapon
                system in military operations, consistent with applicable IHL.

    6.   States must ensure effective domestic mechanisms for the investigation, reporting and redressal of incidents that
         may involve violations of IHL. These domestic mechanisms should address, where relevant, incidents related to
         the development and use of LAWS.

    7.   States must take all appropriate steps, including legislative and other measures to prevent and suppress
         violations of IHL relating to LAWS by persons or on territory under their jurisdiction.

                      40.    Upon introducing Box V, the Chairperson noted, that compared to the previous
                      version, paragraph 1 includes an overarching principle aligned with Guiding Principle (b) 7
                      about IHL obligations on human responsibility and accountability. Some new language was
                      introduced to further specify how human responsibility and accountability can be promoted.
                      41.    Overall, delegations welcomed this Box affirming that accountability and
                      responsibility cannot be transferred to weapon systems. Some delegations suggested that the
                      language could be streamlined, and that some of its paragraphs – given their significance –
                      might be better placed in other boxes of the rolling text. A number of delegations expressed
                      concern over inconsistencies in the terminology used around accountability and called for
                      greater alignment throughout the text.
                      42.    Some delegations observed that Box V does not explicitly reference the notion of
                      international responsibility of States and recommended the inclusion of a dedicated
                      paragraph to address this aspect.
                      43.    Several delegations positively noted that paragraph 5 and its subparagraphs outline
                      useful measures for States to promote responsibility and accountability in the use of LAWS.

 F.   Other matters

      46.     A group of 39 High Contracting Parties and three Observer States delivered a joint
      statement to share their view on the rolling text. The statement voiced support for the efforts
      of the Chairperson, his team and the Friends of the Chair, while noting that further work is
      needed to achieve an outcome by consensus. The statement emphasized that the current
      rolling text contains sufficient substance to address remaining divergences in the framework
      of a future process beyond the current mandate, while also stressing that these High
      Contracting Parties are fully prepared to continue working with other delegations to further
      refine and improve the text, with the fulfilment of the mandate as their constant guiding
      objective.

III. Way forward
      47.     During the second 2025 session, the Group completed a thorough reading of the
      rolling text and engaged in substantive discussions which allowed the Group to identify areas
      of convergence, to understand where divergences remain, and to consider concrete options
      for bridging them. The Group also held several additional rounds of exchanges on Box I,
      following textual suggestions by the Chairperson. These additional discussions deepened the
      collective understanding of the characterization of LAWS, especially with regard to the
      critical functions of LAWS. The Chairperson recognized further work is required,
      particularly in fostering a common understanding of “context-appropriate human judgement
      and control”. As a result, the session was widely regarded as productive, substantive, and
      constructive, making a valuable contribution toward fulfilling the Group’s mandate.
      48.     At the closing of the session, the Chairperson thanked all delegations for their active
      participation, constructive spirit and commitment demonstrated in the pursuit of the Group’s
      mandate. He informed delegations of his plans to circulate a Chair’s summary reflecting the
      discussions held during this second session, consistent with previous practice. The
      Chairperson also expressed his intention to share an updated version of the rolling text, based
      on the deliberations and proposals put forward during this session.
      49.     The Chairperson further announced plans to organize informal intersessional
      consultations to continue substantive exchanges on the elements and to begin preparing for
      the first 2026 session. These consultations will aim to facilitate progress toward a common
      understanding on key concepts and focus on a revised rolling text. The Chairperson shared
      his intention with regard to the handling of the report to be delivered to the Seventh Review
      Conference of the CCW in 2026. The Chairperson also outlined his plans for the first session
      in 2026, indicating that discussions will centre on the new version of the rolling text to ensure
      that the elaboration of a set of elements will remain the primary substantive focus of the
      Group’s work.

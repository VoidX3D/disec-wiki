# Sample Draft Resolutions

The delegation maintains **8** draft resolutions for the committee.

## 1. Ensuring Meaningful Human Control over Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems

**Committee:** DISEC  
**Sponsors:** Iran, Mexico, Austria, South Africa, New Zealand

PP1: Recalling the principles of the Charter of the United Nations and International Humanitarian Law, including the principles of distinction, proportionality, and precaution,

PP2: Noting with concern the rapid development of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems that could operate without meaningful human control,

PP3: Reaffirming that States bear responsibility under international law for all actions of their armed forces, including actions taken through autonomous systems,

PP4: Recognizing the legitimate security concerns of all States, including the right to self-defence under Article 51 of the Charter,

PP5: Expressing deep concern that the absence of universally accepted definitions and regulatory standards for LAWS undermines international efforts at governance,

> OP1: Calls upon all Member States to ensure that weapons systems retain meaningful human control over the selection and engagement of targets;

> OP2: Urges the Group of Governmental Experts on LAWS to develop a universally accepted definition of "meaningful human control" with full participation of developing States;

> OP3: Recommends the establishment of transparency and confidence-building measures regarding military AI development;

> OP4: Requests the Secretary-General to submit a report on the feasibility of a legally binding instrument on LAWS;

> OP5: Encourages all Member States to share best practices on maintaining human oversight in military AI applications;

> OP6: Calls for the establishment of a technical assistance programme to support developing countries in understanding AI governance.

## 2. Preventing an Uncontrolled Artificial Intelligence Arms Race

**Committee:** DISEC  
**Sponsors:** Mexico, Costa Rica, Ireland, Japan, New Zealand

PP1: Recognizing the potential of artificial intelligence to contribute to international peace, security, and sustainable development when responsibly governed,

PP2: Deeply concerned that the absence of regulatory frameworks for military AI may trigger an uncontrolled arms race increasing international tension,

PP3: Emphasizing the need for inclusive multilateral dialogue involving all Member States, particularly developing countries, in shaping AI governance,

PP4: Noting that the dual-use nature of AI technologies complicates traditional arms control approaches,

PP5: Recalling the successful precedents of arms control agreements including the NPT, CWC, and BWC in preventing weapons proliferation,

> OP1: Encourages Member States to adopt national moratoria on the development of fully autonomous lethal weapons systems pending international agreement;

> OP2: Calls for the establishment of a United Nations High-Level Panel on Military Artificial Intelligence to develop governance recommendations;

> OP3: Urges technologically advanced States to provide technical assistance and capacity-building to developing countries regarding AI safety and governance;

> OP4: Decides to include the item "Responsible Military AI Governance" on the agenda of the General Assembly at its next session;

> OP5: Requests the Secretary-General to establish a voluntary register of military AI development programmes.

## 3. Strengthening the Role of Developing Countries in LAWS Governance

**Committee:** DISEC  
**Sponsors:** Iran, South Africa, India, Brazil, Nigeria

PP1: Reaffirming the principle of sovereign equality of all States as enshrined in the Charter of the United Nations,

PP2: Emphasizing that all States, regardless of technological capacity, have an equal right to participate in shaping the governance of LAWS and military AI,

PP3: Noting with concern that the absence of inclusive participation risks creating regulatory frameworks that disproportionately benefit technologically advanced States,

PP4: Recognizing that developing countries face unique vulnerabilities from LAWS proliferation, including threats from non-state actors,

PP5: Acknowledging that the digital divide may translate into a military capability divide if governance mechanisms are not inclusive,

> OP1: Calls upon the Group of Governmental Experts to ensure equitable geographical representation in all LAWS-related negotiations;

> OP2: Urges the establishment of a Technology Access Fund to enable developing countries to participate in AI safety research and governance;

> OP3: Recommends that any future legally binding instrument on LAWS include provisions for technology transfer and capacity-building for developing States;

> OP4: Requests the Secretary-General to report on the impact of the digital divide on LAWS governance.

## 4. Establishing International Verification Mechanisms for Military AI

**Committee:** DISEC  
**Sponsors:** Norway, Australia, Germany, Japan, Sweden

PP1: Recognizing the difficulty of verifying compliance with international agreements governing autonomous weapons due to the software-based nature of AI,

PP2: Noting that traditional arms control verification mechanisms may not be sufficient for AI-enabled weapons systems,

PP3: Emphasizing that transparency and confidence-building measures can reduce uncertainty and build trust among States,

PP4: Recalling the importance of verification in successful arms control agreements such as the CWC and NPT,

> OP1: Calls for the development of new verification methodologies adapted to AI-enabled weapons systems;

> OP2: Encourages States to voluntarily share information about their military AI programmes, testing procedures, and safety mechanisms;

> OP3: Requests the Secretary-General to convene an expert group to study feasible verification measures for military AI;

> OP4: Recommends the establishment of an international database of military AI capabilities for transparency purposes.

## 5. Addressing the Accountability Gap in Autonomous Weapons Systems

**Committee:** DISEC  
**Sponsors:** Austria, Mexico, Switzerland, Brazil, Belgium

PP1: Reaffirming that States are responsible under international law for the actions of their armed forces,

PP2: Noting with concern that autonomous weapons systems may create an accountability gap where responsibility for unlawful acts cannot be clearly attributed,

PP3: Recognizing the importance of maintaining clear chains of responsibility for decisions involving the use of lethal force,

PP4: Emphasizing that accountability is a fundamental principle of international humanitarian law,

> OP1: Calls upon all Member States to establish clear national accountability frameworks for the use of autonomous weapons systems;

> OP2: Urges the International Law Commission to study the question of responsibility for unlawful acts committed by autonomous systems;

> OP3: Recommends that all autonomous weapons systems maintain auditable records of human decisions in the chain of command;

> OP4: Requests the Secretary-General to prepare a study on the accountability gap and propose solutions.

## 6. Promoting Responsible Military AI Through Confidence-Building Measures

**Committee:** DISEC  
**Sponsors:** Finland, Canada, Netherlands, Chile, Ghana

PP1: Recognizing that transparency and confidence-building measures can reduce the risk of misunderstanding and accidental escalation,

PP2: Noting that military AI development often occurs without adequate transparency regarding capabilities and intentions,

PP3: Emphasizing the importance of preventing miscalculation in situations where autonomous systems may interact unpredictably,

PP4: Recalling successful confidence-building measures in previous arms control regimes,

> OP1: Encourages States to establish bilateral and multilateral channels for sharing information about military AI development;

> OP2: Calls for annual transparency reports on military AI programmes to be submitted to the Secretary-General;

> OP3: Recommends the establishment of a communication mechanism to prevent accidental escalation involving autonomous systems;

> OP4: Urges States to conduct joint exercises and workshops on responsible military AI governance.

## 7. Regulating the Transfer of Autonomous Weapons Technologies

**Committee:** DISEC  
**Sponsors:** France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Poland

PP1: Recognizing the risk that autonomous weapons technologies may proliferate to irresponsible actors,

PP2: Noting that many AI components have dual-use applications that complicate traditional export controls,

PP3: Emphasizing the importance of preventing the transfer of autonomous weapons to non-state actors and entities involved in human rights violations,

PP4: Recalling the obligations of States under the Arms Trade Treaty regarding responsible arms transfers,

> OP1: Calls upon all Member States to establish export control mechanisms for autonomous weapons technologies;

> OP2: Urges the development of common international standards for assessing the risks of autonomous weapons transfers;

> OP3: Recommends enhanced information-sharing between States regarding suspected unauthorized transfers;

> OP4: Requests the Secretary-General to report on the effectiveness of existing export control mechanisms for autonomous weapons.

## 8. Protecting Civilian Populations from Autonomous Warfare

**Committee:** DISEC  
**Sponsors:** ICRC, UNHCR, Amnesty International (observer), Mexico, Costa Rica

PP1: Recalling the obligation of parties to armed conflict to protect civilian populations under international humanitarian law,

PP2: Deeply concerned that autonomous weapons systems may be unable to adequately distinguish between combatants and civilians in complex environments,

PP3: Recognizing that the speed and scale of autonomous warfare could increase the consequences of targeting errors,

PP4: Emphasizing that civilian protection must remain a central consideration in the development and deployment of military technologies,

> OP1: Calls upon all Member States to ensure that autonomous weapons systems undergo rigorous testing for civilian protection before deployment;

> OP2: Urges the establishment of mandatory post-deployment assessments of autonomous weapons incidents involving civilian harm;

> OP3: Recommends the creation of an international monitoring mechanism for tracking civilian harm from autonomous weapons;

> OP4: Requests the Secretary-General to report on the humanitarian impact of autonomous weapons in contemporary conflicts.

# Strategy & Resolution Approach

Iran's negotiation strategy combines support for inclusive multilateral governance with defence of the sovereign right to develop defensive asymmetric capabilities.

## Core Strategic Positions

### Position on LAWS

Iran supports international negotiations on LAWS but emphasizes the right of states to develop defensive capabilities. Advocates a balanced approach considering both humanitarian concerns and national security needs.

### Key Talking Points

Emphasize sovereignty and the right to self-defence · highlight the dual-use nature of AI · stress inclusive negotiations · address technological hegemony · support confidence-building measures.

### Proposed Solutions

Establish a multilateral LAWS governance framework · ensure technology-transfer provisions · create transparency mechanisms · develop verification measures · include developing nations in decision-making.

### Negotiation Strategy

Build coalitions with like-minded states, emphasize common interests, propose constructive compromises, and maintain flexibility while protecting core national interests.

## Resolution Strategies

### Push for inclusive governance framework

Advocate for a framework that ensures all states, regardless of technological capacity, have equal voice in LAWS governance. Oppose any instrument that preserves technological advantages of major powers.

**Key clauses:**

- Ensures equitable geographical representation in all negotiations
- Establishes Technology Access Fund for developing countries
- Includes provisions for technology transfer and capacity-building

### Defend sovereign right to asymmetric capabilities

Maintain that states facing security threats have the right to develop asymmetric capabilities including autonomous systems as deterrents.

**Key clauses:**

- Recognizes right to self-defence under Article 51
- Distinguishes between offensive and defensive autonomous systems
- Opposes discriminatory restrictions on developing state capabilities

### Promote meaningful human control

Support the principle of meaningful human control while ensuring it is defined in a way that is practical and non-discriminatory.

**Key clauses:**

- Calls for universally accepted definition of meaningful human control
- Requires human involvement in target selection and engagement
- Allows for varying levels of human oversight depending on context

### Address proliferation to non-state actors

Support measures to prevent proliferation of autonomous weapons to non-state actors while opposing restrictions that affect state sovereignty.

**Key clauses:**

- Calls for responsible technology transfers
- Opposes transfer to non-state actors and terrorist organizations
- Supports export control mechanisms

### Oppose unilateral coercive measures

Argue that unilateral sanctions and export controls violate sovereign equality and prevent developing states from accessing defensive technologies.

**Key clauses:**

- Calls for elimination of unilateral coercive measures
- Opposes extraterritorial application of national sanctions
- Supports multilateral approaches to technology governance

# PART II-A: HISTORICAL BACKGROUND AND DEVELOPMENT OF THE AGENDA TOPIC

> *Part II – Background & Foundations*

Evolution of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems and Military Artificial Intelligence from Early Automation to Contemporary Strategic Competition

The historical development of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) and Military Artificial Intelligence reflects a long evolutionary process in which military organizations have continuously attempted to enhance operational effectiveness through technological innovation. The movement from manually operated weapons to automated and intelligent systems has been shaped by advances in computing, robotics, communications, data processing, and artificial intelligence. While autonomous military technologies have become a major contemporary concern, their origins can be traced back to earlier attempts to reduce human involvement in dangerous military operations and improve the speed and accuracy of battlefield decision-making.

The foundations of military automation emerged during the twentieth century, particularly during periods of major technological competition such as the Second World War and the Cold War. Early examples of automation were primarily focused on mechanical systems, guided weapons, and defensive technologies rather than independent decision-making. The development of radar-guided defence systems, cruise missile technologies, and automated targeting mechanisms demonstrated the possibility of integrating machines into military operations. These systems, however, remained dependent on human operators for critical decisions regarding the use of force.

The Cold War significantly accelerated research into automated military technologies due to strategic competition between major powers. The United States and the Soviet Union invested heavily in missile guidance systems, surveillance technologies, early warning networks, and command-and-control infrastructure. These developments introduced the idea that information processing and rapid decision-making could provide strategic advantages during conflict. The emergence of computer technologies during this period created the foundation for future artificial intelligence applications in military environments (Singer, 2009).

The development of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) represented another important stage in the evolution of autonomous military systems. Initially designed for reconnaissance and surveillance purposes, UAVs allowed armed forces to gather intelligence without exposing human pilots to direct danger. During the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, improvements in sensors, communication networks, and satellite technology transformed UAVs from simple observation platforms into sophisticated military tools capable of conducting precision operations. The increased use of remotely operated drones in conflicts such as Afghanistan and Iraq demonstrated the growing importance of unmanned systems in modern warfare (Scharre, 2018).

The emergence of artificial intelligence introduced a fundamental shift from automation toward autonomy. Traditional automated systems follow predetermined instructions, whereas artificial intelligence systems are designed to analyse information, identify patterns, adapt to changing environments, and support decision-making.

This distinction became increasingly important as machine learning technologies improved and military organizations began exploring AI applications in intelligence analysis, surveillance, logistics, cyber operations, and autonomous platforms.

During the early twenty-first century, rapid advances in computing power, big data, machine learning algorithms, and robotics accelerated military interest in artificial intelligence. Defence organizations recognized that future conflicts would increasingly depend on information superiority, rapid decision-making, and the ability to process complex battlefield data. As a result, major military powers began investing in AI-driven capabilities as part of broader strategies for maintaining technological advantage.

The United States became one of the earliest countries to integrate artificial intelligence into defence planning. Through organizations such as the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA), the United States explored autonomous vehicles, intelligent systems, and AI-assisted decision-making. The Department of Defense identified artificial intelligence as a critical component of future military capabilities, emphasizing applications such as intelligence processing, predictive maintenance, autonomous systems, and command support (U.S. Department of Defense, 2023).

Similarly, China identified artificial intelligence as a strategic technology capable of transforming military power. Through its national AI development strategies and military modernization programmes, China emphasized the importance of intelligentized warfare, where artificial intelligence would become integrated into military operations, decision-making, and autonomous platforms. Chinese military thinking increasingly considers AI as a major factor in future strategic competition (Kania, 2017).

Russia has also pursued autonomous military technologies as part of its modernization efforts. Russian defence development has focused on autonomous vehicles, unmanned systems, robotics, and AI-supported military capabilities. Moscow views technological innovation as essential for maintaining strategic competitiveness, particularly in an environment where information warfare and advanced technologies play an increasingly important role.

The concept of LAWS gained significant international attention during the 2010s as advances in artificial intelligence raised concerns about weapons capable of independently selecting and engaging targets. Unlike traditional drones or automated defence systems, LAWS represent a potential shift where machines could perform critical functions traditionally carried out by human operators. This possibility generated debates regarding human control, accountability, international humanitarian law, and ethical responsibility.

International concern increased significantly following discussions within the United Nations framework. In 2013, states began examining autonomous weapons under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW), leading to the establishment of the Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on LAWS in 2016. These discussions focused on understanding technological developments, assessing legal implications, and exploring possible regulatory approaches. Although states have reached agreement on several principles, including the continued applicability of international humanitarian law and the importance of human responsibility, disagreements remain regarding whether autonomous weapons require prohibition or regulation (UNODA, 2024).

Humanitarian organizations have also played an important role in shaping the international debate. The International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) has highlighted concerns regarding the ability of autonomous systems to comply with humanitarian principles such as distinction and proportionality. The organization argues that decisions involving the use of lethal force require human judgment and accountability, emphasizing the importance of maintaining meaningful human control over weapons systems (ICRC, 2021).

The contemporary development of Military Artificial Intelligence reflects a broader transformation in international security. Conflicts in Ukraine, the Middle East, and other regions demonstrate the increasing role of AI-enabled technologies, autonomous drones, cyber capabilities, and algorithm-assisted military operations. While fully autonomous lethal weapons remain limited, the integration of AI into military systems has already changed the character of warfare.

The historical evolution of LAWS and Military AI therefore demonstrates a gradual movement from mechanical automation toward intelligent autonomy. This transformation has created both opportunities and challenges. While AI can enhance defence capabilities and reduce risks to human personnel, it also raises fundamental questions regarding responsibility, legality, ethics, and the future governance of warfare. Understanding this historical development is essential for evaluating how the international community should respond to one of the most significant technological challenges of the modern security environment.

# Part III-B: Europe and NATO

The European position on regulating Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) and Military Artificial Intelligence reflects a complex balance between humanitarian concerns, technological ambitions, alliance commitments, and evolving security threats. Unlike major military powers such as the United States, China, and Russia, European states generally place greater emphasis on international law, ethical governance, transparency, and human control over lethal decision-making. However, Europe is not a unified actor with a single position. Different states demonstrate varying levels of urgency depending on their geographical location, defence priorities, technological capacity, and perceptions of security threats. While Western European states often prioritize legal and ethical frameworks, Eastern European states increasingly emphasize military effectiveness and deterrence due to proximity to geopolitical tensions and conventional security threats.

The European Union (EU) has emerged as one of the strongest advocates of responsible artificial intelligence governance. Although defence policy remains primarily under national authority, EU institutions have increasingly addressed AI through broader regulatory frameworks focusing on transparency, accountability, safety, and human oversight. The European approach emphasizes that artificial intelligence systems, including those used in security and defence environments, must remain compatible with democratic values, human rights, and international humanitarian law. The EU has promoted the concept of “trustworthy AI,” which highlights human agency, technical robustness, privacy, transparency, and accountability as essential principles for responsible technological development (European Commission, 2021).

Within discussions on autonomous weapons, many European governments support maintaining meaningful human control over decisions involving lethal force. They argue that the use of military force must remain connected to human judgment because warfare involves complex moral and legal considerations that cannot be fully delegated to algorithms. European policymakers frequently emphasize that machines should not independently determine matters involving human life and death. This perspective has positioned several European states closer to humanitarian organizations and civil society groups advocating stronger international regulation.

Germany represents one of the most influential European voices supporting restrictive governance of autonomous weapons. German policy traditionally emphasizes international law, multilateral diplomacy, and humanitarian principles. Germany has repeatedly argued that decisions regarding the use of lethal force must remain under meaningful human control and that autonomous systems should not independently make life-and-death decisions (German Federal Foreign Office, 2023). Germany’s historical experience with military technology and its constitutional commitment to human dignity significantly influence its cautious approach toward autonomous warfare.

However, Germany does not advocate eliminating all military autonomy. Berlin recognizes the operational advantages of AI-enabled systems, particularly in defensive applications, intelligence analysis, logistics, and force protection. Germany supports the development of AI technologies that enhance military effectiveness while maintaining clear human responsibility. Its position reflects a distinction between autonomous assistance and autonomous lethal decision-making.

Germany’s approach is also influenced by its role within NATO. As European security challenges have intensified, particularly following Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, Germany has increased defence investment and recognized the importance of technological modernization. The challenge for Berlin is balancing increased military preparedness with its traditional preference for arms control and humanitarian regulation.

Italy represents another important European defence actor with a pragmatic approach toward military AI. As a NATO member with significant aerospace and defence industries, Italy recognizes artificial intelligence as a critical component of future military capabilities. Italian defence policy supports technological innovation in areas such as autonomous systems, surveillance, cyber operations, and intelligence processing. However, Italy also supports international discussions aimed at ensuring responsible development and maintaining human accountability.

Italy’s position generally aligns with NATO’s broader approach that emphasizes responsible use rather than prohibition. Rome considers autonomous systems valuable for improving operational efficiency and protecting military personnel. Nevertheless, Italy recognizes the importance of legal compliance and supports continued international dialogue through frameworks such as the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons.

Spain similarly supports responsible military AI development while emphasizing international humanitarian law and ethical considerations. Spanish policy generally aligns with EU principles regarding trustworthy AI and human oversight. Madrid supports the use of AI for defensive and operational purposes but remains cautious about systems capable of independently applying lethal force. Spain’s position reflects the broader European preference for regulation through international cooperation rather than unilateral restrictions.

The Netherlands occupies a particularly significant position because it has been one of the more active European states in discussions concerning autonomous weapons. Dutch policymakers have emphasized the importance of human control, legal accountability, and transparency in military AI. The Netherlands has supported international efforts to establish clearer norms regarding autonomy in weapons systems and has contributed substantially to research examining the legal and ethical challenges associated with autonomous warfare.

The Dutch position is influenced by its strong commitment to international law and its role as a host country for major international institutions. The Netherlands argues that future military technologies must strengthen security without weakening humanitarian principles. It has advocated for practical measures including clearer definitions, improved transparency, testing requirements, and responsible deployment standards.

Poland represents a different European perspective shaped by geographical proximity to Russia and concerns regarding regional security. Since Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, Poland has significantly expanded military modernization efforts and increased defence cooperation with NATO allies. Warsaw views emerging technologies, including AI-enabled systems and autonomous platforms, as important tools for strengthening deterrence and national defence.

Unlike some Western European countries that prioritize restrictions, Poland places greater emphasis on ensuring that NATO maintains technological superiority. Polish policymakers are more concerned about preventing strategic disadvantages caused by excessive restrictions on defence innovation. Nevertheless, Poland remains committed to NATO principles regarding responsible military AI and compliance with international humanitarian law.

Norway provides an example of a Nordic approach combining humanitarian concerns with advanced technological capability. Norway has traditionally supported arms control, international law, and humanitarian initiatives. It has participated actively in discussions concerning autonomous weapons and has emphasized the importance of human judgment in warfare. At the same time, Norway is a technologically advanced NATO member that recognizes the importance of AI for defence modernization, particularly in surveillance, maritime security, and Arctic operations.

Switzerland represents one of the strongest supporters of humanitarian regulation due to its neutrality, diplomatic traditions, and historical role in international humanitarian law. As the host country of the International Committee of the Red Cross, Switzerland has played an important role in discussions concerning the humanitarian implications of autonomous weapons. Swiss policy emphasizes legal clarity, accountability, and the preservation of human responsibility in decisions involving lethal force.

Canada, although geographically located in North America, is often considered within the Western alliance perspective due to its NATO membership and close security cooperation with European partners. Canada supports responsible military AI development while emphasizing compliance with international humanitarian law. Ottawa has participated actively in international discussions concerning autonomous weapons and has generally supported transparency, accountability, and meaningful human involvement.

NATO’s Position on Military AI and Autonomous Weapons

The North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO) has increasingly recognized artificial intelligence as a strategic technology influencing future defence capabilities. NATO’s approach does not support a prohibition of military AI or autonomous systems but emphasizes responsible development, interoperability, accountability, and compliance with international law.

In 2021, NATO adopted its Artificial Intelligence Strategy, highlighting principles including lawfulness, responsibility, explainability, reliability, governability, and bias mitigation (NATO, 2021). These principles aim to ensure that allied states develop and deploy AI technologies in ways consistent with democratic values and international legal obligations.

NATO considers AI essential for maintaining collective defence capabilities, particularly in areas such as intelligence, surveillance, cyber defence, logistics, and decision support. However, the alliance recognizes that autonomous weapons introduce unique risks, including unintended escalation, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and challenges regarding accountability.

The NATO position reflects the strategic realities faced by member states. While many allies support stronger international regulation, they are also concerned that adversaries may develop autonomous capabilities without similar restrictions. Consequently, NATO emphasizes responsible innovation rather than prohibition.

The war in Ukraine has further accelerated NATO’s interest in military AI. The extensive use of drones, electronic warfare, battlefield data analysis, and automated systems has demonstrated the growing importance of AI-enabled capabilities in modern conflict. NATO members increasingly view AI superiority as essential for maintaining deterrence and operational effectiveness.

European and NATO Bloc Analysis

Overall, Europe and NATO demonstrate a relatively balanced position between humanitarian regulation and military innovation. European states generally agree that human beings must remain responsible for lethal decisions, but they differ regarding the extent of international restrictions required.

Western European countries such as Germany, France, the Netherlands, and Switzerland tend to emphasize legal and ethical regulation. Eastern European members such as Poland and Baltic states prioritize technological readiness due to security concerns. NATO as a collective organization seeks to maintain technological competitiveness while promoting responsible use principles.

The main challenge for Europe and NATO is reconciling two competing priorities: preventing uncontrolled autonomous warfare while ensuring that democratic states are not technologically disadvantaged against potential adversaries. This tension remains central to future international negotiations regarding LAWS and Military AI.

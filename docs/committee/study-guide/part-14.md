# PART IX – IMPACT OF THE AGENDA TO THE PRESENT WORLD

Global Consequences of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems and Military Artificial Intelligence on Security, Humanity, and International Order

The emergence of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) and Military Artificial Intelligence represents a profound transformation in the global security environment. The impact of these technologies extends beyond military operations and affects international peace, strategic stability, humanitarian protection, economic competition, technological inequality, international law, and the future character of warfare. While artificial intelligence provides opportunities to improve military efficiency, reduce risks to soldiers, and enhance defensive capabilities, uncontrolled development may introduce unprecedented challenges to global stability.

Unlike previous military revolutions, the transformation created by Military AI is unique because it combines speed, autonomy, data-driven decision-making, and adaptability. Traditional weapons generally required direct human operation, whereas AI-enabled systems can increasingly analyse information, identify patterns, and support or execute military decisions at speeds beyond human capacity. This creates both opportunities and risks, making effective governance essential for ensuring that technological advancement contributes to security rather than instability.

The impact of LAWS and Military AI can therefore be examined through multiple dimensions, including international security, humanitarian consequences, military transformation, economic competition, technological inequality, and global governance.

1. Impact on International Peace and Security

One of the most significant impacts of Military AI is its influence on international peace and security. Autonomous systems have the potential to alter the balance of power among states by providing advantages in speed, intelligence collection, precision, and operational effectiveness.

For technologically advanced states, AI-enabled military capabilities may strengthen deterrence by improving surveillance, early warning systems, and defensive capabilities. Autonomous systems can monitor borders, detect threats, and respond rapidly to emerging situations.

However, the same technologies may increase instability by lowering the threshold for conflict. If states believe that autonomous systems can conduct military operations with fewer risks to human personnel, political leaders may become more willing to authorize the use of force.

Furthermore, AI-enabled weapons could accelerate the pace of warfare. Traditional military decision-making often provides time for human assessment, diplomatic intervention, and strategic reconsideration. Autonomous systems operating at machine speed may reduce this decision-making window, increasing the possibility of accidental escalation.

The risk becomes particularly serious in regions involving major power competition, such as the United States–China relationship, the Russia–NATO confrontation, and tensions in the Indo-Pacific. A technological arms race involving autonomous systems could create strategic instability similar to previous nuclear and conventional arms competitions.

2. Transformation of Modern Warfare and Military Doctrine

Military AI is fundamentally changing the way armed forces understand and conduct warfare. The traditional model of warfare based on human-operated weapons, physical strength, and numerical superiority is increasingly being replaced by information-driven warfare.

AI enables militaries to process enormous amounts of information collected from satellites, drones, sensors, communication networks, and intelligence systems. This allows commanders to make faster and more informed decisions.

Autonomous systems are also transforming battlefield operations. Drones, robotic vehicles, automated defence systems, and AI-supported command platforms allow militaries to conduct operations with reduced human exposure.

The transformation has led to the emergence of concepts such as network-centric warfare, algorithmic warfare, and intelligentized warfare. These concepts emphasize the importance of information dominance, technological superiority, and machine-assisted decision-making.

However, increased dependence on technology creates vulnerabilities. Military forces relying heavily on AI systems may become exposed to cyberattacks, technical failures, or manipulation of data. Therefore, technological superiority does not automatically guarantee strategic success.

3. Humanitarian Impact and Civilian Protection

The humanitarian consequences of autonomous weapons represent one of the most debated aspects of Military AI. Supporters argue that AI systems may reduce civilian casualties by improving targeting accuracy, reducing human error, and allowing more precise military operations.

For example, autonomous systems may provide better surveillance capabilities, identify threats earlier, and assist commanders in avoiding unnecessary civilian harm.

However, critics argue that autonomous weapons create serious risks for civilian protection. Warfare often involves unpredictable environments where decisions require contextual understanding, moral judgment, and interpretation of human behaviour.

Artificial intelligence systems may struggle to distinguish between civilians and combatants, particularly in urban conflicts where military and civilian populations are closely interconnected.

Additionally, the speed and scale of autonomous systems could increase the consequences of mistakes. A human operator may make one incorrect decision, but an automated system operating at high speed could potentially repeat errors across multiple operations.

The humanitarian challenge therefore lies in ensuring that technological efficiency does not undermine the fundamental principles of protecting human life during conflict.

4. Impact on Human Rights and Ethical Responsibility

Military AI raises important ethical questions regarding the relationship between humans and machines. The most fundamental concern is whether machines should ever possess the authority to independently decide matters involving human life and death.

Human rights principles emphasize dignity, responsibility, and accountability. Critics argue that allowing autonomous systems to determine lethal outcomes risks reducing human life to algorithmic calculations.

Another concern involves transparency. Many artificial intelligence systems operate through complex algorithms that may be difficult even for developers to fully explain. This creates challenges regarding accountability and legal review.

The ethical issue is therefore not only whether AI systems can perform military functions but whether they should be entrusted with decisions involving human survival.

5. Impact on International Law and Legal Governance

The emergence of autonomous weapons challenges existing international legal frameworks. International humanitarian law was developed around the assumption that humans make decisions during warfare.

While existing legal principles such as distinction, proportionality, and precaution remain applicable, autonomous systems create uncertainty regarding interpretation and enforcement.

A central legal challenge is determining responsibility when autonomous systems cause unlawful harm. Existing legal systems rely on identifying responsible individuals, but autonomous weapons introduce complex chains involving programmers, manufacturers, commanders, and operators.

The development of LAWS therefore requires adaptation of international legal frameworks to ensure accountability remains clear.

Without effective legal governance, autonomous warfare may create situations where violations occur without meaningful consequences.

6. Impact on Global Power Balance

Military AI has significant implications for the distribution of global power. Countries with advanced technological infrastructure, computing capacity, research institutions, and semiconductor industries are likely to gain strategic advantages.

The United States, China, and other technologically advanced states currently possess major advantages because of their AI research capabilities and defence industries.

However, emerging powers such as India, South Korea, Israel, and the United Arab Emirates are rapidly increasing their technological capabilities.

This creates the possibility of a new hierarchy of military power based not only on traditional factors such as territory, population, and military size but also on data availability, computing power, and technological innovation.

Countries lacking advanced AI infrastructure may become increasingly dependent on technologically dominant states for security technologies.

7. Impact on Developing Countries and Global Inequality

Developing countries face unique challenges regarding Military AI. While autonomous technologies may provide benefits in areas such as border security, disaster response, and peacekeeping, limited technological capacity may create dependency on foreign suppliers.

A major concern is that military AI could deepen existing inequalities between developed and developing states. Countries with advanced technological industries may gain disproportionate influence over international security decisions.

Many developing countries therefore emphasize that global governance mechanisms must ensure inclusiveness, technology access, and equal participation in decision-making processes.

The regulation of Military AI should not become another area where technological power determines diplomatic influence.

8. Economic and Technological Impact

The development of Military AI is also transforming global economic competition. Artificial intelligence has become closely connected with industries including semiconductors, robotics, cybersecurity, telecommunications, and advanced manufacturing.

Countries investing heavily in AI research may gain economic advantages beyond defence applications.

However, military AI development also creates risks of increased defence spending and diversion of resources away from social development priorities. Developing countries may face pressure to invest in expensive military technologies despite limited resources.

The economic impact of AI warfare therefore depends on whether technology development is managed responsibly and whether benefits are distributed fairly.

9. Impact on Arms Control and International Diplomacy

Military AI challenges traditional arms control approaches because autonomous technologies are difficult to define, monitor, and verify.

Traditional arms control agreements often focus on physical weapons that can be counted and inspected. AI systems, however, involve software, algorithms, data, and dual-use technologies.

This makes verification significantly more complicated.

International diplomacy must therefore develop innovative approaches involving transparency measures, ethical standards, confidence-building mechanisms, and information-sharing.

The future of arms control will likely depend less on restricting physical weapons alone and more on governing technological processes.

10. Long-Term Impact on Future Generations

The long-term impact of Military AI will extend beyond current conflicts and influence future generations. Decisions made today regarding autonomous weapons governance will shape the future relationship between humans and machines in warfare.

If responsibly managed, AI could contribute to safer defence systems, improved peacekeeping operations, and reduced risks to military personnel.

However, if developed without sufficient regulation, autonomous warfare could create a future where conflicts become easier to initiate, harder to control, and more difficult to resolve.

The challenge for the international community is therefore ensuring that technological progress remains guided by human values, ethical responsibility, and international cooperation.

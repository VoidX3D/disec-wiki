# PART XI – POSSIBLE WAY THROUGH THE AGENDA

Potential Approaches and International Roadmap for Responsible Governance of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems and Military Artificial Intelligence

The regulation of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) and Military Artificial Intelligence requires a balanced approach that recognizes both the opportunities and risks associated with emerging military technologies. A complete rejection of artificial intelligence in defence applications may be unrealistic due to the strategic, technological, and security interests of states. At the same time, allowing unrestricted development and deployment of autonomous weapons could create serious risks to international peace, humanitarian protection, and accountability.

Therefore, the most practical path forward requires establishing a comprehensive governance framework that promotes responsible innovation while preventing unacceptable levels of autonomy in warfare. Such a framework must combine international law, ethical principles, technical standards, transparency mechanisms, and cooperation among states, international organizations, and technological communities.

The future governance of Military AI should not focus only on restricting technology but on ensuring that technological advancement remains controlled by human decision-makers and consistent with international humanitarian principles.

1. Establishing a Universal Definition and Classification Framework

One of the first requirements for effective regulation is developing a commonly accepted definition of LAWS and different levels of military autonomy. The absence of a universal definition remains one of the greatest obstacles to international agreement.

Different states currently interpret autonomy differently. Some define autonomous weapons narrowly as systems capable of independently selecting and engaging targets, while others include broader categories involving automated decision-support systems.

Without a shared understanding, international negotiations cannot establish effective rules.

A possible solution is the creation of a classification system based on levels of autonomy, such as:

• Human-operated systems where all decisions remain under direct human control

• Human-supervised systems where machines perform certain functions, but humans retain authority

• Highly autonomous systems where machines perform significant operational functions

• Fully autonomous lethal systems capable of independently selecting and engaging targets

2. Developing an International Treaty or Binding Regulatory Framework

One possible approach is the creation of an international legal instrument specifically addressing autonomous weapons.

Several states, humanitarian organizations, and civil society groups have supported a legally binding treaty that would prohibit certain categories of autonomous weapons and regulate others.

## A future agreement could include

• Prohibition of autonomous systems operating without meaningful human control

• Restrictions on weapons capable of independently selecting and attacking humans

• Obligations for testing and verification before deployment

• Requirements for accountability mechanisms

• Transparency measures between states

However, achieving a treaty would require balancing different national interests. Some technologically advanced states may oppose strict restrictions because they believe autonomous systems provide legitimate defence advantages.

Therefore, a realistic agreement may require a gradual approach beginning with political commitments, confidence-building measures, and shared principles before moving toward legally binding obligations.

3. Ensuring Meaningful Human Control

Maintaining human control should remain the central principle of international governance.

Any future framework should establish that humans must remain responsible for decisions involving lethal force. This does not necessarily require eliminating all forms of automation but ensuring that machines do not independently determine matters of life and death.

## Meaningful human control should include

Human Understanding: Operators and commanders must understand how autonomous systems function, their limitations, and possible risks.

Human Ability to Intervene: Military personnel must possess the ability to stop, modify, or override autonomous actions when necessary.

Human Responsibility: Individuals and institutions must remain legally accountable for decisions involving autonomous systems.

This principle would preserve the connection between technological capability and human responsibility.

4. Creating Strong Accountability and Legal Responsibility Mechanisms

A major requirement for responsible military AI governance is establishing clear accountability structures.

Future frameworks should clarify responsibility throughout the entire lifecycle of autonomous weapons, including:

• Research and development

• Manufacturing

• Testing

• Deployment

• Operational use

• Post-conflict assessment

Military commanders should remain responsible for authorizing the use of autonomous systems, while developers and manufacturers should ensure reliability and safety.

Additionally, states should maintain detailed records of autonomous system operations to support investigation when incidents occur.

Accountability mechanisms would prevent situations where responsibility disappears because decisions involve complex technological systems.

5. Establishing International Testing and Verification Standards

Autonomous weapons should undergo rigorous testing before deployment. Unlike traditional weapons, AI systems can behave unpredictably due to changing environments, data limitations, and software vulnerabilities.

## International standards should require

• Technical reliability testing

• Cybersecurity evaluation

• Bias assessment

• Human oversight testing

• Compliance with international humanitarian law

Verification mechanisms could involve independent assessments, reporting requirements, and international monitoring systems.

Although complete verification may be difficult due to technological secrecy, establishing common testing standards would increase confidence among states.

6. Developing Responsible Military Artificial Intelligence Principles

A global framework should promote responsible AI principles applicable to military applications. These principles should include:

• Lawfulness: Military AI systems must operate within international law, including humanitarian and human rights obligations.

• Responsibility: Human actors must remain responsible for decisions involving force.

• Transparency: States should provide information regarding their approaches to military AI governance.

• Reliability: Systems must function safely and predictably under operational conditions.

• Security: AI systems must be protected against cyber threats and manipulation.

• Human-Centred Development: Technology should support human decision-making rather than replace human responsibility.

These principles would provide a foundation for international cooperation.

7. Strengthening International Cooperation and Confidence-Building Measures

Due to the global nature of artificial intelligence development, cooperation between states is essential.

Confidence-building measures can reduce misunderstandings and prevent unnecessary escalation. These measures may include:

• Sharing national military AI principles

• Establishing communication channels during crises

• Conducting international expert meetings

• Developing common terminology

• Promoting scientific cooperation

Such mechanisms can improve trust without requiring states to reveal sensitive military information.

8. Preventing Proliferation and Irresponsible Technology Transfer

Future governance must address the spread of autonomous weapons technologies.

Because many AI components have civilian applications, controlling proliferation is more complicated than traditional arms control.

## Possible measures include

• Responsible export controls

• Monitoring high-risk technologies

• Preventing transfers to non-state actors

• International cooperation against illegal acquisition

These measures should balance security concerns with legitimate technological development.

9. Promoting Inclusion of Developing Countries in Global Governance

A sustainable regulatory framework must include perspectives from developing countries.

Many developing states are concerned that Military AI governance may become dominated by technologically advanced countries. Inclusive participation is necessary to ensure that international rules reflect global interests rather than only the priorities of powerful states.

## International organizations should support developing countries through

• Technical assistance

• Research cooperation

• Capacity-building programmes

• Access to knowledge regarding responsible AI governance

This approach would make global regulation more legitimate and effective.

10. Strengthening the Role of International Organizations

International institutions have a crucial role in coordinating global responses.

The United Nations can provide a diplomatic platform for negotiations and norm development. The Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons remains an important mechanism for dialogue.

Organizations such as the International Committee of the Red Cross can continue providing humanitarian guidance, while institutions such as UNIDIR and SIPRI can contribute research and policy analysis.

NATO and regional organizations can promote responsible military AI standards among member states.

A coordinated international approach is necessary because autonomous weapons affect global security rather than only individual countries.

11. Encouraging Ethical Research and Industry Responsibility

Technology companies and researchers also have important responsibilities because many AI capabilities originate from civilian innovation.

## Private companies developing artificial intelligence should consider

• Ethical implications of military applications

• Security risks

• Potential misuse

• Transparency regarding capabilities

Researchers should contribute to developing safer AI systems and participate in international discussions regarding responsible innovation.

The private sector cannot be excluded from governance because much of AI development occurs outside traditional military institutions.

12. Developing a Long-Term Global Governance Model

The regulation of Military AI should be viewed as an ongoing process rather than a single agreement. Artificial intelligence will continue evolving, and future technologies may create new challenges.

## A long-term governance model should include

• Continuous scientific assessment

• Periodic review mechanisms

• International cooperation

• Adaptable legal frameworks

• Monitoring of technological developments

The objective should be creating a system capable of responding to future innovations while protecting international security.

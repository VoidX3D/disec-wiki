# PART VIII – CURRENT WORLD SCENARIO OF THE AGENDA IN CONTEMPORARY ISSUES

Contemporary Global Developments and the Increasing Role of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems and Military Artificial Intelligence in Modern Security

The contemporary international security environment demonstrates that Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) and Military Artificial Intelligence are no longer theoretical concepts limited to future warfare scenarios. Instead, artificial intelligence-enabled technologies are increasingly influencing present-day military operations, strategic competition, intelligence gathering, surveillance, targeting processes, and battlefield decision-making. Although fully independent weapons capable of making unrestricted lethal decisions remain limited, many modern conflicts already involve varying levels of autonomy through unmanned aerial systems, algorithm-assisted targeting, automated defence systems, cyber operations, and AI-supported military planning.

The current global scenario reflects a transition from traditional industrial warfare toward information-driven and algorithmic warfare. States are increasingly competing not only through conventional military strength but also through technological capabilities involving data, artificial intelligence, robotics, autonomous platforms, and cyber infrastructure. This transformation has intensified concerns regarding accountability, civilian protection, escalation risks, and the possibility of an international AI arms race.

The contemporary relevance of LAWS and Military AI is particularly visible in ongoing conflicts and strategic competitions across Eastern Europe, the Middle East, East Asia, and other regions. These developments demonstrate both the military advantages and humanitarian risks associated with autonomous technologies.

1. Ukraine–Russia Conflict: The First Major Battlefield Experiment of AI-Enabled Warfare

The conflict between Russia and Ukraine has become one of the most significant examples of how emerging technologies are transforming modern warfare. Although neither side has deployed fully autonomous lethal weapons in the strictest sense, the conflict has demonstrated extensive use of AI-supported military technologies, including drones, autonomous navigation systems, electronic warfare, intelligence analysis, and automated surveillance.

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have become among the most important technologies in the conflict. Both Russian and Ukrainian forces have relied heavily on drones for reconnaissance, artillery coordination, battlefield observation, and targeted attacks. The widespread use of relatively inexpensive drones has changed traditional military calculations by allowing smaller units to conduct surveillance and precision strikes previously requiring expensive aircraft.

Ukraine has integrated artificial intelligence into battlefield operations through systems designed to analyse satellite imagery, identify military movements, and support targeting decisions. Commercial AI technologies have also been used to process large volumes of information collected from various sources.

Russia has similarly expanded its use of drones, electronic warfare systems, and autonomous technologies. The conflict demonstrates how AI can accelerate decision-making by reducing the time between identifying a target and conducting an operation.

However, the Ukraine conflict also highlights significant risks. The increasing speed of automated targeting systems creates concerns about accidental escalation and reduced human oversight. As warfare becomes increasingly dependent on algorithms and automated systems, the distinction between human-controlled and machine-assisted warfare becomes more complicated.

The conflict has therefore become an important case study influencing international discussions on future military AI governance.

2. Israel–Hamas Conflict: AI-Assisted Targeting and Civilian Protection Concerns

The conflict between Israel and Hamas has generated significant international debate regarding the use of artificial intelligence in military operations, particularly concerning AI-assisted intelligence analysis and targeting systems.

Israel possesses one of the world’s most advanced military technology ecosystems and has developed extensive capabilities in surveillance, intelligence processing, unmanned systems, and automated defence technologies. AI systems have reportedly been used to analyse large amounts of intelligence data, identify potential threats, and support operational decision-making.

Supporters of AI-assisted military technologies argue that these systems can improve accuracy, process information faster, and reduce risks to military personnel. Advanced technologies may allow armed forces to identify threats more efficiently and respond rapidly to security challenges.

However, humanitarian organizations and international observers have raised concerns regarding the possibility of excessive reliance on automated systems, particularly in densely populated areas. The use of AI-supported targeting raises questions regarding human verification, proportionality assessments, civilian protection, and accountability.

The conflict demonstrates one of the central dilemmas of military AI: whether technological precision can adequately replace human judgment in complex environments where civilian and military actors are closely interconnected.

The debate surrounding AI-assisted targeting in this conflict has increased international pressure for clearer standards regarding human control, transparency, and accountability.

3. United States–China Strategic Competition and the Global AI Arms Race

The strategic competition between the United States and China represents one of the most important drivers of military AI development. Both countries view artificial intelligence as a transformative technology capable of influencing future military superiority, economic power, and geopolitical influence.

The United States has invested heavily in AI-enabled defence capabilities, including autonomous systems, intelligence analysis, cyber operations, and advanced command networks. Through cooperation between government institutions, defence industries, and technology companies, Washington seeks to maintain technological advantage.

China has similarly identified artificial intelligence as a strategic priority. Beijing’s military modernization efforts emphasize the integration of AI, robotics, information systems, and autonomous technologies into future operations. China’s large technology sector, extensive data resources, and state-supported AI development provide significant advantages.

This competition has created concerns regarding a global AI arms race. Unlike traditional arms races, competition in artificial intelligence is characterized by rapid technological change, dual-use applications, and difficulty in monitoring development.

A major concern is that strategic competition may encourage states to prioritize technological superiority over safety and regulation. The pressure to avoid falling behind competitors may reduce willingness to support restrictive international agreements.

4. China–Taiwan Security Tensions and Autonomous Warfare Concerns

The Taiwan Strait represents another important scenario where military AI may influence future security dynamics. China considers Taiwan a core national interest, while Taiwan seeks to maintain its security and autonomy.

Both sides have increased investment in advanced military technologies. China has developed capabilities involving drones, cyber operations, AI-supported intelligence systems, and autonomous platforms. Taiwan has focused on asymmetric defence strategies involving surveillance, unmanned systems, and advanced information technologies.

The possibility of conflict in the Taiwan Strait raises concerns regarding the role of autonomous systems in high-intensity warfare. A conflict involving major technological powers could involve extensive use of AI-enabled surveillance, cyber operations, electronic warfare, and autonomous platforms.

The speed of AI-driven decision-making could increase escalation risks, particularly in a crisis situation where human decision-makers have limited time to assess information.

Therefore, the Taiwan Strait represents a critical example of how military AI could influence strategic stability.

5. Expansion of Autonomous Drone Warfare

One of the most visible contemporary developments related to military AI is the rapid expansion of drone warfare. Unmanned aerial systems have become increasingly accessible due to advances in commercial technology, reduced production costs, and improvements in artificial intelligence.

## Modern drones can perform functions including

• Surveillance and reconnaissance

• Target identification

• Navigation

• Communication

• Precision attacks

• Battlefield coordination

The increasing availability of drones has transformed military capabilities by allowing smaller states and non-state actors to acquire advanced operational tools.

Examples from conflicts in Ukraine, the Middle East, and Africa demonstrate that drone technology is no longer restricted to major military powers.

This development creates significant challenges for arms control because many drone components are commercially available and difficult to regulate.

6. Rise of AI-Enabled Cyber Warfare

Military AI is also transforming the cyber domain. States increasingly use artificial intelligence for cyber defence, threat detection, information warfare, and network analysis.

At the same time, AI can assist malicious actors in developing sophisticated cyberattacks, identifying vulnerabilities, and conducting information operations.

Cyber warfare combined with autonomous systems creates new security risks because military infrastructure increasingly depends on digital networks.

A successful cyberattack against autonomous weapons could create unpredictable consequences, particularly if systems operate at high speed with limited human intervention.

7. Increasing Role of Non-State Actors

A major contemporary concern is the possibility that autonomous technologies could spread beyond state militaries. Terrorist organizations, criminal networks, and insurgent groups may exploit commercially available drones, AI software, and automated systems.

The accessibility of civilian technologies creates challenges for traditional arms control mechanisms. Unlike nuclear weapons, autonomous systems do not necessarily require large industrial infrastructure.

This raise concerns that future conflicts may involve autonomous capabilities used by actors outside traditional military structures.

8. International Diplomatic Developments (2024–2026)

Recent years have witnessed increased international attention toward responsible military AI governance. Governments, international organizations, and expert groups have accelerated discussions regarding principles, transparency, and accountability.

The United Nations has continued emphasizing the importance of addressing emerging military technologies through international cooperation. Several countries have supported developing norms ensuring human responsibility and preventing uncontrolled autonomous warfare.

At the same time, disagreements remain regarding whether international regulation should involve a complete prohibition of certain systems or flexible guidelines allowing responsible innovation.

The period between 2024 and 2026 has therefore represented a critical phase in shaping the future governance of military AI.

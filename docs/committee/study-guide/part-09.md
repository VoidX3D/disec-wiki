# PART IV – INTERCONTINENTAL REPRESENTATION OF THE AGENDA WITH HIGH RATIO OF LAWS AND MILITARY AI INFLUENCE

Global Distribution and Strategic Influence of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems and Military Artificial Intelligence

The development and deployment of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) and Military Artificial Intelligence represent a fundamental transformation in the nature of global security. Unlike previous military technologies that were largely concentrated within a limited number of powerful states, artificial intelligence-enabled military capabilities are increasingly becoming distributed across multiple regions due to advances in commercial technologies, robotics, computing power, satellite systems, and unmanned platforms. The influence of LAWS and Military AI is therefore not limited to one continent but extends across North America, Europe, Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Latin America, with each region experiencing different levels of technological adoption, strategic importance, and regulatory challenges.

The global landscape of military AI is characterized by a concentration of advanced capabilities among technologically developed states, while emerging powers and developing countries increasingly seek access to these technologies for strategic, defensive, and economic purposes. The uneven distribution of AI capabilities creates a new dimension of international inequality, where computational infrastructure, semiconductor production, data availability, research capacity, and defence innovation determine military advantage. Consequently, the regulation of LAWS is not only a question of arms control but also a question of technological governance and global power distribution.

1. North America: Technological Leadership and Military AI Dominance

North America, particularly the United States and Canada, represents one of the most influential regions in the development of Military AI. The United States possesses the world's most advanced defence technology ecosystem, supported by extensive military research institutions, private technology companies, advanced computing infrastructure, and one of the largest defence budgets globally.

The United States has integrated artificial intelligence into numerous military domains, including intelligence collection, autonomous aircraft, cybersecurity, battlefield analysis, logistics, command systems, and missile defence. Programmes developed through organizations such as the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) and the Department of Defense have accelerated research into autonomous platforms, machine learning applications, and human-machine collaboration.

The American military approach increasingly emphasizes the concept of "algorithmic warfare," where AI assists commanders by processing enormous volumes of information faster than human operators. AI-enabled systems can analyse satellite imagery, identify potential threats, predict equipment failures, and improve operational planning. These capabilities provide significant advantages in speed, accuracy, and decision-making.

However, the United States has not pursued fully autonomous lethal decision-making as an official policy objective. Instead, American doctrine emphasizes human judgment and accountability in the use of force. The United States argues that responsible development, testing, and deployment of autonomous systems can provide humanitarian benefits by reducing human error and improving precision (U.S. Department of Defense, 2023).

Canada, while possessing smaller military capabilities, supports responsible military AI governance through its commitment to international law, NATO cooperation, and humanitarian principles. Canada generally emphasizes transparency, accountability, and human oversight.

North America therefore represents the highest concentration of military AI influence globally, combining technological leadership with significant strategic competition.

2. Europe: Regulatory Leadership and Responsible Military AI Development

Europe represents a unique combination of advanced technological capabilities and strong emphasis on humanitarian regulation. Unlike the United States, Europe’s approach is generally more cautious regarding autonomous lethal systems, influenced by its historical commitment to international law, human rights, and multilateral governance.

Countries such as France, Germany, the United Kingdom, and the Netherlands possess advanced defence industries and significant military research capabilities. France and the United Kingdom have developed sophisticated autonomous systems, including drones, naval technologies, and AI-enabled intelligence platforms. Germany and the Netherlands have focused more strongly on legal frameworks, ethical governance, and human control principles.

The European Union has played an important role in promoting responsible AI governance. Through its broader artificial intelligence regulatory approach, the EU emphasizes transparency, human oversight, risk management, and accountability. Although defence applications remain primarily under national authority, European principles increasingly influence discussions on military AI governance.

The European approach generally supports the development of AI for defensive purposes, intelligence, logistics, and operational assistance but expresses greater concern regarding autonomous systems capable of independently selecting and engaging human targets.

European states therefore represent a regulatory balancing model: maintaining technological competitiveness while seeking stronger international norms.

3. East Asia: Strategic Competition and Rapid Military AI Expansion

East Asia represents one of the most strategically significant regions for military AI due to competition among China, Japan, South Korea, and North Korea. The region demonstrates how geopolitical tensions accelerate technological military development.

China has emerged as one of the leading global competitors in artificial intelligence. Beijing considers AI essential for future warfare and has incorporated concepts of intelligentized warfare into military modernization strategies. China has invested heavily in autonomous drones, robotics, surveillance technologies, cyber capabilities, and AI-supported decision systems.

China’s military AI development is closely connected with its broader technological ambitions. The country’s large technology sector, semiconductor industry, and data resources provide significant advantages in AI development. Beijing considers technological superiority a central component of national security.

Japan and South Korea have also increased investment in military AI due to regional security challenges. Japan focuses on AI-enabled surveillance, maritime security, and defensive technologies, while South Korea emphasizes autonomous systems due to the threat environment created by North Korea.

North Korea represents a different category. Although it lacks the technological resources of China, Japan, or South Korea, it has demonstrated interest in cyber capabilities, drones, and asymmetric technologies. Autonomous systems provide smaller powers with opportunities to challenge technologically superior opponents.

East Asia therefore represents one of the highest-risk regions for military AI competition because technological development is closely linked with geopolitical rivalry.

4. South Asia: Emerging Military AI Capabilities and Strategic Competition

South Asia is becoming increasingly important in discussions regarding military AI due to the strategic competition between India and Pakistan, both nuclear-armed states.

India has emerged as the region’s most advanced military AI actor. Through defence AI initiatives, India is developing applications in surveillance, autonomous systems, cybersecurity, and intelligence analysis. The country views AI as essential for strengthening national security and maintaining strategic autonomy.

Pakistan has also explored AI-enabled defence capabilities, particularly unmanned aerial systems and surveillance technologies. Military AI development is influenced strongly by regional security competition with India.

Smaller South Asian states such as Nepal and Bangladesh have limited involvement in military AI development. Their priorities focus more on peacekeeping, diplomacy, development, and ensuring that emerging technologies do not create additional global inequalities.

South Asia demonstrates the challenge of regulating military AI among states with different technological capacities and security concerns.

5. Middle East: Autonomous Systems and Regional Security Competition

The Middle East has become an important region for military AI due to conflicts, security competition, and technological investment. Israel, Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the United Arab Emirates represent the primary factors influencing regional developments.

Israel possesses some of the world’s most advanced military AI and autonomous systems, particularly in drones, surveillance, and missile defence. Its security environment has encouraged rapid technological innovation.

Iran has developed significant drone capabilities and uses autonomous systems as part of asymmetric military strategies. Gulf countries, particularly the UAE and Saudi Arabia, have invested heavily in artificial intelligence as part of economic modernization and defence transformation.

The Middle East demonstrates how autonomous technologies can spread beyond traditional military superpowers and become important tools for regional powers.

6. Africa: Limited Capability but Significant Security Implications

Africa currently possesses comparatively limited military AI capabilities; however, the continent remains strategically important because of potential proliferation risks and security challenges.

Many African states lack the technological infrastructure required to independently develop advanced autonomous weapons. However, concerns exist regarding the transfer of autonomous systems from external suppliers and the possibility of their use in conflicts involving non-state actors.

Countries such as South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, and Egypt have shown interest in technological modernization but generally prioritize cybersecurity, surveillance, and defence support systems rather than autonomous lethal weapons.

The African perspective emphasizes preventing technological inequality and ensuring that emerging military technologies contribute to peace rather than conflict escalation.

7. Latin America: Humanitarian Regulation and Limited Militarization

Latin America has historically maintained strong commitments to arms control and peaceful international relations. Most countries in the region possess limited military AI capabilities and have not prioritized autonomous weapons development.

Countries such as Brazil, Argentina, and Mexico support technological development but emphasize international regulation, transparency, and humanitarian principles.

Brazil represents the region’s most significant defence technology actor and seeks to develop advanced capabilities while maintaining strategic autonomy.

Mexico has adopted one of the stronger humanitarian approaches, emphasizing human control and accountability in military applications of AI.

Comparative Regional Influence Ranking

The geographical distribution of LAWS and Military AI demonstrates that autonomous warfare is no longer a future possibility but an emerging reality shaping international security. While North America and East Asia currently possess the greatest technological influence, Europe plays a significant role in establishing regulatory norms, and regional powers in the Middle East and South Asia are increasingly developing autonomous capabilities.

The central challenge for the international community is ensuring that technological competition does not outpace ethical and legal governance. The future of military AI regulation will depend on balancing innovation with responsibility, strategic interests with humanitarian principles, and national security with global stability.

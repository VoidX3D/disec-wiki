# Rules of Procedure

RULES OF PROCEDURE OF MUN

## Attendance (Motion to Verify the Quorum)

At the commencement of each session and upon the Motion to Verify the Quorum, the

Chairperson shall call on all Member States in alphabetical order to declare their attendance

status. Member States may respond with "present" or "present and voting." Delegates who

respond with "present and voting" shall forfeit the right to abstain from voting on any

substantive matter.

If any Representative is absent during the Roll Call, they must notify the Chairperson upon

arrival and clarify their attendance status.

## Quorum

The quorum refers to the minimum number of delegates required to be present to open the

session for debate.

The quorum is considered met when at least one-third of all registered delegates are present

at the committee session. The quorum shall be verified through the Roll Call at the beginning

of each session.

## Setting the Agenda

Setting the agenda is the first matter to be addressed by the committee.

a. The motion to place a topic on the agenda must be made first. This motion requires a

second.

b. Delegates may propose only those topics that have been listed by the Secretariat in the

provisional agenda, i.e., the topics that were set by the Secretariat before the conference.

c. If there is no opposition to the motion, it is considered adopted.

d. In the event of opposition to the proposed order of the agenda, a Speaker's List shall be

established, with two or four delegates speaking “for” and “against” the order. The number of

speakers on each side must be equal.

## Debate

Debate may proceed in three distinct modes, depending on the stage of the session:

1. General Speaker's List

2. Moderated Caucus

3. Unmoderated Caucus

Upon the setting of the agenda, the Speaker's List is opened. This can either be done at the

discretion of the Chairs or by delegates proposing a Motion to Open the Speaker's List.

## Formal and Informal Sessions

The General Speaker’s List is part of the formal session, where delegates make speeches in

turn.

Moderated Caucus is a more structured, informal session, allowing for short, focused

speeches on a specific aspect of the agenda.

Unmoderated Caucus is an informal session where delegates can engage in free-form

discussions and negotiations.

## General Speaker’s List

The General Speaker's List reflects the order of speakers for the topic currently under

discussion and remains open for the entire duration of the debate on that topic.

The speaking time for each delegate on the General Speaker's List is set at 90 seconds. This

time limit may be altered through a Motion to Change the Speaker's Time.

The Speaker's List is exclusively open for the topic currently on the agenda and cannot be

used for discussions on other topics.

## Moderated Caucus

The moderated caucus is designed to facilitate and expedite the discussion of key issues

deemed essential and critical to the topic currently on the agenda.

The motion for a moderated caucus may be introduced by any delegate once the Floor is open

for Points and Motions. The delegate proposing the motion must specify the following:

The total time for the moderated caucus (which must not exceed 20 minutes).

The individual speaker's time for each delegate (which should not exceed the time set for the

General Speaker's List).

The purpose of the motion must be related to the topic currently under discussion and should

focus on a more specific aspect of the issue at hand.

A Simple Majority of the quorum is required for the motion to pass. If multiple moderated

caucuses are proposed, the committee will vote on them in descending order based on the

total time allotted for each caucus, from the longest to the shortest, as determined by the

Chairs.

## Unmoderated Caucus

The unmoderated caucus is the most informal mode of debate, allowing delegates to engage

in free-form discussions on all issues related to the topic on the agenda. During this time,

delegates can lobby for their interests, resolve complex questions, and collaborate on drafting

working papers and resolutions.

Any delegate can introduce a motion for an unmoderated caucus once the Floor is open for

Points and Motions. The delegate proposing the motion must specify:

1. The purpose of the motion,

2 . The total time allotted for the unmoderated caucus (which must not exceed 30 minutes).

A Simple Majority of the quorum is required for the motion to pass.

## Yields

A delegate who has been granted permission to speak by the Chairpersons has the right to

yield any remaining speaking time as follows:

1. Yield to Another Delegate:

The remaining speaking time is transferred to another delegate as specified by the delegate

speaking. The delegate receiving the yield may not yield further, except to the Chairpersons.

2. Yield to a Point of Information:

If the delegate is open to questions, the Chairpersons may grant the opportunity for another

delegate to ask an inquiry within the remaining speaking time.

Questions are not counted as part of the remaining speaking time, but the answers given by

the delegate will be.

The delegate who has yielded their time to questions may refuse to answer any question at

their discretion.

The Chairpersons are responsible for calling to order any inquiry that does not meet the

standards of decorum.

3. Yield Back to the Chairpersons:

If there is remaining time and the delegate does not wish to answer questions or yield their

time to another delegate, they may yield their time back to the Chairpersons. The

Chairpersons will then call upon the next delegate on the Speaker’s List or a delegate wishing

to speak.

## Points

A point is a formal request made by the delegates to the chair to address specific procedural

or personal matters during committee sessions that are not part of the main substantive

debate.

## Point of Personal Privilege

A delegate may raise a Point of Personal Privilege if they experience any personal discomfort

that hinders their full participation in the debate. This could include issues such as difficulty

hearing other speakers or the temperature of the room.

A Point of Personal Privilege may interrupt a speaker only in the case of poor audibility.

## Point of Inquiry

A delegate may raise a Point of Inquiry to seek clarification on aspects of the Rules of

Procedure from the Chairpersons.

Points of Inquiry cannot interrupt speakers and may only be introduced when the Floor is

open for Points and Motions.

## Point of Order

A delegate may raise a Point of Order if there is a discrepancy or improper application of the

Rules of Procedure by delegates or the Chairpersons.

The Chairpersons have the discretion to decide if the Point of Order is valid and to clarify any

irregularities.

The Point of Order cannot interrupt a speech.

## Point of Information

As explained previously, a Point of Information allows a delegate to pose a question or seek

clarification during the General Speaker's List or Moderated Caucuses. The response from the

delegate was questioned as part of their allocated speaking time.

## Right of Reply

The Right of Reply may be granted by the Chair (or President) to the delegate of any State

who requests it, either in writing or by raising their country nameplate, in cases where their

personal or national integrity has been affected or insulted by another delegate. This right

allows the affected delegate to respond to the issue raised, ensuring their dignity and that of

their nation are protected.

## MUN Position Paper Guidelines

Delegates are required to submit their Position Papers before the commencement of the

committee sessions. To ensure the integrity of submissions, AI-generated content must not

exceed 11%. Any Position Paper found exceeding this limit will receive a score of zero.

Additionally, delegates who fail to submit their Position Papers will not be considered for

awards.

Structure & Content

## Introduction

Provide a brief overview of the issue at hand.

Outline your country’s historical background related to the issue.

Explain your country’s current policies regarding the issue.

## Body

Present a detailed explanation of your country’s stance on the issue.

Support your position with facts, statistics, and evidence from credible sources.

Address potential counterarguments that other nations or perspectives may raise.

Propose specific solutions or policies that your country believes should be implemented.

## Conclusion

Summarise your country’s official stance on the issue.

Reiterate the importance of addressing the issue.

Provide a call to action or outline your country’s desired outcome in future discussions.

## References

Provide credible sources used in your research (e.g., government reports, UN documents,

academic journals, and reputable news sources).

AI-generated content is strictly prohibited.

Wikipedia is not an acceptable source.

What is a Directive?

MUN Crisis Directives are written requests that are sent to, read by, and approved or denied

by the Dias. Directives are your main tool for affecting the crisis.

To have the best chance of getting your directives accepted by the Dias, they need to be clear,

concise, and well-written. The objective of directives is to further your cabinet’s goals and/or

your personal goals.

No request or instruction, sent in a directive, becomes “true” unless you either get a positive

reply from the Dias or you see the outcome of your directive in the crisis news.

## Rules for Good Directives

●​ Provide Clear Instructions

●​ Be Concise and to the Point

●​ Create Your Plan Step-by-Step

●​ One Issue Per Directive

●​ When in Doubt, refer to Your Previous Directives

●​ Use Exact Numbers

## Communiqués in MUN

A communiqué is a formal statement or declaration issued by a committee, an individual

delegate, or a group of delegates in an MUN conference. It serves as an official document to

communicate a shared position, decision, or call to action.

## Purposes of a Communiqué

●​ Announcing a Joint Decision

●​ Expressing a Shared Opinion

●​ Calling for Action

## Types of Communiqués

●​ Individual Communiqué

●​ Joint Communiqué

## Format of a Communiqué

●​ Heading

●​ Body

●​ Closing

What is a Working Paper?

A working paper is an informal document used in MUN committees to outline ideas,

proposed solutions, or discussions before a formal draft resolution is created.

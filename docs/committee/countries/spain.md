## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Spain supports responsible military AI development while placing strong emphasis on international humanitarian law and ethical considerations. Madrid's policy generally aligns with EU principles regarding "trustworthy AI" and human oversight, reflecting the broader European preference for regulation achieved through international cooperation rather than unilateral restrictions. Spain recognizes artificial intelligence as a strategically important technology for defence modernization, particularly in surveillance, intelligence processing, logistics, and decision support.

Madrid supports the use of AI for defensive and operational purposes but remains cautious about systems capable of independently applying lethal force. Spanish officials consistently stress that decisions involving the use of force must remain connected to human judgment and that accountability for military operations must rest with persons, not algorithms. This places Spain within the group of European states that back meaningful human control without endorsing an outright prohibition of all autonomous technologies.

Spain has participated actively in the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) and the Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on LAWS, supporting the continued development of agreed principles and practical measures. Its approach is incremental and consensus-oriented: clearer definitions, transparency, testing and verification requirements, and responsible deployment standards. Madrid prefers strengthening existing frameworks and international law over negotiating restrictive new instruments in isolation from NATO and EU partners.

Within the European Union, Spain supports extending the AI Act's human-oversight and risk-management logic to security and defence applications where feasible. Spanish contributions to EU defence initiatives, including cooperative projects on drones, robotics, and AI-enabled systems, reflect a desire to develop these technologies responsibly while sustaining European industrial capacity. Spain's position is therefore pragmatic — open to regulation, committed to international law, and attentive to the operational needs of allied militaries.

## Strategic Interests

- Supporting European and international governance of military AI that preserves allied operational effectiveness.
- Ensuring Spanish and EU defence industries remain competitive in AI-enabled platforms such as unmanned systems and robotics.
- Reinforcing Spain's role in NATO's southern flank, including maritime security and surveillance in the Mediterranean and the Atlantic approaches.
- Upholding international humanitarian law and human oversight in the use of force.
- Balancing the EU's regulatory ambitions with the military modernization needs of member states.
- Contributing to a rules-based international order through inclusive, consensus-driven negotiation.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **NATO** — Supports the alliance's Artificial Intelligence Strategy, emphasizing responsible development, interoperability, and human accountability.
- **European Union** — Applies the "trustworthy AI" framework and coordinates positions with EU partners in the First Committee and the GGE.
- **CCW / GGE on LAWS** — Participates constructively in Geneva, backing definitions, principles, and practical standards.
- **OSCE** — Engages in European confidence-building measures on emerging military technologies.
- **EU Defence Initiatives (PESCO / EDF)** — Develops AI-enabled defence capabilities in cooperation with European partners.

## UN Voting Record

Spain has voted in favour of the General Assembly resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024), supporting the continuation of urgent international discussions. In the First Committee, Madrid aligns with the EU bloc on language regarding human control, international humanitarian law, and the GGE process. Spain favours building consensus on guiding principles and practical measures before committing to a legally binding instrument, reflecting its cautious, cooperation-first approach.

## Key Talking Points

- Autonomous technologies have legitimate defensive and operational value and should be developed and used responsibly.
- Decisions involving lethal force must remain under meaningful human control.
- International humanitarian law applies fully to AI-enabled weapons; no system may be exempt from accountability.
- Regulation should be pursued through inclusive international cooperation rather than unilateral restrictions.
- Clear definitions, transparency, and testing requirements should be agreed before a legally binding instrument is negotiated.
- European "trustworthy AI" principles should be extended to security and defence applications.
- Rules-based international order requires consensus-building that includes NATO, EU, and non-aligned states alike.

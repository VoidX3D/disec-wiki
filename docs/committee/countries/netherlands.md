## LAWS & Military AI Stance

The Netherlands occupies a particularly significant position in international discussions on autonomous weapons as one of the most active European states on the issue. Dutch policymakers have consistently emphasized the importance of human control, legal accountability, and transparency in military AI. The Netherlands has supported international efforts to establish clearer norms regarding autonomy in weapons systems and has contributed substantially to research examining the legal and ethical challenges associated with autonomous warfare.

The Dutch position is shaped by a strong commitment to international law and by the country's role as a host for major international institutions, including the International Court of Justice, the International Criminal Court, and the Organisation for the Prohibition of Chemical Weapons in The Hague. The Netherlands argues that future military technologies must strengthen security without weakening humanitarian principles. It has been among the most vocal advocates of practical measures, including clearer definitions of autonomy, improved transparency, testing and verification requirements, and responsible deployment standards.

The Hague has become an important venue for the LAWS debate. The Netherlands co-hosted a high-level international conference on autonomous weapons in 2019, gathering representatives from capitals to advance consensus on human control, and it continues to host formal CCW and GGE-related meetings and side events. This "capitals-level" engagement reflects the Dutch conviction that real progress requires political commitment at the highest levels, not only technical discussion among experts.

Within NATO and the EU, the Netherlands promotes the alliance's Artificial Intelligence Strategy and the EU's "trustworthy AI" framework, insisting that accountability, lawfulness, and explainability be applied to defence technologies. The Dutch Ministry of Defence has published its own vision on AI and autonomy, committing to human control over lethal effects and to transparency in the development and use of AI-enabled systems. The Netherlands therefore combines a strong humanitarian and legal orientation with pragmatic engagement in allied military AI programmes.

## Strategic Interests

- Advancing legally meaningful regulation of autonomous weapons, including human control, transparency, and testing standards.
- Reinforcing the Netherlands' identity as a hub of international law and dispute settlement in The Hague.
- Ensuring NATO and EU military AI capabilities develop within clear legal and accountability frameworks.
- Preventing an unregulated global arms race in autonomous weapons through consensus-building among states and civil society.
- Protecting the Dutch knowledge economy and defence research base in robotics, AI, and cyber technologies.
- Strengthening the rules-based international order, including through the role of international courts and the ICRC's humanitarian mission.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **NATO** — Champions responsible military AI within the alliance's Artificial Intelligence Strategy, including human accountability and interoperability.
- **European Union** — Applies the EU's "trustworthy AI" framework; a leader in coordinating European positions on LAWS in Geneva and New York.
- **CCW / GGE on LAWS** — One of the most active participants; host of CCW/GGE-related capitals-level debates and international conferences in The Hague.
- **OSCE** — Supports confidence-building measures on emerging military technologies in Europe.
- **Host of International Law Institutions** — The Hague's courts and bodies give the Netherlands a distinctive voice on accountability and the rule of law.

## UN Voting Record

The Netherlands has voted in favour of and supported the annual General Assembly resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024), and has been an active co-sponsor of strong language on human control. In the First Committee, it consistently works within the EU bloc to push for negotiation of a legally binding instrument while seeking to build bridges with humanitarian-aligned states and civil society. The Netherlands repeatedly calls for the GGE to be mandated to negotiate, arguing that political declarations and guiding principles are necessary but not sufficient.

## Key Talking Points

- The GGE must be empowered to negotiate a legally binding instrument on LAWS; voluntary principles alone will not prevent an arms race.
- Meaningful human control over lethal force must be defined and enshrined in international law.
- Transparency, testing, and verification requirements are essential and should be agreed as practical first steps.
- As host of major international legal institutions, the Netherlands stands for accountability and the rule of law in military AI.
- Regulating autonomy protects security as much as humanitarian values; unpredictable systems create escalation risks for everyone.
- Civil society, the ICRC, and scientific expertise should inform negotiations, not only military and diplomatic perspectives.
- NATO and EU members should lead by example through domestic commitments to human control.

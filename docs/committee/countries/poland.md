## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Poland represents a distinctive European perspective shaped by geographical proximity to Russia and acute concerns regarding regional security. Since Russia's invasion of Ukraine, Warsaw has significantly expanded military modernization efforts and deepened defence cooperation with NATO allies. Poland views emerging technologies, including AI-enabled systems and autonomous platforms, as important tools for strengthening deterrence and national defence against a demonstrated conventional threat.

Unlike several Western European countries that prioritize restrictions, Poland places greater emphasis on ensuring that NATO maintains technological superiority. Warsaw is concerned that excessive or premature restrictions on defence innovation could create strategic disadvantages precisely when Europe faces its most serious security challenge in decades. Poland therefore approaches LAWS discussions with a strong presumption in favour of preserving allied technological edge, while remaining committed to NATO principles on responsible military AI and compliance with international humanitarian law.

Poland's defence build-up is among the most rapid in NATO: it has announced defence spending at approximately 4 percent of GDP — among the highest shares in the alliance — and is modernizing across land, air, maritime, and drone capabilities. In this context, Warsaw supports the development of AI for intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance, air and missile defence, cyber operations, and decision support. Polish officials see the responsible use of AI as a force multiplier that helps a large but resource-constrained military deter more powerful adversaries.

In the CCW Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on LAWS, Poland participates constructively but resists moves toward a broad prohibition that could constrain NATO capabilities. Warsaw favours a negotiated outcome that keeps human beings responsible for the use of force while preserving the legitimate defensive applications of autonomy. It is also attentive to the risk that restrictive regimes could be exploited by adversaries — including Russia — that may not honour them, and it insists that any binding instrument include effective verification and that all major military powers participate.

## Strategic Interests

- Preserving NATO's technological superiority as the cornerstone of deterrence against Russia.
- Continuing Poland's rapid military modernization, including AI-enabled and autonomous systems, at roughly 4 percent of GDP in defence spending.
- Ensuring international rules on LAWS do not create strategic disadvantages for democratic states or hand advantages to non-compliant adversaries.
- Reinforcing allied forward presence and collective defence on NATO's eastern flank.
- Maintaining human accountability and compliance with international humanitarian law in the use of force.
- Integrating AI-enabled capabilities with NATO and US systems for interoperability and interoperability testing.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **NATO** — Supports the alliance's Artificial Intelligence Strategy, prioritizing technological edge, interoperability, and responsible use; hosts substantial allied forward presence.
- **European Union** — Engages with the EU's "trustworthy AI" framework while pressing for defence capabilities and the EU's strategic autonomy in security.
- **CCW / GGE on LAWS** — Participates in negotiations while guarding allied operational flexibility and insisting on universal participation and verification.
- **OSCE** — Works on confidence-building and transparency in European security, given Russia's war in Ukraine.
- **Three Seas Initiative / Bucharest Nine** — Coordinates regional security positions with Central and Eastern European allies.

## UN Voting Record

Poland has voted in favour of the General Assembly resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024), consistent with its support for continued international dialogue. In the First Committee, Warsaw aligns with the EU and NATO blocs but does not champion the strongest prohibitionist language. Poland's posture reflects its core tension: it supports the development of international norms and human responsibility, while resisting constraints that could undermine the technological deterrence it regards as essential given the war in Ukraine.

## Key Talking Points

- Europe's immediate threat is Russia's aggression; military AI is a critical tool for deterrence and defence.
- Regulation must not deny NATO and democratic states the technological edge that underpins peace.
- Any binding instrument must be universal and verifiable — rules ignored by major powers would only weaken honest states.
- Human beings must remain responsible for decisions involving lethal force; Poland fully supports this principle.
- Responsible use, compliance with international humanitarian law, and interoperability matter more than symbolic prohibitions.
- Poland's rapid modernization, at roughly 4 percent of GDP, shows how AI-enabled systems strengthen collective defence.
- Confidence-building and transparency measures can complement negotiated rules without compromising deterrence.

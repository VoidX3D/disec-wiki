## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Indonesia approaches military AI through the perspectives of sovereignty, regional stability, and strategic autonomy. Jakarta recognizes the importance of emerging technologies but remains cautious about the risk of an arms race in Southeast Asia.

Indonesia supports peaceful technological development and regional cooperation through ASEAN frameworks. Jakarta emphasizes that emerging military technologies should not undermine regional stability or erode the sovereign choices of member states.

Indonesia's position is sovereignty-focused and pragmatic. It resists overly restrictive binding treaties that it perceives as infringing on national defence modernization, preferring inclusive dialogue and confidence-building measures over confrontational arms control approaches.

Jakarta nonetheless supports responsible use of AI and meaningful human control over lethal force. As an archipelagic state dependent on secure maritime sea lanes, Indonesia views autonomous surveillance and maritime technologies as relevant to its security, while insisting they be developed responsibly.

As an NPT non-nuclear-weapon state and a CCW State Party, Indonesia participates constructively in the GGE on LAWS and advocates frameworks that balance humanitarian concerns with the right of states to lawful self-defence and peaceful technological development.

## Strategic Interests

- Securing the maritime domain and sea lanes of the Indonesian archipelago.
- Maintaining ASEAN centrality and stability across Southeast Asia.
- Preserving strategic autonomy and sovereign choice in technology adoption.
- Preventing a regional arms race in emerging military technologies.
- Combating terrorism and preventing proliferation to non-state actors.
- Promoting peaceful uses of AI and South–South cooperation.
- Ensuring inclusive, consensus-based disarmament diplomacy that reflects Global South diversity.

## Alliances & Memberships

- ASEAN: the central forum for regional security, confidence-building, and dialogue on emerging technologies.
- Non-Aligned Movement (NAM): Global South alignment on disarmament and technology equity.
- Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC): solidarity with Muslim-majority states.
- Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW): State Party engaged in the GGE on LAWS.
- Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA): maritime cooperation and security dialogue.

## UN Voting Record

Indonesia generally votes in favour of the consensus resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems in the First Committee, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024). Jakarta approaches binding obligations cautiously, preferring political principles, confidence-building measures, and inclusive dialogue.

Through ASEAN, Indonesia advocates frameworks that preserve sovereign choice while addressing humanitarian concerns. Jakarta supports meaningful human control but resists provisions it perceives as infringing on sovereignty or on the lawful modernization of defence capabilities, and it consistently stresses the importance of regional consensus.

## Key Talking Points

- "Indonesia prioritizes sovereignty and regional stability over imported arms-control prescriptions."
- "ASEAN dialogue and confidence-building, not confrontation, are the right path."
- "We support responsible use of AI and meaningful human control of lethal force."

- "Autonomous technologies must not fall into the hands of non-state actors."
- "Jakarta calls for inclusive negotiations that reflect the diversity of the Global South."
- "Peaceful uses of AI and technology transfer should accompany any restrictions."

## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Japan occupies a unique position in the Indo-Pacific due to its advanced technological capabilities, constitutional limits on the military use of force, its alliance with the United States, and growing security concerns in East Asia. Historically cautious about military technology because of its post-Second World War security identity, Tokyo has nonetheless expanded defence modernization in response to challenges involving China and North Korea.

The Japanese Ministry of Defense regards artificial intelligence as an essential component of future defence capabilities, exploring applications in intelligence, surveillance, autonomous vehicles, cybersecurity, information processing, and decision-support systems. Japan views AI as necessary for maintaining operational effectiveness while confronting demographic limitations and an increasingly demanding regional security environment.

Japan generally supports responsible military AI development and emphasizes full compliance with international humanitarian law. Tokyo does not advocate a complete ban on autonomous weapons, but supports appropriate human involvement and accountability mechanisms. It argues that autonomous technologies can provide genuine defensive benefits, including improved missile detection, maritime surveillance, and disaster-response capabilities.

Japan's position is strongly influenced by its alliance with the United States. While Tokyo supports international regulation, it also recognizes the importance of maintaining technological compatibility with allied military systems. Japan therefore favours frameworks that promote responsible innovation rather than restrictive agreements that could create strategic disadvantages relative to competitors such as China and Russia.

As an NPT party and non-nuclear-weapon state with strong disarmament credentials, and as a CCW State Party active in the GGE, Japan seeks to combine its humanitarian credibility with its practical defence needs — backing robust human-control standards while resisting an outright prohibition of autonomous technology.

## Strategic Interests

- Deterrence and defence against ballistic missile, maritime, and other threats from China and North Korea.
- Preserving interoperability and technological compatibility with United States and allied military systems.
- Compensating for demographic decline and manpower constraints through automation and autonomy.
- Developing defensive autonomy for missile defence, maritime surveillance, and disaster response.
- Upholding the rule of law and humanitarian credibility as a disarmament-conscious NNWS.
- Avoiding strategic disadvantage in military AI relative to China and Russia.
- Promoting Indo-Pacific stability and freedom of navigation.

## Alliances & Memberships

- United States–Japan Security Treaty: cornerstone alliance; requires interoperability of allied AI and autonomous systems.
- Quad (Quadrilateral Security Dialogue): cooperation with the United States, India, and Australia on secure technology and maritime order.
- G7: coordination among advanced economies on AI governance and responsible military AI principles.
- ASEAN Dialogue Partner: engagement on regional stability and maritime security.
- Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW): State Party and constructive participant in the GGE on LAWS.

## UN Voting Record

Japan has voted in favour of the consensus resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems in the First Committee, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024). Tokyo is broadly supportive of negotiating a legally binding instrument that codifies meaningful human control and accountability, provided it preserves lawful defensive applications and allied technological cooperation.

In the CCW GGE, Japan is a pragmatic voice that endorses political principles and international initiatives such as the Responsible AI in the Military Domain (REAIM) process, seeking convergence on human control while avoiding measures it believes would leave Japan and its allies at a strategic disadvantage.

## Key Talking Points

- "As the only nation to have suffered nuclear attack, Japan champions the rule of law and human dignity in conflict."
- "Autonomy can serve defensive and humanitarian purposes, such as missile defence and disaster response."
- "Machines must never decide the use of lethal force without meaningful human control."
- "A legally binding instrument should codify human control and accountability while preserving lawful defensive capabilities."
- "Japan's defence modernization remains purely defensive and fully compliant with international humanitarian law."
- "Practical restrictions must not sacrifice technological compatibility with allied systems."
- "Transparency and confidence-building are essential to prevent an AI arms race."
- "Autonomous systems help compensate for the demographic constraints facing Japan's Self-Defense Forces."

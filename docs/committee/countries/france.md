## LAWS & Military AI Stance

France is one of the most influential European voices in the LAWS and military AI debate. As a permanent member of the Security Council, a nuclear-armed state, and one of Europe's largest defence powers, French policy seeks to balance two competing objectives: preserving strategic autonomy and technological competitiveness while ensuring that AI-enabled military systems remain consistent with international humanitarian law and ethical principles.

France supports a regulatory approach centred on maintaining meaningful human involvement in decisions concerning the use of lethal force. Paris has repeatedly stated that human responsibility must remain central in military operations and that autonomous systems should not independently make decisions on the intentional targeting and destruction of human beings. This reflects a key French distinction between autonomous functions and autonomous lethal decision-making: France accepts autonomy for navigation, logistics, surveillance, and defensive systems, but rejects machines independently determining when and whom to kill.

France argues that military AI development is unavoidable and that international security is better served through responsible governance than absolute prohibition. Artificial intelligence is treated as a strategic technology essential for military effectiveness, with French defence modernization covering intelligence analysis, autonomous vehicles, cyber operations, battlefield management, satellite systems, and information warfare. The 2021 report of the Ministry of Armed Forces on AI and the national AI investment strategy underpin this programme.

French policy is deeply shaped by European strategic culture, which emphasizes international law, multilateral institutions, and humanitarian considerations. France has actively supported CCW discussions on LAWS and participated in developing international principles for responsible military AI, but it has not supported proposals for an immediate comprehensive ban, considering many autonomous technologies to have legitimate defensive and operational applications. Together with Germany, France tabled a working paper at the GGE on human-machine interaction, emphasizing that humans must remain in control of engagement decisions.

France also places high priority on technological sovereignty. As U.S.–China competition intensifies, European states increasingly seek independent capabilities in artificial intelligence, semiconductors, cybersecurity, and defence technologies. Paris views military AI development as necessary to prevent excessive dependence on external technology suppliers and to preserve European strategic autonomy. France therefore supports international cooperation, but seeks frameworks that regulate irresponsible use rather than restrict technological innovation entirely.

## Strategic Interests

- Preserve European strategic autonomy in AI, semiconductors, and defence technology.
- Reduce dependence on U.S. and Chinese technology suppliers while maintaining NATO interoperability.
- Maintain a leading European voice in shaping international norms for military AI.
- Ensure regulation targets irresponsible use without restricting legitimate defensive innovation.
- Protect the independent French nuclear deterrent and its command-and-control modernization.
- Strengthen the Franco-German axis and EU coordination on AI governance.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **European Union / PESCO** — leads European efforts to develop trustworthy AI principles and coordinated defence-technology programmes.
- **NATO** — member committed to the 2021 AI Strategy and allied interoperability, while retaining independent capabilities.
- **Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI and Autonomy** — endorsed, supporting the responsible-use approach.
- **CCW GGE on LAWS** — co-author of the human-machine interaction working paper with Germany; active promoter of meaningful human involvement.

## UN Voting Record

France has voted in favour of and joined consensus on the annual UN General Assembly resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024), supporting the continuation of the GGE process. In the GGE, France has been among the most constructive major powers, co-authoring the human-machine interaction working paper with Germany and arguing for robust international rules on meaningful human control while resisting a comprehensive ban. Paris is open to negotiating legally binding norms on specific high-risk systems if they preserve legitimate autonomy for defensive and operational functions. France has endorsed the U.S. Political Declaration on Responsible Military Use of AI and Autonomy and works to ensure European positions remain influential in both the GGE and the wider UNGA debate.

## Key Talking Points

- Meaningful human involvement must be the organizing principle for governing autonomous weapons.
- Autonomy for functions is legitimate; autonomy for lethal decision-making is not.
- International humanitarian law must be operationalized for autonomous systems, not replaced by a blanket ban.
- A comprehensive prohibition would destroy defensive and dual-use innovation with genuine humanitarian value.
- Europe must build strategic autonomy in AI to avoid dependence on external technology powers.
- Human-machine interaction standards, testing, and transparency should anchor any future instrument.
- Accountability must remain with the military chain of command under international law.
- The GGE must remain inclusive so that a fragmented, region-by-region norm-making process is avoided.

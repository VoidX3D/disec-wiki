## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Bangladesh represents a smaller South Asian state whose position differs from the major military powers due to its limited defence resources and strategic priorities focused primarily on development, peacekeeping, and regional stability. Dhaka does not pursue significant autonomous weapons capabilities of its own.

Bangladesh has traditionally emphasized peaceful diplomacy, United Nations peacekeeping, and responsible international security governance. As one of the world's largest troop contributors to UN peacekeeping operations, Bangladesh carries significant moral authority on issues of civilian protection and the conduct of armed forces.

While it does not possess meaningful autonomous weapons capabilities, Bangladesh recognizes the importance of preventing uncontrolled proliferation of emerging military technologies. Its approach generally aligns with humanitarian principles and supports international cooperation in governing LAWS and military AI.

Dhaka supports transparency, international regulation, and humanitarian approaches toward autonomous weapons governance. Its peacekeeping experience makes it a natural proponent of meaningful human control, accountability, and strict compliance with international humanitarian law.

As an NPT non-nuclear-weapon state and a CCW State Party, Bangladesh is a credible and consistent participant in disarmament forums, advocating a framework that protects civilians while ensuring that developing states are not excluded from peaceful and defensive applications of technology.

## Strategic Interests

- Protecting civilians and upholding international humanitarian law, informed by decades of peacekeeping experience.
- Preventing the proliferation of autonomous weapons to non-state actors and conflict zones.
- Ensuring developing states' access to technology, capacity building, and training.
- Supporting stability in South Asia and the Bay of Bengal region.
- Strengthening the UN multilateral disarmament machinery and the CCW process.
- Avoiding an AI arms race that diverts resources from development and peacebuilding.
- Sustaining Bangladesh's reputation as a Global South humanitarian voice.

## Alliances & Memberships

- Non-Aligned Movement (NAM): Global South alignment on humanitarian and disarmament issues.
- Organisation of Islamic Cooperation (OIC): solidarity with Muslim-majority states on security questions.
- Commonwealth: multilateral cooperation and diplomatic coordination.
- D-8 (Developing-8): South–South cooperation on development and technology.
- Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW): State Party engaged in the GGE on LAWS.
- UN peacekeeping: one of the largest troop-contributing countries worldwide.

## UN Voting Record

Bangladesh has voted in favour of the consensus resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems in the First Committee, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024). Dhaka supports the push toward a legally binding instrument that enshrines meaningful human control and accountability.

Bangladesh's humanitarian orientation and peacekeeping record align it with states urging stronger restrictions, although it also insists that any framework be accompanied by technology access and capacity building so that developing states are not disadvantaged by new rules.

## Key Talking Points

- "As one of the world's largest troop contributors, Bangladesh knows the human cost of conflict."
- "Autonomous weapons that cannot distinguish combatants from civilians violate the principles we defend in peacekeeping."
- "We support a legally binding instrument anchored in meaningful human control."
- "Proliferation of autonomous weapons to non-state actors must be prevented."
- "Regulation must be accompanied by technology access and capacity building for developing states."
- "Transparency and accountability are non-negotiable in the use of lethal force."
- "Emerging military technologies must not drain resources from development and peace."
- "Bangladesh stands for dialogue, international law, and the protection of civilians."

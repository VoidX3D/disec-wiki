## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Türkiye is one of the world's leading powers in unmanned and autonomous systems, driven by a vigorous domestic defence industry. The Bayraktar TB2 combat drone, produced by Baykar, has been combat-proven in Ukraine, Libya, and the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict, and Türkiye's product line now extends to the Akıncı and the jet-powered Kızılelma unmanned combat aircraft. Ankara has an active national AI defence agenda, investing in autonomous platforms, AI-assisted targeting, intelligence surveillance and reconnaissance, and command-and-control systems.

Türkiye's regulatory posture is pragmatic and innovation-oriented. Ankara supports the responsible use of autonomous systems, meaningful human control over lethal decision-making, and compliance with international humanitarian law, but it firmly opposes blanket prohibitions that would restrict legitimate national development and defence exports. As a significant exporter of armed drones, Türkiye has a strong interest in ensuring that international rules do not disadvantage its defence industry or its partner states.

Ankara emphasizes technological sovereignty and the reduction of dependence on foreign arms suppliers. Its defence-industrial strategy treats AI and autonomy as critical for national security, regional deterrence, and export competitiveness. Türkiye argues that middle powers and developing states must have a voice in shaping international AI norms and that governance frameworks must respect each state's right to develop its own capabilities.

In the CCW Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on LAWS, Türkiye participates constructively, supporting continued dialogue, transparency, and confidence-building measures. It has not been a leading advocate of a legally binding treaty, preferring practical measures and responsible-use principles that preserve room for legitimate development and export. Ankara aligns broadly with NATO's approach of responsible innovation rather than prohibition.

Türkiye views autonomous and AI-enabled systems as essential force multipliers that protect military personnel, improve operational efficiency, and enable asymmetric capabilities. It also recognizes the need for safeguards, including human oversight of lethal targeting and measures to prevent the proliferation of autonomous weapons to non-state actors, given the low cost and commercial availability of drone and AI technologies.

## Strategic Interests

- Protect and expand the domestic drone and AI defence-industrial base.
- Preserve the ability to export armed drones and AI-enabled systems to partner states.
- Maintain NATO interoperability while pursuing increasingly independent military capabilities.
- Use autonomous systems for counterterrorism and regional deterrence in the Aegean, Syria, and broader neighbourhood.
- Position Türkiye as a technological leader among middle powers and in the wider non-Western world.
- Ensure international rules respect national sovereignty and the right to develop defence technology.
- Prevent proliferation of autonomous weapons to terrorist and non-state actors.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **NATO** — second-largest allied military; contributor to the 2021 AI Strategy and interoperability framework.
- **European Union candidate** — engaged with EU AI governance discussions while defence remains nationally controlled.
- **Defence export partnerships** — arms and drone supply relationships with Ukraine, Azerbaijan, and other partner states.
- **CCW GGE on LAWS** — participant supporting responsible-use principles and human oversight.

## UN Voting Record

Türkiye has voted in favour of and joined consensus on the annual UN General Assembly resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024), supporting the continuation of the GGE process. In the GGE, Ankara adopts a constructive, practical approach, supporting meaningful human control, transparency, and confidence-building while opposing moves toward a comprehensive prohibition that would restrict legitimate development and exports. Türkiye is generally not among the states pressing for a legally binding instrument, and it aligns with NATO's responsible-use philosophy.

## Key Talking Points

- Autonomous systems reduce risks to pilots and ground personnel and improve precision in military operations.
- The combat record of the Bayraktar TB2 demonstrates that autonomous and AI-assisted systems can be used responsibly.
- Regulation should focus on misuse and on systems that cannot comply with international humanitarian law, not on legitimate national development.
- National sovereignty and technological independence are non-negotiable for all states, especially middle powers.
- Türkiye supports meaningful human control over lethal decision-making.
- The international community must prevent autonomous weapons from proliferating to terrorist and non-state actors.
- International testing, export, and transparency standards should support responsible innovation.
- Middle powers and developing states must have a seat at the table in shaping global AI governance.

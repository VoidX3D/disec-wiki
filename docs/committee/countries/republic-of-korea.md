## LAWS & Military AI Stance

The Republic of Korea is among the most technologically advanced military powers in Asia and has invested heavily in AI-enabled defence systems because of its unique security environment involving North Korea. The constant military tension on the Korean Peninsula has driven Seoul to develop autonomous surveillance systems, unmanned vehicles, robotics, and AI-supported command capabilities.

Korea's defence industry has produced advanced autonomous technologies, including unmanned ground vehicles, surveillance drones, robotic systems, and AI-assisted battlefield management tools. Seoul considers military AI essential for improving operational efficiency and for compensating for demographic challenges that are shrinking the pool of military recruits.

At the same time, the Republic of Korea has recognized the importance of responsible governance. Seoul supports international discussions on autonomous weapons and emphasizes that military AI must comply fully with international humanitarian law. Like other technologically advanced states, it does not support a complete prohibition of autonomous systems because it considers AI essential to national defence against the North Korean threat.

Korea has sought to shape the global conversation rather than merely react to it. Seoul co-hosted the inaugural Responsible AI in the Military Domain (REAIM) summit in The Hague with the Netherlands in 2023 and hosted the second REAIM summit in Seoul in 2024, promoting principles of responsible use, reliability, and human oversight.

The South Korean position illustrates the dilemma of technologically advanced states: while genuinely concerned with humanitarian norms, existential security threats create powerful incentives to continue developing military AI and autonomous capabilities.

## Strategic Interests

- Deterrence and defence against North Korean conventional, nuclear, and missile threats.
- Preserving a technological edge in autonomy, robotics, and AI-enabled command systems.
- Compensating for demographic decline and falling recruitment through unmanned and autonomous systems.
- Maintaining interoperability with United States and allied forces.
- Sustaining a competitive defence export industry in unmanned and AI technologies.
- Leading international norm-building on responsible military AI through the REAIM process.
- Contributing to stability and the rules-based order in Northeast Asia.

## Alliances & Memberships

- United States–Republic of Korea Mutual Defense Treaty: core alliance underpinning joint command and technology interoperability.
- REAIM (Responsible AI in the Military Domain): co-initiator with the Netherlands of the premier global forum on responsible military AI.
- Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW): State Party and active participant in the GGE on LAWS.
- UN peacekeeping and disarmament machinery: engaged across First Committee processes.
- Indo-Pacific and Northeast Asian security partnerships: cooperative frameworks with allied and like-minded states.

## UN Voting Record

The Republic of Korea has voted in favour of the consensus resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems in the First Committee, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024). Seoul supports international standards that uphold meaningful human control and accountability, but it consistently opposes a total ban on autonomous systems that it regards as vital to national defence.

In the CCW GGE and through REAIM, Korea promotes responsible-use principles — reliability, testing, legal review, and human oversight — while working to ensure that regulation does not disadvantage states facing grave security threats or undermine allied interoperability.

## Key Talking Points

- "Korea's investment in autonomous defence is a direct response to an existential threat on the Peninsula."
- "Autonomy strengthens deterrence; irresponsible regulation could undermine it."
- "We support meaningful human control and strict compliance with international humanitarian law."
- "A complete ban on autonomy would deny states essential defensive capabilities."
- "Korea leads global dialogue through REAIM; we invite all states to join responsible-use principles."
- "Autonomous systems help offset the manpower constraints facing modern armed forces."
- "Verification, testing, and reliability standards must accompany any framework."
- "North Korea's nuclear and missile programmes must remain central to any security framework in our region."

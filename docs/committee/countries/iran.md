## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Iran's position on lethal autonomous weapons and military AI is shaped by regional competition, strategic deterrence, and the pursuit of asymmetric military capabilities under conditions of severe economic sanctions and technological isolation. Despite these constraints, Tehran has invested significantly in unmanned aerial vehicles, missile technology, cyber capabilities, and indigenous defence innovation, and it has developed a substantial drone industry combining autonomous navigation, surveillance, and precision-strike functions. These capabilities have made Iran both a developer of and a participant in the regional proliferation of inexpensive autonomous systems.

Iran supports international discussion of autonomous weapons regulation but is deeply cautious about restrictions that could limit its technological development or reinforce the military advantage of advanced powers. Tehran repeatedly frames the debate in terms of sovereignty, equality among states, and opposition to technological monopolisation by the United States and other technologically dominant states. From Iran's perspective, strict limitations imposed by major powers could entrench existing inequalities in military capability and deny developing states — including those under sanctions — access to defence technologies they need for self-defence.

Iran's diplomacy on LAWS therefore emphasises regulation that prevents misuse of autonomous weapons while resisting frameworks that restrict access to advanced defence technologies. It is a strong voice for the Global South and the Non-Aligned Movement, arguing that governance of military AI must not become a vehicle for great-power domination, and that developing states must have an equal say in setting the rules.

Iran's position is complicated by the nature of its own forces: it relies heavily on drones, missiles, and proxy networks, and it perceives autonomous and AI-enabled systems as necessary for offsetting the conventional and technological superiority of adversaries such as Israel and the United States. Tehran is therefore unlikely to support a comprehensive ban on autonomous weapons, which it would see as disarming precisely the states that need asymmetric capabilities for deterrence.

At the same time, Iran participates in the CCW GGE and UN debates, and it has supported the general direction of multilateral LAWS discussions, including principles of human responsibility and accountability. Its posture is that of a state willing to engage on governance, but determined that the outcome must protect sovereign equality and equitable access to technology for the developing world.

## Strategic Interests

- Preserving access to drone, missile, and cyber technology that underpins Iranian deterrence.
- Preventing the United States and other powers from monopolising military AI governance and technology.
- Defending sovereignty and equality among states in the LAWS negotiations.
- Preventing proliferation of autonomous weapons into non-state actor conflicts across the region.
- Using its 2025–2026 Security Council term to advance Global South and NAM positions.
- Avoiding restrictions that would reinforce sanctions-based technological isolation.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM):** Principal bloc for advancing Global South positions on disarmament and technology.
- **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (observer):** Coordination with China, Russia, and Central Asian states on technology and security governance.
- **Economic Cooperation Organisation / D-8:** Regional and Islamic-world solidarity on equitable development and technology access.
- **CCW GGE:** Participant supporting regulation of misuse while resisting restrictive frameworks.
- **UN Security Council (2025–2026):** Platform for amplifying developing-state perspectives on LAWS.

## UN Voting Record

Iran has consistently voted in favour of UNGA disarmament and arms-control resolutions, including the CCW framework, and it voted in favour of A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024) on lethal autonomous weapons systems, which were adopted by overwhelming majorities. Tehran supports multilateral approaches to LAWS governance and the consultative process initiated by the General Assembly, but it has not supported proposals perceived as favouring technologically dominant states at the expense of developing countries. In the GGE, Iran backs consensus principles on human responsibility and IHL while resisting language or mechanisms that would restrict developing-state access to defence technologies.

## Key Talking Points

- The LAWS debate must not become a tool for the United States and other powers to monopolise military AI.
- Iran supports regulations that prevent misuse of autonomous weapons but rejects frameworks that deny developing states technology they need.
- Sovereignty and equality among states must be the foundation of any governance regime.
- Strict bans imposed by advanced powers would entrench existing inequalities in military capability.
- Autonomous systems used by states under sanctions are matters of legitimate self-defence.
- Proliferation of cheap autonomous systems into regional conflicts threatens all states in the Middle East.
- As a 2025–2026 Security Council member, Iran will champion Global South and NAM positions.
- Meaningful human control and accountability are principles Iran supports — applied equally to all states.

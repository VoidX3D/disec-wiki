## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Italy represents an important European defence actor with a pragmatic approach toward military AI. As a NATO member with significant aerospace and defence industries, Rome recognizes artificial intelligence as a critical component of future military capabilities. Italian defence policy supports technological innovation in autonomous systems, surveillance, cyber operations, and intelligence processing, viewing AI as a dual-use technology that can enhance the effectiveness and safety of military operations.

Italy's position generally aligns with NATO's broader approach, which emphasizes responsible use rather than prohibition. Rome considers autonomous and semi-autonomous systems valuable for improving operational efficiency, protecting military personnel, and strengthening situational awareness on the battlefield. At the same time, Italy supports international discussions aimed at ensuring responsible development and maintaining human accountability over the use of force.

Rome recognizes the importance of legal compliance and has supported continued international dialogue through the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW). Italy has engaged constructively in the Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on LAWS, favouring a pragmatic, incremental approach that develops clearer definitions, agreed principles, and practical standards rather than an immediate comprehensive ban. Italy's position reflects the view that autonomous technologies have legitimate defensive and operational applications that a blanket prohibition would unnecessarily forgo.

Within the European Union, Italy backs the "trustworthy AI" framework and its emphasis on human oversight, transparency, and accountability. Rome advocates extending these principles to defence applications while ensuring that regulation remains proportionate and does not undermine the competitiveness of European industry or the operational readiness of allied militaries. Italian industry — including major players in aerospace, missiles, and naval systems — is an active participant in European and transatlantic defence programmes, giving Italy a direct stake in the shape of any future regulatory regime.

## Strategic Interests

- Preserving the competitiveness of Italy's defence and aerospace industry (aerospace, missiles, and naval systems) in AI-enabled weapons markets.
- Maintaining NATO interoperability and technological edge, particularly in the Mediterranean and southern flank.
- Supporting international law and human accountability in military AI without accepting restrictions that create strategic disadvantages.
- Ensuring European strategic autonomy in emerging defence technologies alongside transatlantic cooperation.
- Countering hybrid, cyber, and drone threats in the Mediterranean basin and North Africa.
- Positioning Italy as a bridge between European, NATO, and broader international positions on AI governance.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **NATO** — Aligns with the alliance's 2021 Artificial Intelligence Strategy, supporting responsible development, interoperability, and human accountability rather than prohibition.
- **European Union** — Applies the EU's "trustworthy AI" and human-oversight principles; coordinates First Committee positions within the EU bloc.
- **CCW / GGE on LAWS** — Engages constructively in Geneva to develop definitions, principles, and practical measures.
- **OSCE** — Participates in European confidence-building on emerging military technologies.
- **G7** — Contributes to global discussions on AI governance, including the Hiroshima AI Process.

## UN Voting Record

Italy has voted in favour of the annual General Assembly resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024), reflecting its support for sustained international discussion and eventual regulation. In the First Committee, Italy generally aligns with EU partners, supporting language on human control, compliance with international humanitarian law, and the continued work of the GGE. Rome's voting posture signals openness to strengthened norms while guarding the operational and industrial flexibility it considers essential for NATO and European defence.

## Key Talking Points

- Military AI offers real benefits for situational awareness, force protection, and operational efficiency and should not be dismissed outright.
- Regulation should focus on responsible use, human accountability, and compliance with international humanitarian law rather than a blanket ban.
- The GGE should agree clear definitions and practical standards before states rush to negotiated instruments.
- European industry must remain competitive and interoperable with allied systems under any future regulatory framework.
- Mediterranean and hybrid threats demonstrate the defensive value of AI-enabled surveillance and unmanned systems.
- Human oversight must be preserved over decisions that involve the use of lethal force.
- International dialogue should be inclusive, bridging European, NATO, and Global South perspectives.

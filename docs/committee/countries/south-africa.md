## LAWS & Military AI Stance

South Africa occupies a distinctive place in global disarmament diplomacy as the only state ever to develop and then voluntarily dismantle its own nuclear weapons programme, joining the NPT in 1991 and later acceding to the Chemical and Biological Weapons Conventions. This experience gives Pretoria exceptional moral authority and explains why it is one of the most consistent African voices pressing for strong international regulation of lethal autonomous weapons.

South Africa supports a legally binding instrument on LAWS and has been a leading advocate within the CCW GGE for prohibiting weapons systems that operate without meaningful human control. Pretoria argues that autonomous weapons raise profound ethical and humanitarian concerns, that the delegation of life-and-death decisions to machines is fundamentally incompatible with human dignity, and that international law must evolve to address these emerging technologies rather than waiting for the law to catch up after the fact.

South Africa frames the LAWS issue within a broader Global South agenda of accountability, transparency, and prevention of technological domination. It warns that military AI risks creating a new technological hierarchy in which advanced states and private defence companies control the tools of warfare, while weaker states become more vulnerable. Pretoria insists that governance must be inclusive, equitable, and based on the rule of law — themes consistent with its role as a leader of African and non-aligned diplomacy.

As an emerging power with a growing defence-industrial base, including significant unmanned and aerospace capability, South Africa balances its humanitarian stance with recognition that technology has legitimate uses. It argues for regulation that prevents irresponsible and destabilising autonomous warfare while preserving space for responsible defence development, and it has sought to ensure that African states are represented in global AI governance discussions.

South Africa frequently represents broader African concerns in UN fora, coordinating positions within the African Group and the African Union, which has emphasised responsible technology governance and the link between emerging technology and development. Pretoria is thus both a leading humanitarian voice and a bridge to the wider African consensus on preventing new forms of technological inequality.

## Strategic Interests

- Securing a legally binding instrument prohibiting autonomous weapons without meaningful human control.
- Leveraging its disarmament credentials to lead African and Global South positions on LAWS.
- Preventing a technological divide that increases the vulnerability of African and developing states.
- Ensuring accountability and transparency in the development and use of military AI.
- Preventing proliferation of autonomous systems to non-state actors and destabilising forces on the continent.
- Supporting the African Union's framework for responsible technology governance.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **African Union:** Leading African positions on disarmament, peace, and technology governance.
- **BRICS:** Coordination with emerging powers on equitable, non-hegemonic AI governance.
- **SADC / Non-Aligned Movement / G77+China:** Alignment with Global South solidarity on disarmament.
- **CCW GGE:** Active participant in the coalition pressing for a binding instrument.
- **NPT Review Conferences:** Established voice for disarmament accountability, extending credibility to its LAWS position.

## UN Voting Record

South Africa voted in favour of A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024) on lethal autonomous weapons systems and is a vocal supporter of the Secretary-General's consultative process and meetings of States. In the GGE, Pretoria has argued that consensus principles are a floor, not a ceiling, and that the GGE should progress toward negotiating a legally binding outcome. South Africa consistently supports humanitarian-coalition language on meaningful human control and accountability, and it frequently speaks on behalf of the African Group in First Committee debates.

## Key Talking Points

- South Africa gave up its nuclear weapons voluntarily — no state should need such weapons, and no machine should decide to kill.
- Autonomous weapons operating without meaningful human control must be prohibited by a binding instrument.
- International humanitarian law applies, but the law must evolve to close the gaps created by autonomous systems.
- Military AI must not create a new technological hierarchy that makes developing states more vulnerable.
- Accountability and transparency must be built into the development and deployment of every weapon system.
- Proliferation of autonomous systems to non-state actors is a direct threat to African security.
- The African Union and the Global South must have a genuine seat at the governance table.
- South Africa urges the GGE to move from principles to negotiation without delay.

## LAWS & Military AI Stance

The United Arab Emirates is among the most technologically ambitious states in the Middle East and one of the clearest examples of a Global South power seeking to become a producer rather than a consumer of military AI. Abu Dhabi appointed the world's first Minister of State for Artificial Intelligence in 2017, launched a national AI strategy, established the Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence, and built an ecosystem of AI, robotics, and autonomous-systems companies — including EDGE, the state-owned defence conglomerate that develops unmanned platforms, autonomous vehicles, and surveillance systems.

The UAE's position in the LAWS debate reflects the interests of an emerging technological power: it supports international cooperation and responsible-use governance but resists restrictive frameworks that would limit domestic innovation and technological development. Abu Dhabi participates actively in the CCW GGE and in UN-level discussions, consistently endorsing principles of human responsibility, compliance with international humanitarian law, accountability, and transparency, while steering clear of calls for a comprehensive ban on autonomous systems.

The UAE has positioned itself as a bridge between the humanitarian agenda and the responsible-use camp. As a non-nuclear state party to the NPT and a CCW party, it speaks credibly on disarmament matters; as an active developer and exporter of autonomous technology, it insists that governance recognise the legitimate defensive and civilian applications of AI. Abu Dhabi has also been a strong advocate for equitable access to AI for developing states, arguing that capacity-building and technology transfer should be part of the global AI governance agenda.

The UAE's security posture — including participation in regional coalition operations and protection of critical Gulf infrastructure — has driven investment in drones, cyber defence, and autonomous air and missile defence, and Abu Dhabi argues that these systems reduce risks to personnel and enhance precision. It has also deepened defence-technology cooperation with the United States, Israel (under the Abraham Accords), and other partners, giving it a stake in permissive, standards-based governance rather than binding prohibitions.

Abu Dhabi's diplomatic ambition extends to AI governance globally: it has engaged in UN AI governance processes, hosted major AI summits, and promoted voluntary frameworks and confidence-building measures. In the LAWS context, the UAE therefore champions a governance architecture built on shared principles, transparency, reliability, and human oversight — one that keeps the door open for responsible innovation.

## Strategic Interests

- Preserving room for the UAE's national AI strategy and defence-industrial ambitions in any global regulatory framework.
- Strengthening its profile as a leading Global South voice on AI governance and capacity-building.
- Defending the legitimate use of autonomous systems for missile defence and critical-infrastructure protection.
- Maintaining permissive, standards-based governance rather than binding prohibitions.
- Deepening defence-technology partnerships with the US, Israel, and European and Asian partners.
- Ensuring developing states receive equitable access to AI technologies and know-how.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC):** Coordinated Gulf positions on technology governance and disarmament.
- **CCW GGE:** Active participant in the responsible-use coalition within the LAWS negotiations.
- **Abraham Accords:** Defence-technology and AI cooperation with Israel and regional normalisation partners.
- **United States defence partnership:** Major arms and AI-technology relationships shaping its military modernisation.
- **Global AI summits and UN AI governance processes:** Leadership in civilian AI governance that supports its responsible-use framing.

## UN Voting Record

The UAE voted in favour of A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024) on lethal autonomous weapons systems and supported the General Assembly's consultative process and meetings of States. In the GGE, Abu Dhabi backs consensus principles on human responsibility and IHL while resisting a legally binding ban, preferring voluntary political commitments and the responsible-use approach exemplified by the Political Declaration on Responsible Military AI. The UAE frequently positions itself as a consensus-builder that urges states to focus on shared principles rather than adversarial treaty negotiations.

## Key Talking Points

- The UAE is a global leader in AI — governance should enable responsible innovation, not block emerging powers.
- Autonomous defence systems protect critical infrastructure and reduce risks to military personnel.
- Human oversight and accountability are essential principles that the UAE fully endorses.
- A comprehensive ban would freeze the technological hierarchy and deny developing states a voice.
- Capacity-building and equitable access to AI must be part of the governance agenda.
- The CCW GGE should build on consensus principles rather than deadlock over a treaty.
- The UAE supports transparency, reliability testing, and confidence-building measures.
- As a UNSC member (2022–2023) and active mediator, the UAE urges pragmatic, bridge-building outcomes.

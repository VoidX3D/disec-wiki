## LAWS & Military AI Stance

The Democratic People's Republic of Korea's position on military AI and autonomous weapons is less transparent than most, given the limited public information about its defence technology programmes. Available evidence nevertheless indicates growing interest in unmanned systems, cyber capabilities, artificial intelligence, and advanced military technologies.

The DPRK has demonstrated significant cyber capabilities and has invested heavily in asymmetric military technologies. While its conventional military technology may lag behind major powers, autonomous systems and cyber warfare offer opportunities to challenge technologically superior opponents at relatively lower cost.

The country's approach is likely shaped by strategic deterrence against the United States and the Republic of Korea. Autonomous weapons, cyber capabilities, and AI-enabled systems could enhance the DPRK's ability to conduct surveillance, disrupt military networks, and improve battlefield effectiveness against more advanced adversaries.

North Korea has historically been cautious toward international arms control agreements that could restrict its strategic capabilities. It is therefore unlikely to support regulations perceived as limiting technological development, particularly any framework negotiated without addressing its own security grievances.

As a nuclear-armed state outside the NPT and not a party to the CCW, the DPRK engages with disarmament forums selectively. Pyongyang frames its position in terms of self-defence and opposition to what it calls double standards in international disarmament, rejecting regimes it considers tools of US policy.

## Strategic Interests

- Regime survival and the preservation of strategic deterrence against the United States and the Republic of Korea.
- Maintaining asymmetric advantage through cyber, autonomous, and AI-enabled capabilities.
- Avoiding commitments that constrain technological development or missile and nuclear programmes.
- Countering perceived US–ROK technological superiority and alliance build-ups.
- Leveraging security dialogue for sanctions relief and international recognition.
- Framing disarmament demands as reciprocal obligations on the United States and its allies.
- Resisting any regulatory framework that enshrines major-power advantage over developing states.

## Alliances & Memberships

- China–DPRK friendship treaty: principal strategic relationship and traditional source of diplomatic support.
- Russia: deepening military and strategic cooperation amid evolving alignments.
- Non-Aligned legacy: rhetorical alignment with anti-imperialist and Global South frames in UN debates.
- UN General Assembly: the primary channel through which Pyongyang engages disarmament issues, as a non-party to the CCW.

## UN Voting Record

The DPRK does not participate meaningfully in the CCW GGE on LAWS, as it is not a State Party to the CCW. In the First Committee, it generally abstains on or rejects disarmament resolutions it regards as politically motivated, and it has historically opposed resolutions targeting its own nuclear and missile programmes.

On LAWS specifically, Pyongyang would resist any instrument that could constrain its autonomous or cyber capabilities. It nonetheless uses its statements to advance a broader critique — that disarmament frameworks are applied selectively and that genuine disarmament must begin with the elimination of hostile policies and alliance threats against the DPRK.

## Key Talking Points

- "The DPRK's right to self-defence is non-negotiable and cannot be constrained by discriminatory regimes."
- "Double standards in disarmament undermine international confidence and stability."
- "Autonomous and cyber technologies reflect the realities of modern warfare; states must not be denied modern means of self-defence."
- "Regulations are meaningless unless they address the root causes of insecurity, including hostile policies toward our state."
- "Weapons regulation must apply equally to all states and not serve the interests of a few powerful nations."
- "The United States and its allies bear responsibility for regional instability through military exercises and alliance build-ups."
- "Any framework that ignores the DPRK's legitimate security concerns is illegitimate."
- "We support general disarmament, but only on a just and equal footing for all states."

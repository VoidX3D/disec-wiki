## LAWS & Military AI Stance

Ukraine has become the world's most prominent battlefield experiment in AI-enabled warfare. Since the full-scale Russian invasion in 2022, Ukrainian forces have integrated artificial intelligence into nearly every layer of military operations, including AI-assisted targeting, satellite imagery analysis, intelligence processing, battlefield coordination, and autonomous drone navigation. Ukraine has fielded extensive drone fleets for reconnaissance, artillery correction, and strike, and has conducted trials of autonomous drone swarms and MLAT (matrix) autonomous systems in which drones operate with reduced human intervention in the terminal phase of engagement.

Ukraine's position on regulation is shaped by this experience. Kyiv supports the principle of meaningful human control over lethal decision-making and recognizes the humanitarian concerns raised by fully autonomous weapons, including the risks of misidentification, accidental escalation, and accountability gaps. At the same time, Ukraine insists that any regulatory framework must preserve the right of states to develop and use defensive AI and autonomous systems in self-defence. Ukraine argues that responsible use of AI-assisted precision can actually reduce civilian harm by improving targeting accuracy and force protection.

Ukraine is a leading voice for accountability in the application of international law to emerging military technology. It draws attention to Russian use of drones, electronic warfare, and automated systems in the conflict and argues that rules governing AI in warfare must include strong accountability mechanisms. Kyiv also warns about the risks of proliferation of autonomous capabilities to irresponsible state and non-state actors, given the low cost and commercial availability of drone and AI technologies.

In the CCW Group of Governmental Experts (GGE) on LAWS and in UN forums, Ukraine supports continued international dialogue and the development of concrete norms, transparency, testing, and verification measures. It advocates for standards that target systems incapable of complying with international humanitarian law, rather than a blanket prohibition that would deny states access to legitimate defensive technology. Ukraine's position reflects a balance between humanitarian principles and the urgent practical need to defend against an adversary employing AI-enabled systems.

Ukraine also uses its position as a frontline state to call for international cooperation and technology-sharing with democratic partners, including the global drone coalition and digital infrastructure support. It argues that democracies must not be disadvantaged in military AI, and that the international community has a collective interest in preventing an uncontrolled AI arms race.

## Strategic Interests

- Maintain defensive technological advantage against Russia through AI, drones, and autonomous systems.
- Preserve access to allied AI and drone technology through international cooperation and technology-sharing.
- Ensure any international regulation does not handicap states defending themselves against aggression.
- Establish accountability mechanisms for the use of autonomous and AI-enabled systems in conflict.
- Prevent proliferation of autonomous weapons to non-state actors and irresponsible regimes.
- Rebuild and modernize the Ukrainian defence-industrial base, including AI and unmanned systems.
- Use the 2025–2026 UN Security Council term to keep military AI governance and Russian conduct on the agenda.

## Alliances & Memberships

- **European Union candidate** — aligned with EU "trustworthy AI" principles and regulatory approaches.
- **NATO Enhanced Opportunity Partner** — deep interoperability with NATO and support for the 2021 AI Strategy principles.
- **Global drone coalition and allied defence cooperation** — practical technology-sharing for drone and AI capabilities.
- **GUAM** — regional grouping with Georgia, Azerbaijan, and Moldova on shared security concerns.
- **CCW GGE on LAWS** — active participant pressing for human-control safeguards and accountability.

## UN Voting Record

Ukraine has voted in favour of and joined consensus on the annual UN General Assembly resolutions on lethal autonomous weapons systems, including A/RES/78/241 (2023) and A/RES/79/62 (2024), and it supports the continuation of the CCW Group of Governmental Experts. In the GGE, Ukraine supports meaningful human control, transparency, and testing requirements, and it backs legally binding norms that target systems that cannot comply with international humanitarian law while preserving the right to use AI and autonomy defensively. During its 2025–2026 Security Council term, Ukraine is well placed to press for accountability, to highlight battlefield lessons from the war, and to advocate for frameworks that prevent uncontrolled autonomous warfare.

## Key Talking Points

- Ukraine is the living laboratory of AI-enabled warfare; the world must learn the lessons of the battlefield.
- Responsible use of autonomous and AI-assisted systems can protect civilians and military personnel through greater precision.
- Meaningful human control must be preserved, but self-defence must not be handicapped by regulation.
- Russia's widespread use of drones and automated systems demonstrates the urgent need for norms and accountability.
- Support international testing, transparency, and confidence-building measures.
- Autonomous weapons must not proliferate to non-state actors or irresponsible regimes.
- Legally binding norms should target systems incapable of complying with international humanitarian law.
- Democracies must cooperate and share AI defence technology so responsible states are not disadvantaged.

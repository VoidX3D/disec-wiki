Committee: DISEC (Disarmament and International Security Committee)

Country: Russian Federation ​

Agenda: Regulating the Illicit Trade of Small Arms and Light Weapons (SALW)​

​

Small Arms and Light Weapons (SALW) are the weapons designed to be used by individuals or

small groups. These types of man-portable arms include pistols, rifles, and submachine guns and

heavier portable arms like machine guns, grenade launchers, and shoulder-fired missiles. Illicit

trade of SALW is the unregulated and illegal trade of such arms and weapons. illegal buying and

selling over the black market, slipping out of legal trade, and deals against international bans are

some examples. These actions lead to more violence, support terrorism, and help grow

international crime. The Russian Federation realizes the threat of illicit SALW trade but calls

upon the fact that implications must preserve state sovereignty and avoid politicization.​

​

The Russian Federation remains the world's second top legal arms exporter. We support

combating the illicit trade in SALW but insist that all regulatory frameworks must respect

national sovereignty and avoid politicized enforcement. Russia has strengthened border control

and participates in bilateral agreements and technical cooperation with the UNPoA and ITI. The

national framework for SALW control is anchored in its Federal Law No. 150-FZ on Weapons

ensuring licensing and criminalizing unauthorized possession and trafficking. ​

​

Russia’s arm trades are centralized under Rosoboronexport, which enforces licensing authorities

and post-shipment verification. The Arms Trade Treaty (ATT) is refused to be ratified because of

its extreme politicization and unequal enforcements. However, Russia actively supports the

United Nations Program of Actions and International Tracing Instrument. It has signed over 40

bilateral defense agreements since 2014, often involving SALW transfers and training. Russia

participates in regional dialogues and supports tracing and stockpile management in Central Asia

and the Caucasus.

4

Russia signed the UN Protocol against the Illicit Manufacturing of and Trafficking in Firearms.

Russia participates in the Vienna Document as a member of OSCE. Russia is a participating state

in the Wassenaar Arrangement on Export Controls for Conventional Arms and Dual-Use Goods

and Technologies. While not SALW-specific, Russia is a party to this Convention on Certain

Conventional Weapons (CCW) protocol. Russia regularly submits reports to UNROCA,

including data on SALW exports and imports. Russia has signed regional agreements on arms

control and SALW stockpile management.

The Russian Federation calls upon the committee to engage in an effective dialogue regarding

the illicit trade of small arms and light weapons. It must be pragmatic and

sovereignty-respecting. In order to get positive outcomes the following facets must be addressed

and prioritized to the fullest.

1.​ To prevent diversion of legally exported SALW, universally accepted post-shipment

verification protocols must be used.

2.​ Global registry of unlicensed SALW production must be ensured to curb unauthorized

manufacturing.

3.​ Creation of a UN-led technical assistance fund for developing states to enhance marking,

record-keeping, and border control infrastructure.

4.​ Legitimate arms transfers, when responsibly managed, support national defense and

regional balance, and should not be conflated with illicit trade.

5.​ Foreign interference and diversion from Western stockpiles have contributed to black

market surges, notably in the Sahel and Eastern Europe.

The Russian Federation stands ready to offer constructive solutions to curb the illicit trade in

SALW. Our proposals aim to address root causes, enhance accountability, and promote practical

cooperation.

1.​ To guarantee that weapons only reach their intended recipients and are not diverted into

illegal markets, mandate universal post-shipment verification.

2.​ To monitor and eradicate illegal production, create a global registry of unlicensed SALW

production.

3.​ Establish regional centers for tracing and interdiction to facilitate coordinated action and

real-time intelligence sharing.

4.​ Create a technical assistance program headed by the UN to assist developing nations in

putting in place border control, marking, and record-keeping systems.

5.​ Establish a global code of conduct for private arms brokers that mandates end-user

certification, licensing, and openness in transfers involving sensitive conflicts.

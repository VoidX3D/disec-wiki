# MUN Procedure — Chits, Caucus & Debate Flow

A practical walkthrough of how a DISEC session actually runs: the debate phases,
motions, and the chit system — with templates. Pair this with the
[Rules of Procedure](rules-of-procedure.md) and the
[Position Paper](position-paper-guide.md) / [Resolution](resolution-paper-guide.md) guides.

## 1. Order of a committee session

```
1. Roll Call               → "Present" / "Present and voting"
2. Opening speeches        → General Speakers' List opens
3. Setting the agenda      → if >1 agenda item, motion to set agenda (needs simple majority)
4. General Speakers' List  → substantive debate, 1st / 2nd / 3rd speakers' lists
5. Caucuses                → moderated / unmoderated (see below)
6. Draft resolutions       → introduced via speakers' list or caucus
7. Voting procedure        → on draft resolutions (amendments first, then the resolution)
8. Closing                 → motion to adjourn / close debate
```

**During every phase, the Chair runs the "delegate queue":**

```
Request to speak    → "The delegate of Iran wishes to take the floor."
Point of Order      → interrupts on a rule violation: "Point of order, the delegate of Iran."
Point of Inquiry    → questions the Chair about procedure, not substance.
Right of Reply      → responds to a personal attack; granted at Chair's discretion.
Motion              → to do something (caucus, divide the house, etc.).
```

## 2. The chit system (notes between delegates)

A **chit** is a small slip of paper (or a chat message where the platform allows)
used to communicate privately during committee. This is how you lobby for votes,
form blocs, and coordinate amendments — *without* leaving your seat.

### Physical chit format

MUNs use standardized chit paper. A correct chit looks like:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  TO:  United States (Delegation)             │
│  FROM: Islamic Republic of Iran              │
│                                              │
│  [Message body — keep it short and concrete] │
│                                              │
│  ▢ URGENT     ▢ QUESTION     ▢ LOBBYING     │
│  Signature: ________________  Room: DISEC   │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Digital chit (if the MUN uses a notes app / committee chat)

```
/note to: United States
Iran here. We support your clause on meaningful human control.
Vote yes on US draft, we will co-submit. Confirm?
```

### Chit etiquette & strategy

- **Be specific and short.** One ask per chit. No one reads essays mid-caucus.
- **Always identify your delegation** in the header — no anonymous notes.
- **Use chits for:** vote-counting, co-submitting a resolution, proposing a bloc
  merge, requesting another delegation's draft text, scheduling a bilat.
- **Use the floor for:** substantive arguments, rebuttals, formal motions.
- **Track your chits:** keep a tally of who promised what — this becomes your
  whip list for the vote.
- **Never fabricate** another delegation's support in the room. Doha round-trip
  confirmation before you claim a co-sub.

## 3. Caucuses

The two caucus types and how to run each as Iran.

### Moderated Caucus (MC) — structured debate

**Motion:** "Motion for a moderated caucus of *X* minutes with a *Y*-second speaking
time on *topic*."

| Motion | Votes | Debate continues? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moderated caucus | Simple majority | Yes | Speaking time set by the Chair, usually 30s–90s |
| Extend (same topic) | Simple majority | Yes | Same format, usually half the original time |
| Unmoderated caucus | Simple majority | Yes | Free lobbying (below) |

**MC best practices:**

- Use it to build a **focused narrative** on one subtopic (e.g., "meaningful human
  control," "verification," "the developing-country perspective on dual-use AI").
- Iran's role: frame the debate around **sovereignty, tech access, and the
  developed-vs-developing divide** — not just ban-vs-allow.
- Every MC you speak in should **advance one line of argument and one concrete
  ask** (a phrase for the resolution, a co-sub, a counter to the US).

### Unmoderated Caucus (UC / "moderated caucus off") — free lobbying

**Motion:** "Motion for an unmoderated caucus of *X* minutes."

- No formal speaking; delegates move around the room. This is where **blocs form**.
- Iran's approach:
  1. **Open** — get a list of who shares the "tech-access / sovereignty" line
     (typically NAM, G77, NAM-adjacent states, many African/Asian delegations).
  2. **Merge** — agree on a single draft to co-submit instead of competing ones.
  3. **Count** — confirm every co-sub and every "lean yes" on your whip list.
  4. **Close** — agree on who speaks for the bloc during the next MC.

## 4. Speakers' lists

| List | Purpose | Order |
|---|---|---|
| **General Speakers' List (GSL)** | Broad national positions | Opened first, runs all session |
| **1st/2nd/3rd Speakers' Lists** | Substantive rounds on the topic | After GSL yields |
| **Right of Reply** | Personal attack response | Chair's discretion |
| **Closing speeches** | Final statement before vote | 2–3 minutes |

**Motion to open debate (before GSL):** if there are two agenda items, move
"Motion to set the agenda to *X*." This needs a **simple majority**.

**Motion to close debate (before voting):** needs a **two-thirds majority** and
ends all further debate — use it only when your resolution is clearly winning.

## 5. Voting procedure

Order of voting:

1. **Amendments** (friendly first, then unfriendly — in order submitted).
2. **The draft resolution** (as amended), clause by clause if "division" is moved.
3. After the vote: explanation of vote (if you were granted the right by answering
   "present and voting" — you cannot abstain).

### Voting motions Iran should know

| Motion | Vote needed | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Motion to divide the question | Simple majority | Vote on each clause separately |
| Motion to reconsider | Two-thirds | Re-vote on a resolution (only if passed/failed recently) |
| Motion to adopt by acclamation | Consensus | No formal count — use only when unanimous |
| Point of Order on voting | — | Chair corrects procedure |

## 6. Iran's cheat-sheet for motions

| You want to... | You say... |
|---|---|
| Start debating your agenda | "Motion to set the agenda to **LAWS & Military AI**." |
| Speak on a topic | "Motion for a moderated caucus of 10 minutes, 60-second speaking time on **meaningful human control**." |
| Lobby for co-subs | "Motion for an unmoderated caucus of 15 minutes." |
| Extend a good MC | "Motion to extend the moderated caucus by 5 minutes." |
| Stop all debate and vote | "Motion to close debate." |
| Vote clause-by-clause | "Motion to divide the question." |
| Stop a bad motion | "Point of order." or "Motion to table the motion." (if in rules) |

## 7. Full chit + caucus workflow (the Iran playbook)

1. **Pre-session:** 2 chits to your likeliest allies (NAM anchor + regional bloc)
   proposing a shared "tech-access & sovereignty" framing.
2. **GSL:** deliver Iran's position (position paper's core stance).
3. **MC 1:** open with "meaningful human control must not become a pretext for
   restricting access to defensive technologies."
4. **UC 1 (15 min):** form the bloc, circulate your draft, count co-subs.
5. **MC 2:** have the bloc's representative counter the US/EU ban-first position;
   introduce a "dual-use access + verification" clause.
6. **UC 2 (10 min):** merge drafts, fix the co-sub list, confirm whips.
7. **Vote:** friendly amendments first, then vote your merged draft; if you're
   "present and voting," deliver an explanation of vote afterward.

## Key terms quick-glance

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Quorum | 1/3 of registered delegates present to open |
| Simple majority | More than half of *present* delegations |
| Two-thirds majority | 2/3 of present delegations |
| Yielding | Giving remaining time to the Chair (or another delegate, if permitted) |
| Time remaining | Chair warns at 5s left — finish your thought |
| Directive / Press release | Some committees issue these — check the agenda |

# Military Forces & Equipment

A consolidated estimate of the conventional and strategic order of battle. Iran's
forces are organized under the **Artesh** (conventional armed forces) and the
**Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC / Sepah)**, which runs its own land, air,
naval (IRGCN) and space forces and controls the strategic missile program.

Figures are the consensus of public reporting (IISS Military Balance, CIA World
Factbook, SIPRI, CSIS, Middle East Institute). Ranges reflect varying estimates.

## Personnel

| Force | Active | Reserve / para-military |
|---|---|---|
| Army (Artesh) | ~350,000 | ~350,000 reserve |
| IRGC (Sepah) | ~190,000 | Basij militia ~1,000,000 |
| Navy (incl. IRGCN) | ~45,000 | — |
| Air Force (incl. IRGC AF) | ~37,000 | — |
| **Total active** | **~610,000** | Basij + reserves (several million) |

## Land systems — main battle tanks

Iran fields a mixed inventory of aging domestically-upgraded Western/Soviet tanks
plus indigenously built types. Total in service is roughly **2,000–2,300**.

| Type | Origin | Approx. in service |
|---|---|---|
| T-72S / T-72M1 (+ upgraded) | USSR / Iran (license) | ~480 |
| Zulfiqar-1/2/3 | Iran (domestic) | ~150–300 |
| T-54/T-55 / Type 59 | USSR / China | ~540 (most in reserve) |
| M60A1/A3 | US (pre-1979) | ~150 |
| Chieftain Mk3/5 (Mobarez) | UK / Iran (upgraded) | ~100 |
| Merkava / other | — | negligible (small trials) |
| Safir-74 (T-54 upgrade) | Iran | ~250 |

> The tank force is **numerous but technically dated**; the West assesses a large
> share as stored/reserve rather than operational.

## Armored & artillery

- **Armored personnel carriers / IFVs:** ~1,300–1,600 (BTR-50/60, BMP-1/2,
  M-113, Boragh domestics).
- **Self-propelled artillery:** ~300+ (M109, Thunder-1, Raad-1).
- **Towed artillery:** ~2,000+ (incl. M-46, D-30, GHN-45 copies).
- **Multiple Launch Rocket Systems (MLRS):** ~1,000+ (Fadjr-3/5, Haddid, Arash, etc.).
- **Anti-tank guided missiles:** thousands (Towsan, Toofan, Dehlavieh — indigenous).

## Air systems

Iran's fixed-wing combat inventory is dominated by pre-1979 US types and ageing
Russian/Chinese fighters, supported by a substantial UAV fleet.

| Type | Role | Approx. in service |
|---|---|---|
| F-4 Phantom II | Multirole | ~60 |
| F-5E/F (+ Azarakhsh/Saeqeh derivatives) | Fighter | ~60 |
| F-14 Tomcat | Interceptor | ~30 (spares-limited) |
| Mirage F1 / Su-24MK | Strike | ~45 |
| MiG-29 | Air superiority | ~35 |
| Su-22M | Fighter-bomber | ~10 |
| Kowsar (F-5 derivative, domestic) | Fighter | ~20 |
| **Total combat aircraft** | | **~250–300 (some reserve)** |

- **Attack helicopters:** ~50 (AH-1J Cobra, Mi-17, Shahed-285).
- **Drones (UCAV/ISR):** large and growing fleet — Shahed-136 (loitering munition),
  Mohajer-6, Ababil, Karrar, Shahed-129/191, Hamaseh, and others. Widely exported
  (e.g. to Russia). The drone force is the most heavily used Iranian system in
  regional conflicts.

## Missiles & strategic weapons

| Category | Detail |
|---|---|
| Ballistic missiles | Largest inventory in the Middle East — ~3,000+ SRBM/MRBM (Shahab-1/2/3, Fateh-110/313, Qiam, Dezful, Emad, Haj Qasem) |
| Cruise missiles | Soumar, Hoveyzeh, Paveh, Ya Ali (ground/sea launched) |
| Hypersonic (claimed) | Fattah-1 (unveiled 2023) |
| Anti-ship missiles | Noor, Qader, Nasr, Khalij Fars (ballistic anti-ship) — IRGCN emphasis |
| Air defense | Bavar-373, S-300PMU-2, 3rd Khordad, Raad, Mersad, Talash |
| Space | Simorgh/Qased SLVs; military satellites (Noor-1/2/3) |

> **Nuclear:** Iran is a **non-nuclear-weapon state** party to the NPT (ratified
> 1970) — see the [Nuclear Weapons & Mines](nuclear-and-mines.md) page.

## Navy

- **Submarines:** 3 Kilo-class (Russia) + ~30 midget/coastal (Ghadir, Nahang,
  Fateh-class).
- **Surface combatants:** frigates (Jamaran, Sahand, Alvand-class), corvettes,
  ~5-6 IRGCN fast-attack craft squadrons with anti-ship missiles (hundreds of boats).
- Strategy: "asymmetric naval warfare" in the Persian Gulf and Strait of Hormuz.

## Perceptions & committee relevance

- Iranian force structure is **defensive in posture but asymmetric in methods** —
  a fleet of cheap drones and missiles designed to impose costs in the Gulf and on
  nearby adversaries rather than to win a conventional war of attrition.
- This makes Iran a textbook case for **the "cheap autonomous system" proliferation
  debate**: loitering munitions (Shahed-136) cost a fraction of an interceptor, a
  central economic argument in LAWS negotiations.
- Reported use of UAVs with **AI-assisted targeting/guidance** in regional strikes
  has put Iranian programs squarely inside the discussion on autonomy in lethal
  systems.

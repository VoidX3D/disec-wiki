# History of Iran

A complete chronological dossier, from the first Elamite city-states to the
present. Emphasis is placed on the eras that shape Iran's current foreign,
security and military policy — the 1979 Revolution, the Iran–Iraq War, the
nuclear program and the regional wars that forged Iran's drone doctrine.

## 1. Prehistory & the Elamites (c. 3200–640 BCE)

- **c. 3200 BCE** — Elamite civilization emerges in the southwest (Susiana), with
  the city of **Susa** and its own script (Linear Elamite), one of the world's
  oldest writing systems.
- **c. 1900 BCE** — Aryan/Iranian peoples migrate onto the Iranian plateau from
  Central Asia. The **Medes** settle in the northwest, the **Persians** in Fars.
- **c. 700 BCE** — The Medes build the first major Iranian state, with **Ecbatana**
  (modern Hamadan) as capital.

## 2. The Achaemenid Empire (550–330 BCE)

- **550 BCE** — **Cyrus the Great** overthrows the Medes and founds the Achaemenid
  Empire, the largest empire the world had yet seen.
- **539 BCE** — Cyrus conquers Babylon; issues the **Cyrus Cylinder**, often called
  the first declaration of human rights (freed enslaved peoples, restored temples).
- **522–486 BCE** — **Darius I** organizes the empire into 20 satrapies, builds the
  **Royal Road**, introduces a unified coinage (daric), and begins **Persepolis**.
- **499–449 BCE** — Greco-Persian Wars: Marathon (490), Thermopylae (480), Salamis
  (480), Plataea (479). **Xerxes I** fails to conquer Greece.
- **333–330 BCE** — **Alexander the Great** conquers the empire; Persepolis is
  burned (330 BCE).
- Legacy: centralized administration, standardized law, religious tolerance and the
  notion of a "King of Kings" governing diverse peoples — a model every later
  Iranian state has invoked.

## 3. Hellenistic & Parthian eras (330 BCE–224 CE)

- **330–141 BCE** — Seleucid rule over Iran (Greek successor state).
- **247 BCE** — The **Parthians** (Arsacid dynasty) rise from the northeast;
  by 141 BCE they control Mesopotamia.
- **53 BCE** — Parthia defeats Rome at **Carrhae** — the first of many Rome–Persia
  wars; Parthia becomes Rome's only peer rival in the East.
- Parthian innovations: the **Parthian shot** (shooting backwards while riding), a
  prototype of mobile, asymmetric warfare that Iranian strategists still reference.

## 4. The Sassanian Empire (224–651 CE)

- **224 CE** — **Ardashir I** founds the Sassanian Empire, reviving Persian identity
  and Zoroastrian state religion.
- **260 CE** — Shapur I captures the Roman emperor **Valerian** at Edessa — a
  humiliation no Roman emperor suffered before.
- **3rd–6th centuries** — Sassanian Iran is one of two superpowers (with Byzantium),
  running silk-route trade, a professional army, and monumental architecture
  (Taq-e Bostan, Ctesiphon).
- **570 CE** — Traditional birth date of **Muhammad**, in a year marked by the
  (legendary) Persian-Abyssinian war over Yemen.
- **613–637 CE** — Sassanian–Byzantine exhaustion wars; the empire is left bankrupt
  and demographically drained on the eve of the Arab conquest.

## 5. Islamic conquest & the Iranian revival (651–1055)

- **651 CE** — The last Sassanian king, **Yazdegerd III**, is killed at Merv;
  Iran becomes part of the Islamic Caliphate.
- **8th–9th centuries** — Gradual Islamization alongside the **Iranian
  literary/political revival**: Persian remains the language of administration and
  culture. Abbasid viziers (the **Barmakids**, **Nizam al-Mulk**) are Iranians.
- **821–1000s** — Semi-independent Iranian dynasties: **Tahirids, Saffarids,
  Samanids, Buyids**. Persian poetry flourishes (Rudaki, Ferdowsi's *Shahnameh*
  completed c. 1010).
- **930s–1055** — The Buyids rule Baghdad; Iranian Shi'a influence grows.
- Key point: conversion to Shi'a Islam is *later and gradual*; Sunnism dominates
  Iran until the Safavids.

## 6. The Seljuk & Mongol eras (1037–1501)

- **1037–1194** — The **Seljuk Turks**, with Iranian bureaucrats, rule Persia;
  Nizam al-Mulk founds the **madrasa** system.
- **1219–1258** — **Genghis Khan** and the Mongols devastate Iran; cities (Merv,
  Nishapur) are depopulated. The **Ilkhanate** (1256–1335) rules Iran; in 1295 the
  Ilkhan **Ghazan** converts to Islam.
- **1370–1405** — **Tamerlane** (Timur) conquers and again devastates Persia.
- **1501** — The **Safavid** dynasty, led by Shah **Ismail I**, unifies Iran.

## 7. The Safavid Empire (1501–1736) — Shi'a Iran is born

- **1501** — Ismail I declares **Twelver Shi'a Islam the state religion** — the
  single most consequential decision in modern Iranian history, defining Iran's
  religious identity against the Sunni Ottoman/Indian empires.
- **1588–1629** — **Shah Abbas I** ("the Great"): makes **Isfahan** the capital,
  builds the Naqsh-e Jahan square, modernizes the army with gunpowder weapons, and
  recovers territory from the Ottomans (Treaty of Zuhab, 1639 — the modern
  Iran–Ottoman border basis).
- **1722–1736** — Decline and foreign invasions (Afghan Hotakis sack Isfahan in
  1722); the empire collapses.

## 8. Afsharid & Zand (1736–1794)

- **1736–1747** — **Nader Shah** (Afsharid) creates a new empire, sacks Delhi (1739)
  and seizes the **Koh-i-Noor** and Peacock Throne. Assassinated 1747.
- **1751–1794** — The **Zand** dynasty rules from Shiraz under Karim Khan — a
  relatively peaceful interlude.

## 9. The Qajar dynasty (1794–1925) — Great Game and weakness

- **1794** — Agha Mohammad Khan founds the Qajar dynasty; Tehran becomes capital
  (1786/96).
- **1804–1828** — Russo-Persian Wars: Iran loses the Caucasus (Treaties of
  Gulistan 1813, Turkmenchay 1828) — the origin of modern Azerbaijani territory.
- **1826–1857** — Loss of Herat and influence in Afghanistan (British-backed
  defeats). The "**Great Game**" between Britain and Russia reduces Iran to a
  sphere-of-influence battleground.
- **1857–1890s** — Concessions to Europeans: the **Reuter Concession** (1872,
  revoked), tobacco concession (1890) sparks the **Tobacco Protest** (1891) — a
  religious-led national movement and a rehearsal for 1979.
- **1906** — The **Constitutional Revolution**: Iran's first constitution and
  National Consultative Assembly (Majles) established (5 August 1906; Supplementary
  Fundamental Laws 1907) — Iran's first parliament.

## 10. World War I & the rise of Pahlavi (1914–1941)

- **1914–1918** — Iran declares neutrality in WWI but is occupied by Ottoman,
  Russian and British forces; famine of 1917–19 kills perhaps **1–2 million**.
- **1921** — Coup by **Reza Khan** (British-assisted), Minister of War.
- **1925** — Reza Khan crowns himself **Reza Shah Pahlavi**, ending the Qajars.
- **1925–1941** — Forced modernization: railways (Trans-Iranian), secular courts,
  ban on the veil (1936), military conscription, and suppression of the clergy.
- **1935** — Persia is renamed **Iran** (officially at the UN request, 1935).
- **1941** — Britain and the USSR **invade and occupy Iran** (with the US) to secure
  supply lines ("Persian Corridor"); Reza Shah is forced to abdicate in favor of his
  son, **Mohammad Reza Pahlavi** (21-year-old).

## 11. WWII, the 1953 coup & the Pahlavi decades (1941–1978)

- **1941–1946** — Allied occupation; the USSR briefly refuses to leave Azerbaijan
  (Azerbaijan Crisis, 1945–46) — the first Cold War confrontation.
- **1951–1953** — PM **Mohammad Mosaddegh** nationalizes the oil industry (Anglo-
  Iranian Oil Company → NIOC). Britain organizes a blockade.
- **19 August 1953** — **CIA/MI6-orchestrated coup** ("Operation Ajax") topples
  Mosaddegh and restores the Shah. The coup's legacy of foreign intervention
  remains central to Iranian anti-Westernism and to debates over foreign-engineered
  regime change.
- **1963** — **White Revolution**: land reform, women's suffrage, literacy corps —
  opposed by the clergy, including **Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini**, who is exiled
  (1964) first to Turkey, then Iraq, then France.
- **1963–1979** — Oil-boom modernization and repression: SAVAK secret police,
  censorship; dramatic urbanization and inequality.
- **1971** — 2,500-year celebration of the Persian monarchy at Persepolis.
- **1973–74** — Oil price surge makes Iran a major power broker; the Shah builds a
  large, Western-equipped military.

## 12. The 1979 Revolution (1978–1981)

- **1978** — Mass protests build (Jaleh Square massacre, 8 September 1978, "Black
  Friday").
- **16 January 1979** — The Shah flees Iran.
- **1 February 1979** — Khomeini returns to Tehran; the revolution consolidates.
- **11 February 1979** — The armed forces declare neutrality; the Pahlavi monarchy
  ends.
- **30 March 1979** — Referendum establishes the Islamic Republic.
- **4 November 1979** — **US Embassy hostage crisis**: 52 Americans held 444 days
  until 20 January 1981 (the day Reagan took office). US-Iran relations severed.
- **1979** — The **Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC / Sepah)** is founded
  (May 1979), and the **Basij** militia later (1980) — parallel military structures
  loyal to the revolution, not the regular army.
- **1980** — First Majles; **Bani-Sadr** elected first president (Jan 1980).

## 13. The Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988) — the formative trauma

- **22 September 1980** — **Iraq invades Iran**, beginning the war (Iraqi objective:
  annex Khuzestan and destroy the revolution).
- **1982** — Iran recovers its territory and, after Operation Jerusalem (May 1982),
  refuses a ceasefire and pushes into Iraq — the war becomes one of attrition.
- **1983–1988** — **Chemical weapons** used by Iraq against Iran (mustard gas,
  nerve agents, per UN findings; ~50,000 Iranian casualties) — *the single most
  direct historical referent for Iran's stance on weapons-of-mass-effect and on
  international arms-control credibility*.
- **1986** — Iran–Contra: US secretly sells arms to Iran to fund Nicaraguan
  Contras — exposed, further poisoning US-Iran relations.
- **1987–1988** — "Tanker War" in the Persian Gulf; US Navy engages Iranian forces
  (Operation Praying Mantis, April 1988); USS *Vincennes* shoots down **Iran Air
  Flight 655**, killing 290 (July 1988; US calls it an accident).
- **20 July 1988** — Iran accepts UNSCR 598 ceasefire (Khomeini: "drinking the
  poison cup").
- **Casualties:** estimated **200,000–1,000,000+ Iranian dead**, ~4,000 km² of
  border territory contaminated by mines, and the permanent legend of the
  human-wave "Basij" and martyrs' culture.
- **1988** — Mass execution of political prisoners (estimates 3,000–30,000).

> **Why the war matters for this committee:** the war gave Iran (a) a deep distrust
> of international arms-control enforcement (UN failed to condemn Iraqi CW use
> until years later), (b) experience that drove its doctrine of **asymmetric
> warfare, mass-produced cheap weapons (drones, missiles), and self-reliance**,
> and (c) a rhetorical anchor: "we were the victims of weapons of mass destruction
> and the world did nothing" — used in every Iranian LAWS statement.

## 14. Reconstruction & Reform (1989–2005)

- **1989** — Khomeini dies (3 June); **Ali Khamenei** becomes Supreme Leader.
- **1989–1997** — President **Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani**: reconstruction, economic
  liberalization, expansion of the missile program (with Chinese/North Korean
  help) — the **Shahab-3** (tested 1998, range ~1,300 km) is his strategic legacy.
- **1997–2005** — President **Mohammad Khatami**: "dialogue of civilizations,"
  detente with Europe, early nuclear diplomacy (2003 negotiations with EU-3);
  crackdown on student protests (1999).
- **2002** — Iran's secret Natanz uranium-enrichment facility is revealed by an
  exile group (NDCR); the nuclear file reaches the IAEA.
- **2003** — Iran suspends enrichment under the Saadabad Agreement (Oct 2003);
  US invasion of Iraq removes Saddam — a strategic gift and threat to Iran.

## 15. The Nuclear Crisis & Sanctions (2005–2015)

- **2005–2013** — President **Mahmoud Ahmadinejad**: resumes enrichment (2006),
  IAEA refers Iran to the UNSC; UNSC resolutions **1696, 1737, 1747, 1803, 1835,
  1929** impose escalating sanctions (2006–2010).
- **2009** — **Green Movement** protests after the disputed June election; crackdown.
- **2010** — **Stuxnet** cyberattack damages Natanz centrifuges — the first
  known state-on-state cyber-physical attack on nuclear infrastructure and a
  founding case for the "autonomy/cyber" debate.
- **2011–2013** — Assassinations of nuclear scientists (Mostafa Ahmadi-Roshan et al.),
  attributed to Israel; "maximum pressure" sanctions choke the economy.
- **24 November 2013** — **JCPOA** interim deal (Geneva); formal JCPOA signed
  **14 July 2015** (with P5+1), implemented **16 January 2016** ("Implementation
  Day"): enrichment capped at 3.67%, stockpile capped at 300 kg LEU, Fordow
  converted, Arak redesigned, IAEA access; sanctions relief in exchange.

## 16. Regional Expansion & the JCPOA Collapse (2015–2021)

- **2015–2020** — Iran deepens its "axis of resistance": **Qasem Soleimani**
  (Quds Force) commands proxy networks in Iraq, Syria, Lebanon (Hezbollah),
  Yemen (Houthis/Ansarallah), Afghanistan (Fatemiyoun).
- **2017–2021** — Trump administration: **8 May 2018** US withdraws from JCPOA and
  reimposes "maximum pressure" sanctions; **Jan 2020** Soleimani killed in a US
  drone strike in Baghdad (3 January 2020); Iran retaliates with ballistic-missile
  strikes on Al-Asad Airbase (8 January 2020) — no US deaths.
- **2020** — The **COVID-19** pandemic hits Iran early and hard; IRGC admits
  shooting down **Ukraine International Airlines Flight 752** (8 January 2020),
  killing 176, after calling it an accident — a major domestic legitimacy crisis.
- **2020–2021** — Assassination of nuclear scientist **Mohsen Fakhrizadeh**
  (27 November 2020, attributed to Israel); Iran advances enrichment to 60%.

## 17. The 2021–2025 Era: Raisi, protests, and war

- **2021** — **Ebrahim Raisi** elected president (June); JCPOA talks stall at
  Vienna; Iran's stockpile grows to ~6,000 kg of 60% HEU-equivalent by 2024.
- **September 2022** — Death of **Mahsa Amini** (16 September) sparks the **"Woman,
  Life, Freedom"** nationwide uprising — the largest challenge to the regime since
  1979; mass protests, crackdowns, hundreds killed, thousands detained.
- **October 2023** — **Hamas attack on Israel** (7 October) and the Gaza war; the
  "axis of resistance" escalates across the region (Hezbollah, Houthis attacking
  Red Sea shipping).
- **April 2024** — First-ever **direct Iranian attack on Israel** ("True Promise",
  13–14 April): ~300 drones and missiles (Shahed-136s, cruise and ballistic
  missiles); Israel retaliates against Isfahan air-defense site.
- **May 2024** — **Raisi killed in a helicopter crash** (19 May); **Masoud
  Pezeshkian** elected president (July 2024, reformist-backed).
- **October 2024** — Second direct Iran-Israel exchange; Israel strikes Iranian
  air-defense and missile-production sites (26 October).
- **2024–2025** — Russia–Iran deepening: Iranian Shahed drones and missiles used
  in the Ukraine war; **Iran–Russia strategic partnership** signed (17 January
  2025); Iran gains Russian fighter jets (Su-35), air-defense (S-400), and
  satellite help.
- **2025** — **Nuclear stalemate continues**: IAEA reports Iran's stockpile of 60%
  enriched uranium at record levels; negotiations with the P5+1 resume
  intermittently.

## 18. Iran in one paragraph (position-paper summary)

Iran is the heir to three and a half millennia of imperial identity, an
exceptionally **young, literate, urbanized** society, and a state whose security
doctrine was forged by two traumas — the 1953 CIA coup and the 1980–88 war fought
under chemical attack while the world looked away. That history produces four
consistent positions relevant to the LAWS agenda: **full sovereignty and
self-reliance in defense technology; deep distrust of Western-led verification;
insistence that weapons-capable AI be negotiated under existing humanitarian-law
principles (CCW) rather than new binding bans that would freeze unequal
capabilities; and rhetorical deployment of its own victimhood (CW, sanctions,
Stuxnet, drone assassination) as proof that "autonomous" and "remotely-operated"
weapons threaten exactly the states that cannot build them.**

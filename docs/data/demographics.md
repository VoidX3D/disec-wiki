## Age structure

Iran has a "youth bulge" that has already begun to shrink: the cohort born during the
1980s population surge is now moving into middle age. The share of children under 15
has fallen steadily since the early 2000s while the working-age share has peaked.

| Age group | Share of population | Direction | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–14 | ~22.0% | Falling | Children (youth dependency) |
| 15–24 | ~13.5% | Falling | Youngest working-age |
| 15–64 | ~69.0% | Peaking | Working age |
| 25–54 | ~44% | Peak | Core workforce |
| 65+ | ~9.0% | Rising | Elderly (projected to accelerate) |

> **Committee relevance:** a shrinking youth cohort and a maturing workforce shape the
> *personnel* dimension of any LAWS debate — fewer future recruits, more reliance on
> automation and remote systems, and a demographic argument both for and against
> fully autonomous weapons.

Iran graduates a very large STEM workforce — engineering and medical graduates are a
core resource that also underpins the country's missile, drone and AI programmes.
Brain drain remains significant, particularly among engineering and IT graduates.

## Migration & displacement

- Net migration is negative: more Iranians leave than enter each year, driven by
  sanctions-driven unemployment and the diaspora's pull.
- Iran hosts one of the world's largest refugee populations — roughly **3.5–4 million
  Afghans** (largest Afghan refugee population anywhere), alongside Iraqi and other
  refugee communities.
- Large-scale internal displacement is low, but **drought-driven internal migration**
  from the southeast (Sistan–Baluchestan) toward central provinces has grown.

nuclear-weapon states (China, France, Russia, UK, US) plus three de facto

> **Iran:** non-nuclear-weapon state (NNWS) under the NPT, ratified 1970. The JCPOA
> (2015) was in force until the US withdrawal (2018); IAEA verification coverage
> narrowed 2021–2025. As of 2025 Iran's stockpile was evaluated by the IAEA as
> enriched to up to 60% U-235 with no weaponization confirmed.

## Active landmines — who is still emplaced

The **1997 Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention (APMBC / Ottawa Treaty)** has 164
parties. Despite that, **~57 million anti-personnel landmines** remain stockpiled
and **nine countries are assessed as still actively emplacing new mines**
(2023 Landmine Monitor):

| State | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Myanmar | Non-party | Emplaced during civil war |
| Ukraine | Party | Emplaced in defense during the Russian invasion |
| Russia | Non-party | Massive contamination from the invasion |
| Syria | Non-party | Civil war contamination |
| Iran | **Non-party** | Legacy contamination (Iran–Iraq War); stocks held |
| Pakistan | Non-party | Legacy contamination |
| India | Non-party | Border-area legacy mines |
| North Korea | Non-party | Heavy border (DMZ) laying |
| South Korea | Non-party | DMZ deterrent laying (storage) |

### Which states have NOT joined the Mine Ban Treaty (key)

Non-parties include: **China, India, Pakistan, Russia, the United States, Iran,
Israel, Syria, Myanmar, North Korea, South Korea, Cuba, Armenia, Azerbaijan,
Vietnam, Libya, Egypt, Kazakhstan, Mongolia, Nepal, Bhutan, Thailand, Laos**.

## Landmine contamination — Iran at a glance

- **Estimated mine-affected area:** ~4,000 km² across western border provinces
  (Kermanshah, Khuzestan, Ilam, Kurdistan, West Azerbaijan).
- **Origin:** Iran–Iraq War (1980–1988) laid an estimated 16–20 million mines.
- **Casualties:** ~2,300–2,900 total (civilian) since the war; ongoing annual
  casualties in single digits, mostly herders/children.
- **Clearance:** Iran does not participate in the APMBC and does not publish
  clearance statistics; estimates suggest <10% of contaminated land cleared.
- **Position:** Iran rejects APMBC membership on sovereignty/defense grounds and
  the "asymmetric threat" argument, and cites sanctions for the cost of clearance.

## Heavy weapons & "weapons that have a disproportionate effect"

Under the **CCW Protocol V (ERW)** and the broader "inherently indiscriminate
weapons" debate, heavy conventional weapons are often quantified by
**contamination rather than count**. Key ERW hotspots today: Ukraine, Gaza,
Syria, Iraq, Laos, Vietnam, Angola, Mozambique, Cambodia, Afghanistan.

| Region/country | ERW/UXO burden |
|---|---|
| Laos | ~80M cluster bomblets dropped 1964–73; ~25–30% unexploded |
| Vietnam | ~350,000 tonnes of ordnance; ~20% unexploded |
| Cambodia | ~4–6M mines/UXO; ~2,000 km² contaminated |
| Ukraine (since 2022) | ~174,000 km² estimated contaminated (UN Mine Action) |
| Gaza (since 2023) | ~7,500 tonnes of unexploded munitions estimated |

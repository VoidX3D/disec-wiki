# Treaties & Legal Commitments

The full treaty architecture Iran belongs to — arms control, disarmament,
nuclear non-proliferation, conventional weapons, international humanitarian law
and AI governance. Each entry: status, year, Iran's stated position and the
LAWS / military-AI implications.

## Arms-control & disarmament

| Treaty / forum | Year | Iran status | Position & LAWS relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT)** | 1968 / ratified 1970 | Party | Non-nuclear-weapon state. Backs "inalienable right" to peaceful use under Article IV. |
| **Chemical Weapons Convention (CWC)** | 1993 / ratified 1997 | Party | Supports OPCW verification framework. |
| **Biological Weapons Convention (BWC)** | 1972 / ratified 1973 | Party | Supports strengthening compliance. |
| **CCW (Certain Conventional Weapons)** | 1980 / ratified 1989 | Party | **Primary negotiating forum for LAWS.** Iran supports a legally binding instrument. |
| **CCW Amended Protocol II (mines, booby traps)** | 1996 | Party | Restricts use against civilians. |
| **CCW Protocol V (remnants of war)** | 2003 / ratified 2005 | Party | Post-conflict clearance obligations. |
| **Convention on Cluster Munitions (CCM)** | 2008 | **Non-party** | Opposed on humanitarian + security grounds. |
| **Anti-Personnel Mine Ban Convention (APMBC / Ottawa)** | 1997 | **Non-party** | Position: mines remain a legitimate defensive tool. |
| **Arms Trade Treaty (ATT)** | 2013 | **Signed, not ratified** | Supports scope; criticises export-control bias. |
| **Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty (CTBT)** | 1996 | **Signed, not ratified** (Majlis pending) | Supports moratorium; ratification blocked. |
| **Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons (TPNW)** | 2017 | **Non-party** | Position: NPT is the appropriate framework. |

## LAWS-specific fora (CCW track)

| Forum | Iran's position |
|---|---|
| **GGE on Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems** (since 2014) | Supports a **legally binding instrument**; opposes pre-emptive blanket bans; insists on balance with "legitimate defensive needs". |
| **CCW High Contracting Parties meetings** | Active participant; co-sponsors balanced-mandate language. |
| **UNGA First Committee (DISEC)** | Voting bloc leader; pushes "meaningful human control" + "non-discriminatory access to autonomous defensive technologies". |

## UN Security Council resolutions

Iran is subject to (and lifted from) several UNSC resolutions tied to its
nuclear file.

| Resolution | Year | Status (as of late 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| UNSC 1696 (2006) | Demanded suspension of enrichment | Historical |
| UNSC 1717, 1747, 1803, 1835, 1887, 1929 | Sanctions packages 2006–2010 | Historical |
| UNSC 1984 (2011) | Renewed mandate | Historical |
| **UNSC 2231 (2015)** | Endorsed JCPOA | **Active until Oct 2025**, then snapback lapsed. |
| **JCPOA snapback (Oct 2025)** | Snapback provisions expired; UN sanctions not re-imposed | Effective lapse |

## International humanitarian law

| Instrument | Iran status |
|---|---|
| **Geneva Conventions (1949)** | **Not a party** |
| Additional Protocols I & II (1977) | **Not a party** |
| Hague Regulations (1907) | Customary — applies |
| **Rome Statute (ICC)** | **Not a party** |
| Genocide Convention | Party (1949–50) |
| Conventional Weapons Convention | Party |

> Iran's IHL position: existing IHL is **sufficient** and applies to autonomous
> weapons; new law is unnecessary. Iran supports the GGE's "two-tier" approach
> (regulation, not prohibition).

## Key principles in Iran's negotiating posture

1. **Sovereign equality** — no framework that privileges technologically
   dominant states.
2. **Right to self-defence** under Article 51 of the UN Charter, including
   autonomous defensive systems.
3. **Opposition to technological monopolisation** by major powers.
4. **Support for multilateral negotiations** under UN/CCW frameworks.
5. **Recognition that existing IHL applies**; new law unnecessary.
6. **Inclusivity** — Global South must shape governance.
7. **Opposition to unilateral coercive measures** affecting technology access.

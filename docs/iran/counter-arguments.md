# Counter-Arguments & Responses

## 1. Iran's drone programme threatens regional stability

Iran's drone programme is a defensive response to the presence of US military bases in the region, Israeli military aggression, and Gulf state arms purchases. Iran has the right to self-defence under Article 51 of the UN Charter.

## 2. LAWS should be banned entirely

A complete ban is neither realistic nor equitable. Autonomous technologies have legitimate defensive applications. The focus should be on responsible governance, not prohibition that benefits only those who already possess the technology.

## 3. Existing IHL is sufficient to regulate LAWS

While IHL principles remain applicable, new technologies create new challenges that existing frameworks may not adequately address, including the accountability gap and algorithmic decision-making in lethal contexts.

## 4. Iran should support a binding treaty

Iran supports multilateral negotiations but insists that any binding instrument must be equitable, non-discriminatory, and include provisions for technology transfer and capacity-building for developing states.

## 5. Autonomous weapons increase the risk of accidental war

All military technologies carry risks. The answer is responsible development, transparency, and confidence-building measures, not blanket prohibitions that disadvantage states facing genuine security threats.
